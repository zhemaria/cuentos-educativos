The cards with holes that counted a nation

The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Ms. Novak opened a copy of the Constitution and read from Article I. "Every ten years, the United States must count all its people," she said. "It's called the . Why do you think the Founders wrote that into the Constitution?" "To know how many people there are?" asked Israel.

"To decide ," said Ms. Novak. "The number of seats each state has in the House of Representatives depends on its population. States that grow get more seats. So the census must count everyone." "And it decides where money goes for schools and roads," she added.

Then she gave them a challenge: count the answers on 300 cards, how many students walk, ride the bus or come by car, by grade. Salma made tally marks on a huge sheet of paper. After twenty minutes she was still counting, and she had to start over twice. "This is impossible," she groaned.

"That was the problem in 1880," said Ms. Novak. "The country had grown to about 50 million people. Clerks had to all the by hand, and the final results of the 1880 census were not finished until 1887." "Seven years!" said Daniel. "The next census would almost be starting!"

"A young engineer named Herman Hollerith, who had worked on the 1880 census, had an idea," said Ms. Novak. "Each person's information would be recorded as holes in a . A hole in one spot could mean male or female, another spot an age group, another a state." He said he got the idea from train conductors who punched holes in tickets to describe passengers.

Israel punched holes along the edges of index cards to show walk, bus or car. Daniel slid a knitting needle through the stack where the bus hole was. The cards with holes in that spot dropped out. "Thirty-eight bus riders!" he counted in seconds. "Hollerith's machine did it with electricity," said Ms. Novak. "Metal pins pressed on each card. Where there was a hole, a pin touched a cup of mercury, closed an electric circuit and moved a counter on a dial."

"In 1890, clerks counted the population of the whole country with Hollerith's machines in a few months: 62,622,250 people," said Ms. Novak. "The work finished ahead of schedule and under budget." Hollerith later started a company to sell his . After many changes, it became part of IBM, one of the biggest computer companies in the world.

"So computers started with the census?" asked Salma. "Punched cards were used to process data for about 80 years, until modern computers replaced them," said Ms. Novak. "Today, people can answer the census online." Salma looked at her tally sheet and laughed. "Hollerith, you saved me," she said. "Next time, I'm bringing a knitting needle."

Check your thinking

Each dial counted one category, for example one age group or one state, so many things could be counted at the same time. In 1890, offices were full of wooden furniture, and a machine that looked like a desk or cabinet seemed familiar and trustworthy. Design is part of technology: the way a machine looks can help people accept it.

Glossary

census
An official count of all the people who live in a place. The U.S. census is taken every ten years.
censo: Un recuento oficial de todas las personas que viven en un lugar. El censo de EE. UU. se hace cada diez años.
representation
Having people in government who speak and vote for you. The number of a state's representatives depends on its population.
representación: Tener personas en el gobierno que hablan y votan por ti. El número de representantes de un estado depende de su población.
data
Facts and numbers collected to be studied.
datos: Hechos y números que se reúnen para estudiarlos.
tabulate
To count and organize data into tables.
tabular: Contar y organizar los datos en tablas.
punched card
A card with holes punched in certain places to store information that a machine can read.
tarjeta perforada: Una tarjeta con agujeros en ciertos lugares para guardar información que una máquina puede leer.
technology
Tools and machines that people invent to solve problems.
tecnología: Herramientas y máquinas que la gente inventa para resolver problemas.

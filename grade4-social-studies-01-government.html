The story

The school bus left Chicago early in the morning. About three and a half hours later, Israel pressed his nose against the window. "Look at that dome!" he shouted. It was the Illinois State in Springfield, the state capital.

Inside, everyone tipped their heads back to see the ceiling, high above them and full of color. Daniel got a little dizzy. "That's the first time a building ever made me dizzy," he said, and everyone laughed.

Ms. Rivera gathered the class in a hallway. "The Illinois is the plan for our state government," she explained. "It splits the work among three branches, so that no branch has all the power."

First they visited the gallery above the General Assembly. "This is the ," Ms. Rivera said. "Here, lawmakers chosen by voters from all over the state write laws and vote on them. It has two parts: the Senate and the House of Representatives."

Sophia spotted a big board with tiny green and red lights. That day the lawmakers were voting on a , an idea that isn't a law yet. "Green means yes and red means no," Sophia whispered. "If the yes votes win in both chambers, the bill goes to the governor," Ms. Rivera added.

The governor leads the . The governor makes sure the laws are carried out and can sign a bill to make it a law or it. The governor's office was right there in the same building.

Next they walked to a nearby building that looked serious and grand: the Illinois Supreme Court. "This is the ," Ms. Rivera explained. "Judges decide cases and explain what laws mean when people disagree."

On the bus ride home, Israel drew a triangle. At each corner he wrote: "make the laws," "carry out the laws," "explain the laws." "Each branch keeps an eye on the others," he said. "It's like a game. Some people write the rules, someone makes sure everybody follows them, and the referee decides when there's an argument."

"And don't forget the levels of government," Ms. Rivera added. "The mayor leads the city, the governor leads the state and the president leads the country. Each level has its own job."

Daniel gazed out at the cornfields. "I know what I want to be when I grow up," he said. "A state senator. But first I have to finish my homework."

Glossary

capitol
The building where a state's or a country's lawmakers work. The Illinois State Capitol is in Springfield.
constitution
The written plan for a government. It explains how the government is organized and what rights people have.
legislative branch
The part of government that writes laws and votes on them. In Illinois it is called the General Assembly.
bill
An idea for a new law that is still being voted on.
executive branch
The part of government that carries out the laws. In Illinois it is led by the governor.
veto
To reject a bill so that it does not become a law.
judicial branch
The part of government made up of courts and judges, who decide cases and explain the laws.

The great fire, told by everyone

A story about how different people can live through the same event in different ways.

The story

Ms. Ríos told her students about a very important in Chicago's history: the Great Chicago Fire of 1871. An event is something important that happened at a certain time.

More than 150 years ago, many houses in Chicago were made of wood, and that fall had been very dry. One October night, a fire started in a barn. The strong wind spread it, and the city burned for almost two days. Many families lost their homes.

"Today we are going to look at this event from different ," said the teacher. "A perspective is the way a person sees something, based on what they lived through and how they felt." Each child would play the part of a person from that time.

Israel was a firefighter. "We ran from one fire to another," he said. "We were very tired, but we could not stop!" For the firefighter, the fire was a long, hard fight.

Lucía was a girl whose family lost their home. "We ran toward the lake with the few things we could carry," she said. "That night, we slept on the shore." For that family, the fire was a night of fear and sadness. The girl was a : someone who saw the event with her own eyes.

Another classmate played Mrs. Catherine O'Leary, whose barn was where the fire started. Newspapers back then said that her cow had kicked over a lamp, but no one saw it happen. It was a : something people repeat without knowing if it is true. Many years later, the city of Chicago said that blaming her had been .

Last, another boy was a builder. "After the fire, we all helped to the city," he said. "This time we used brick and stone, which do not burn as easily." To rebuild is to build again what was destroyed.

When the play was over, the teacher asked what they had learned. "That the same event can be told in many ways," said Lucía. "To understand it well, you have to listen to all the people who lived through it."

Glossary

event
Something important that happened at a certain time.
perspective
The way a person sees something, based on what they lived through and how they felt.
witness
A person who saw an event with their own eyes.
rumor
Something people repeat from one to another without knowing if it is true.
unfair
Not fair: treating someone badly when they do not deserve it.
rebuild
To build again what was destroyed.

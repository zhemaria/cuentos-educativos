The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

For the last science project of the year, Mr. Mensah turned the gym into a museum. Each table told the story of one great discovery, and the students were the guides. "Your job," he said, "is to tell the story, show the and explain the big idea."

Israel stood by a pot of pea plants with purple and white flowers. "Mendel counted 705 purple flowers and 224 white ones," he told a group of parents. "That's about 3 to 1. That showed him that some traits are dominant and others are recessive." A grandfather nodded. "I always wondered why I have my mother's eyes."

At the next table, Elías held up a willow branch and a cup of water. "Van Helmont thought his tree was made of water," he said, "but most of its mass came from the air: carbon dioxide." Beside him, Daniel turned a crank on a little Ferris wheel. "At the top, the cars have the most potential ," Daniel explained. "On the way down, it changes into kinetic energy. Energy changes form, but it doesn't disappear."

Sophia's table had a clay ichthyosaur next to a striped stack of rock layers. "Mary Anning's fossils were found in old rock layers," she said. "Deeper layers are older, so the fossils show how life changed over time. Ichthyosaurs are extinct now." Next to her, Athena pushed two paper continents together. "And the same fossils on two continents helped prove that the continents move."

Salma and Selena shared a table about rivers and earthquakes. Salma pointed to a graph of fish species in the Chicago River. "When the water got cleaner, the number of species went up from fewer than ten to more than seventy." "One change in an ecosystem affects many populations," she said. "That's why everything in a is connected."

Mr. Mensah walked from table to table, listening. When the families had left, he gathered the class in the middle of the gym. "What do all these discoveries have in common?" he asked. "They all used a ," said Selena. "The peas in the square, the ball on a string, the ramp with marbles."

"And they all needed evidence," said Athena. "Wegener had a great idea, but nobody believed him until Marie Tharp's map showed more evidence." Israel added, "And a lot of them were ignored at first. Mendel, Wegener, Mary Anning." "So a good keeps going, even when nobody listens," said Salma.

Mr. Mensah smiled. "In eighth grade, you'll make discoveries of your own." Daniel looked at the Ferris wheel model, the electromagnet and the pea plants. "Can we keep the museum up until then?" he asked. "The custodian says no," said Mr. Mensah. "But you can keep the ideas."

Glossary

evidence
Facts or observations that help show whether an idea is true.
evidencia: Hechos u observaciones que ayudan a demostrar si una idea es verdadera.
model
A drawing, object or idea that helps explain something too small, too big or too far to see.
modelo: Un dibujo, objeto o idea que ayuda a explicar algo demasiado pequeño, grande o lejano para verlo.
pattern
Something that repeats in a way you can predict, like a 3 to 1 ratio or older rock layers lying deeper.
patrón: Algo que se repite de forma predecible, como una razón de 3 a 1 o las capas de roca más antiguas que están más abajo.
energy
The ability to cause motion or change. It can change forms and move from one object to another.
energía: La capacidad de causar movimiento o cambio. Puede cambiar de forma y pasar de un objeto a otro.
system
A group of parts that work together, like an ecosystem, the solar system or the parts of an electromagnet.
sistema: Un conjunto de partes que funcionan juntas, como un ecosistema, el sistema solar o las partes de un electroimán.
scientist
A person who asks questions about the world and answers them with evidence.
científico: Una persona que se hace preguntas sobre el mundo y las responde con evidencia.

Questions

Choose the correct answer. If you make a mistake, the story shows you where the answer is.

Elige la respuesta correcta. Si te equivocas, el cuento te muestra dónde está la respuesta.

  1. AQuestion A. What pattern did Mendel find in his pea plants?

    ¿Qué patrón encontró Mendel en sus plantas de guisante?

    Mendel counted 705 purple and 224 white flowers, about 3 to 1, which showed dominant and recessive traits.

  2. BQuestion B. What happens to the energy of a Ferris wheel car on the way down?

    ¿Qué pasa con la energía de una cabina de la rueda al bajar?

    At the top the car has the most potential energy, and on the way down it changes into kinetic energy.

  3. CQuestion C. What do fossils in rock layers show?

    ¿Qué muestran los fósiles en las capas de roca?

    Deeper layers are older, so the fossils in them show how life changed over time.

  4. DQuestion D. What did Salma say about ecosystems?

    ¿Qué dijo Salma sobre los ecosistemas?

    When one part of an ecosystem changes, many populations change too, because everything in a system is connected.

  5. EQuestion E. What did Wegener need before people accepted his idea?

    ¿Qué le faltaba a Wegener para que aceptaran su idea?

    People accepted the idea of moving continents only after more evidence, like Marie Tharp's map of the ocean floor.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Connect the ideas

Choose two discoveries from this year that seem very different, for example Galileo's moons and Goodyear's rubber. Explain one way they are alike, using the words evidence, model or energy.

Conecta las ideas. Elige dos descubrimientos de este año que parezcan muy distintos, por ejemplo las lunas de Galileo y el caucho de Goodyear. Explica en qué se parecen, usando las palabras evidence, model o energy.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Example: Galileo and Goodyear both used evidence from careful observation. Galileo drew the positions of the little lights night after night until he could prove they were moons. Goodyear tested rubber again and again until he saw that heat and sulfur changed it. Both showed that science needs patient observation and evidence, not just a good idea.

The story

For Illinois's birthday on December 3, Ms. Rivera's class put on a play. Sophia was the narrator. Israel had the strangest part of all: he was the northern border. He carried a very, very long rope.

"Long ago, this land was part of the Northwest Territory," Sophia read. "In 1809, the Illinois Territory was created. A is a region of the United States that is not a state yet."

"To become a state, the territory needed enough people and the approval of ," Sophia went on. "Then it could have its own , choose its own governor and send representatives to Congress."

Then Selena walked on in a cardboard top hat. She was playing Nathaniel Pope, the Illinois Territory's to Congress. She didn't say a word, because Ms. Rivera had explained that we shouldn't make up words for real people. She just pulled Israel's rope toward the north.

"Nathaniel Pope got the northern moved farther north," Sophia read. "That way, Illinois got a shoreline on Lake Michigan, where Chicago would later grow." Israel took three steps backward, careful not to fall off the stage. The audience applauded the border.

"At last, on December 3, 1818, Illinois became the 21st state of the United States," Sophia announced. The president at the time was James Monroe. All the actors shouted, "Happy birthday, Illinois!"

Next came the part with the star. The first was Kaskaskia, on the Mississippi River. In 1820 the capital moved to Vandalia, and in 1839 it moved to Springfield, closer to the middle of the state. Selena, now wearing a straw hat, carried the cardboard star from one side of the stage to the other. She dropped it twice.

Sophia also told the audience that the state's first governor was Shadrach Bond. "What a name!" someone whispered, and Sophia had to bite her lip to keep from laughing.

At the end, Ms. Rivera reminded everyone that in 2018 Illinois celebrated its : 200 years as a state! Israel coiled up his rope. "Being a border is hard work," he said. "But thanks to that pull, Chicago ended up in Illinois."

Glossary

territory
A region of the United States that is not a state yet.
Congress
The part of the United States government that makes the country's laws.
constitution
The written plan for a government. It explains how the government is organized and what rights people have.
delegate
A person chosen to speak and work for a group or a place.
border
The line where one state or country ends and another begins.
capital
The city where a state's or a country's government works.

Questions

Choose the correct answer. If you make a mistake, the story shows you where the answer is.

  1. AQuestion A. What is a territory?

    A territory is a region of the United States that is not a state yet.

  2. BQuestion B. What did Illinois need to become a state?

    The territory needed enough people and the approval of Congress.

  3. CQuestion C. Why was moving the northern border important?

    Moving the border gave Illinois a shoreline on Lake Michigan, where Chicago later grew.

  4. DQuestion D. When did Illinois become a state?

    Illinois became the 21st state on December 3, 1818.

  5. EQuestion E. What was the first capital of Illinois?

    The first capital was Kaskaskia; then came Vandalia and, since 1839, Springfield.

Selena drew three dots on the board, then a bar, then a little shell. "My grandmother is from Yucatán," she told the class. "She showed me how the Maya wrote numbers. A dot is one, a bar is five, and the shell is zero." Ms. Novak smiled. "Teach us, then."

"The Maya built a great in what is now southern Mexico and Central America," said Ms. Novak. "They had cities with stone pyramids, a writing system, and a number system with a symbol for zero, more than 1,500 years ago." "Before Europe had a zero," said Sophia.

Selena showed them how it worked. "They counted in , by twenties instead of tens," she said. "The numbers are stacked: the bottom place counts ones, the next place up counts twenties. So a dot on top of a shell means one twenty and zero ones: twenty." Daniel tried it and wrote 45: two dots on top for two twenties, and a bar on the bottom for five.

"Why did they need such big numbers?" asked Sophia. "For time," said Ms. Novak. "Maya astronomers watched the Sun, the Moon and Venus for centuries. Their calendars counted days, years and huge cycles of time, and they used zero to keep track of it all." "They wrote their knowledge in books called codices: bark paper painted with glyphs, numbers and pictures, folded like an accordion. Each one was a ."

Sophia held up a picture of one. "How many of these books are left?" Ms. Novak paused. "After the Spanish in the 1500s, many Maya books were burned by Spanish priests who saw them as a threat to their religion. Today only four Maya codices are known to survive. One of them, the Dresden Codex, has tables that track the planet Venus."

"That's so sad," said Daniel. "It was a huge loss," said Ms. Novak. "Historians have to study the past from a few books, carvings in stone and paintings on pottery. And they have to remember that most of the written records from that time were written by the Spanish, from their ."

Then she opened her laptop. On the screen was a teacher from Yucatán, Selena's grandmother's friend, wearing a colorful embroidered blouse. She greeted the class in Yucatec Maya, then in Spanish and English. "The Maya are not only in the past," she said. "Millions of Maya people live in Mexico and Central America today, and many of us still speak Mayan languages. Some children are learning the old number system in school again."

After the call, Ms. Novak drew a T-chart: change and continuity. Under change, the class wrote: the books were burned, Spanish became common, the cities were left behind. Under , they wrote: the languages, the people, the numbers. Selena added one more: "My grandmother's stories."

Check your thinking

A dot is one and a bar is five, so two bars and three dots mean 13. The scribes combined colors, pictures and numbers because the codex was both a book of knowledge and a work of art: the pictures showed who or what the numbers were about, and the colors helped readers find important parts.

Glossary

civilization
A large, organized society with cities, government, writing or record keeping, and shared ideas.
civilización: Una sociedad grande y organizada, con ciudades, gobierno, escritura o registros, e ideas compartidas.
base twenty
A number system that groups by twenties instead of tens. The Maya counted this way.
base veinte: Un sistema de numeración que agrupa de veinte en veinte en lugar de diez en diez. Así contaban los mayas.
codex
A handmade book. Maya codices were painted on bark paper and folded like an accordion.
códice: Un libro hecho a mano. Los códices mayas se pintaban en papel de corteza y se doblaban como un acordeón.
conquest
Taking control of a land and its people by force.
conquista: Tomar el control de una tierra y de su gente por la fuerza.
continuity
Something that stays the same or continues over a long time.
continuidad: Algo que se mantiene igual o continúa durante mucho tiempo.
perspective
A point of view: the way a person or group sees an event.
perspectiva: Un punto de vista: la manera en que una persona o un grupo ve un suceso.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Change and continuity

Make your own T-chart about the Maya, with two changes and two things that continued after the Spanish conquest. Then explain why historians must be careful when most written records come from one group.

Cambio y continuidad. Haz tu propia tabla en T sobre los mayas, con dos cambios y dos cosas que continuaron después de la conquista española. Después explica por qué los historiadores deben tener cuidado cuando la mayoría de los registros escritos vienen de un solo grupo.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Changes: most books were burned; Spanish became an official language; many cities were left behind. Continuity: the Maya people, their languages, their knowledge of numbers and many traditions. Historians must be careful because records written by one group show that group's perspective; they need other sources, like carvings, pottery and the stories Maya families tell today.

Go further

Write the number 47 in Maya numbers. Then explain why a symbol for zero is necessary in a place value system.

Para ir más lejos. Escribe el número 47 con números mayas. Después explica por qué un sistema de valor posicional necesita un símbolo para el cero.

Check your thinking

47 = 2 twenties + 7 ones. Top place: two dots (2 × 20 = 40). Bottom place: a bar and two dots (5 + 2 = 7). Zero is necessary because it holds an empty place: without the shell, one twenty and zero ones (20) would look the same as just one dot (1).

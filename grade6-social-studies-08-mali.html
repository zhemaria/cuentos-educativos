The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Ms. Haddad put two things on her desk: a jar of salt and a rock painted gold. "Which one would you trade your lunch for?" she asked. "The gold, obviously," said Selena. Elías wasn't so sure. "Depends on how hungry I am."

"Seven hundred years ago, in West Africa, people would trade gold for salt," said Ms. Haddad. She pointed to a map. In the north was the Sahara, a huge desert. In the south were green lands along the Niger River. "Deep in the Sahara, there were mines full of salt, but almost nothing grew there. In the south, there was gold in the ground, but very little salt."

"Why did they need salt so much?" asked Selena. "In the hot, wet south, people and animals lose salt when they sweat, and they need it to stay healthy," said Ms. Haddad. "Salt also kept meat and fish from spoiling. In the south, salt was , so people valued it very highly."

Each place had a the other needed. "So merchants crossed the Sahara with caravans of camels, carrying huge slabs of salt south and gold north. Gold was one of the main of the lands in the south, and salt was one of its main ." The gold then traveled on to North Africa and Europe, where it was turned into coins.

Right in the middle of these trade routes grew the of Mali. "Mali's rulers didn't need to dig the gold themselves," said Ms. Haddad. "They controlled the routes and the trading cities, like Timbuktu, and they collected taxes on the goods that passed through. That made Mali one of the richest empires in the world."

"Its most famous ruler was Mansa Musa," she continued. "Mansa means king. In 1324, he made a to Mecca, the holy city of Islam, with thousands of people and many camels carrying gold." "When he passed through Cairo, in Egypt, he gave away and spent so much gold that, for years, gold there was worth less than before."

"He gave away so much gold that gold got cheaper?" said Elías. "That's like when everybody has the same trading card, and nobody wants it anymore." "Exactly," said Ms. Haddad. "And people in Europe and the Middle East started talking about Mali. Mansa Musa also brought scholars and builders back home, and Timbuktu became famous for its mosques and libraries."

At the end of class, Elías picked up the jar of salt. "I'd still trade the gold for the salt," he said. "Why?" asked Selena. "Because if I lived in the south seven hundred years ago, I would be sweaty, and my fish would be spoiled." Ms. Haddad laughed. "Spoken like a true merchant."

Glossary

natural resource
Something from nature that people use, like water, gold, salt or wood.
recurso natural: Algo de la naturaleza que la gente usa, como agua, oro, sal o madera.
scarce
When there is not enough of something for everyone who wants it.
escaso: Cuando no hay suficiente de algo para todos los que lo quieren.
empire
A large area of many lands and peoples ruled by one government or ruler.
imperio: Una gran zona de muchas tierras y pueblos gobernada por un solo gobierno o gobernante.
export
A product that a place sells to other places.
exportación: Un producto que un lugar vende a otros lugares.
import
A product that a place buys from other places.
importación: Un producto que un lugar compra a otros lugares.
pilgrimage
A long journey to a holy place for religious reasons.
peregrinación: Un viaje largo a un lugar sagrado por motivos religiosos.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Connect environment and trade

Explain how the environment of the Sahara and of the lands in the south led to the gold and salt trade. Use the words resource, scarce, export and import.

Conecta el ambiente y el comercio. Explica cómo el ambiente del Sahara y el de las tierras del sur llevaron al comercio de oro y sal. Usa las palabras recurso, escaso, exportación e importación.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

The Sahara had salt as a natural resource, but almost no food grew there. The south had gold, but salt was scarce. So each region traded what it had for what it lacked: gold was an export of the south, and salt was an import. The environment decided what each place could produce and what it needed to buy.

Go further

Elías compared Mansa Musa's gold to a trading card that everybody has. Use this idea to explain why something becomes worth less when there is much more of it. Can you think of a modern example?

Para ir más lejos. Elías comparó el oro de Mansa Musa con una tarjeta que todos tienen. Usa esta idea para explicar por qué algo vale menos cuando hay mucho más. ¿Se te ocurre un ejemplo de hoy?

Check your thinking

When something is scarce, many people want it and it is worth a lot. When there is suddenly much more of it, fewer people need to pay a high price, so its value goes down. Modern examples: a toy that is rare at first and then sold everywhere, or strawberries that are cheaper in summer when there are many of them.

The story

On the playground, Daniel and Salma built a giant sand mountain with a castle on top. "This is going to last forever," said Daniel. That night it rained and rained. By the next morning, the mountain had turned into a flat little lump, and sand was spread all over the playground, even on the basketball court.

"Where did my castle go?" Daniel asked sadly. Ms. Patel walked him back to the classroom. "Let's find out with an experiment."

They filled a tray with sand and soil and tilted it on a book. Salma sprinkled water on it with a watering can, like rain. The water ran down in little channels and dragged the sand into a puddle at the end of the tray. "That's ," the teacher explained. "Water carries away the bits of soil and rock, called , and drops them somewhere else."

They measured with a ruler: the channels were one centimeter deep after the first rain and three centimeters deep after the third. The more water ran down, the more sand it carried away.

"What if the soil had plants on it?" Daniel asked. They set up another tray with grass and little plants and watered it the same way. This time the sand hardly moved. "The roots of hold the soil in place," said the teacher, "and protect it from erosion."

"But rocks aren't made of sand," said Salma. "They're hard." The teacher showed them a rock with a crack in it. "Water gets into cracks. In winter it freezes, and ice takes up more space than water. So it pushes on the rock and breaks it into smaller pieces. That's called ." It happens a lot in Chicago. That's why the streets get so many potholes after winter.

"Wind can carry sand away too," the teacher added, "and tree roots can split rocks apart. And long ago, huge covered most of Illinois. As they moved, they scraped the land and left it very flat."

Athena walked over to the big of the United States on the wall. "Look," she said. "Most of the tall mountains are in the West, and there are some lower ones in the East. In the middle, where Illinois is, there are big plains." Rivers flowed down from the mountains, and many of them joined the Mississippi River, which went all the way to the sea.

"Maps help us see patterns in ," said the teacher, "like where the mountains, valleys, plains and rivers are." Daniel looked at the map and then out the window. "So my castle didn't disappear. It went on a trip, just like the sand that rivers carry to the sea."

Glossary

weathering
When rock breaks into smaller pieces because of water, ice, wind or plants.
erosion
When water, wind or ice carry bits of rock and soil away to another place.
sediment
Bits of sand, soil and rock that water or wind carry away and drop somewhere else.
vegetation
The plants that cover a place, like grass, bushes and trees.
glacier
A huge mass of ice that moves very slowly over the land.
map
A drawing of a place seen from above that shows where things are.
landform
A natural shape of the land, like a mountain, a valley, a canyon or a plain.

Chapter 1: Adrián's drum

Sound and vibrations

Grade 1 · Science · 1-PS4-1

A story about how sounds are made.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • What you hear with your ears.

  • To move back and forth very fast.

  • When there is no sound at all.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Adrián made a drum with a box and a stretched balloon.

He put some grains of rice on top.

Génesis tapped the drum: boom, boom!

The grains of rice jumped!

“The balloon is moving very fast,” said Adrián. “It is vibrating.”

When something vibrates, it makes a sound.

Génesis put her hand on the balloon.

The balloon stopped moving, and the drum was silent.

Then Adrián stretched a rubber band and plucked it with his finger.

The rubber band vibrated and made a sound: boing!

“My dinosaur likes music!” Adrián laughed.

Things that vibrate make sound. When they stop vibrating, the sound goes away.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. What did the grains of rice do when Génesis tapped the drum?

    The balloon vibrated and the grains of rice jumped.

  2. BQuestion B. What happened when Adrián plucked the rubber band?

    The rubber band vibrated, so it made a sound: boing!

  3. CQuestion C. Why did the drum become silent?

    Her hand stopped the balloon; no vibration means no sound.

Illinois standard (NGSS) 1-PS4-1: plan and conduct investigations to show that vibrating materials can make sound and that sound can make materials vibrate.

Chapter 2: The pillow cave

We need light to see

Grade 1 · Science · 1-PS4-2

A story about light and darkness.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • What lets us see things.

  • When there is no light.

  • A small lamp you hold in your hand.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Israel and Lucía made a cave with cushions and a blanket.

They crawled inside and closed the door.

Everything was black. It was darkness!

“I can’t see my book,” said Lucía. “I can’t even see my hand!”

Israel turned on a flashlight.

The light reached the book, and now they could see it!

Israel pointed the flashlight at the roof of the cave.

They saw the little stars on the blanket, but the book disappeared in the dark.

“We only see what the light shines on,” said Lucía.

Israel pointed the flashlight at his teddy bear, Canela. There she was!

At the end, they opened the cave, and the sunlight came in.

To see things, we need light.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. Why couldn’t Lucía see her book?

    In the dark, we cannot see anything.

  2. BQuestion B. What happened when the light reached the book?

    When light shines on something, we can see it.

  3. CQuestion C. Why couldn’t they see the book when the flashlight pointed at the roof?

    The book was left in the dark, with no light.

Illinois standard (NGSS) 1-PS4-2: make observations to show that objects can be seen only when illuminated.

Chapter 3: What lets the light through?

Materials and light

Grade 1 · Science · 1-PS4-3

A story about materials that let light through, block it or bounce it.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • It lets light through; you can see through it.

  • It does not let light through.

  • It bounces light back and shows your reflection.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Génesis and Lucía were playing with a flashlight next to the wall.

They put a glass cup in front of the light.

The light went through the cup! The glass is transparent.

Next, they put a piece of wax paper there.

A little light went through, but the spot on the wall looked blurry.

Then they put a book there.

The light could not go through, and a shadow appeared on the wall. The book is opaque.

Génesis put her teddy bear in front of the light.

“Look, my teddy bear’s shadow has a bow!” she laughed.

Last, Lucía held up a mirror.

The light bounced and made a bright circle on the ceiling.

Some materials let light through, some block it, and some bounce it.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. What happened to the light and the glass cup?

    Glass is transparent: it lets light through.

  2. BQuestion B. Why did a shadow appear when they put the book there?

    The book is opaque: it blocks the light and makes a shadow.

  3. CQuestion C. What did the mirror do with the light?

    A mirror bounces light back.

Illinois standard (NGSS) 1-PS4-3: investigate what happens when different materials are placed in the path of a beam of light.

Chapter 4: The cup phone

Communicating with sound and light

Grade 1 · Science · 1-PS4-4

A story about sending messages with sound and with light.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • What you want to tell someone.

  • A sound or a light that says something without words.

  • A very thin cord.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Adrián and Israel wanted to talk from far away without shouting.

They made a phone with two paper cups and a long string.

Adrián talked into his cup: “Can you hear me, Israel?”

His voice made the string vibrate, and the sound traveled to the other cup.

“I hear you!” Israel answered, with the cup on his ear.

But when the string was loose, they could not hear anything.

“We have to pull the string tight!” said Adrián.

Later, at night, they made up another signal.

With a flashlight, one flash meant “hello,” and two flashes meant “come and play.”

Israel saw two flashes in the window and ran out to play.

Adrián brought his green dinosaur so it could play too.

With sound or with light, we can send a message to someone far away.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. How did Adrián’s voice get to the other cup?

    His voice made the string vibrate, and the sound traveled along it.

  2. BQuestion B. When could they not hear anything?

    The string has to be tight for the sound to travel.

  3. CQuestion C. What did two flashes of the flashlight mean?

    One flash meant “hello” and two flashes meant “come and play.”

Illinois standard (NGSS) 1-PS4-4: use tools and materials to design and build a device that uses light or sound to communicate over a distance.

Chapter 5: Pelusa's burrs

Ideas we copy from nature

Grade 1 · Science · 1-LS1-1

A story about inventions that copy plants and animals.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • A seed with tiny hooks that sticks to clothes and fur.

  • A bent tip that grabs things.

  • A plan for building something.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Pelusa, Génesis’s little dog, came back from the field covered in little balls.

They were burrs, seeds that stick to fur.

Génesis and Lucía looked at a burr with a magnifying glass.

It had lots and lots of tiny hooks!

“That’s why they hold on to Pelusa’s fur,” said Lucía.

Grandma told them that a man noticed the same thing a long time ago.

He copied the tiny hooks on the burrs and invented the Velcro on our shoes.

The girls wanted to copy nature too.

They thought about a turtle’s hard shell, which protects it.

They made a design: a hard helmet to protect your head on a bike.

Pelusa wagged her tail, with no more burrs.

Plants and animals give us ideas for new inventions.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. What did they see with the magnifying glass?

    A burr has lots of tiny hooks that grab on.

  2. BQuestion B. What was invented by copying burrs?

    The tiny hooks on burrs gave the idea for Velcro.

  3. CQuestion C. Which animal gave them the idea for the helmet?

    A hard shell protects the turtle, like a helmet.

Illinois standard (NGSS) 1-LS1-1: use materials to design a solution to a human problem by mimicking how plants and animals use their parts to survive.

Chapter 6: Mother duck and her ducklings

Parents take care of their babies

Grade 1 · Science · 1-LS1-2

A story about how animal parents take care of their young.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • Baby animals.

  • To keep someone safe from harm.

  • The little home birds make for their eggs.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Israel and Lucía went to the park pond with Lucía’s grandpa.

By the water there was a nest with eggs.

One day, five ducklings hatched from the eggs. They were the mother duck’s young.

The ducklings walked in a line behind their mother.

One duckling fell behind and started peeping very loudly: peep, peep!

The mother duck heard it and went back for it.

Then a dog came running over.

The mother duck spread her wings and quacked to protect her ducklings.

The dog went away, and the ducklings hid under their mother’s wings.

“Just like my mom takes care of me,” said Israel.

Grandpa smiled. “Animal parents take care of their children too.”

Young animals call for help, and their parents feed them, care for them and protect them.

  3. CQuestion C. What did the mother duck do when the dog came?

    The mother protects her young from danger.

Young animals are like their parents

A story about how young animals are like their parents, and how they are different.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • Having things that are the same.

  • Not the same.

  • The hair that covers an animal.

The story

Mrs. Rosa’s cat had four kittens.

Génesis and Adrián went to meet them.

The mother cat has black and white fur.

Two kittens are black and white, like their mother.

One kitten is gray, like its father, the neighbor’s cat.

“They are all alike in some ways,” said Génesis. “They have whiskers, tails and pointy ears, like their parents.”

Adrián held up his green dinosaur, and the little kitten sniffed its tail.

“Your dinosaur doesn’t look like anyone here!” Génesis laughed.

Mrs. Rosa told them the kittens will grow up to be big cats.

Young animals are like their parents, but they are not exactly the same.

Chapter 8: Where does the sun go?

Patterns of the sun and moon

Grade 1 · Science · 1-ESS1-1 · 1-ESS1-2

A story about the sun, the moon and the seasons.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • When the sun comes up in the morning.

  • When the sun goes down in the evening.

  • A part of the year: spring, summer, fall or winter.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

One winter day, Adrián left school and it was almost dark.

“I want to play outside!” he said. “Where did the sun go?”

His dad explained, “Every day the sun comes up in the morning. That’s sunrise.”

“And every evening it goes down. That’s sunset.”

The moon appeared in the dark sky. That night it looked like a fingernail.

Every night, Adrián drew the moon in his notebook.

The moon grew and grew until it was a circle. A full moon!

Then it got smaller again. It always follows the same path.

“In winter there are fewer hours of daylight,” said Dad. “In summer there are more.”

Each season has its pattern.

In summer, Adrián and Israel played outside until late.

The sun, the moon and the stars have patterns that repeat.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. When does the sun come up?

    Every day the sun comes up in the morning: that’s sunrise.

  2. BQuestion B. What shape was the moon the first night?

    That night the moon was thin, like a fingernail.

  3. CQuestion C. Which season has more hours of daylight?

    Summer has more hours of daylight than winter.

Illinois standards (NGSS) 1-ESS1-1 and 1-ESS1-2: use observations of the sun, moon and stars to describe patterns that can be predicted, and relate the amount of daylight to the time of year.

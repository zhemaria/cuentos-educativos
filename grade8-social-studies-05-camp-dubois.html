The bus stopped in Hartford, Illinois, near the place where the Missouri River meets the Mississippi. In front of the class stood a log fort and a huge wooden boat. "Welcome to Camp Dubois," said Ms. Novak. "Or at least, a copy of it."

"In December 1803, William Clark and his men built their winter camp near here, on the Illinois side of the Mississippi," she said. "They spent about five months preparing for an up the Missouri River." "Why here?" asked Daniel. "Because the land across the river was still not officially in American hands," said Ms. Novak. "That was about to change."

"Earlier in 1803, the United States had bought a huge piece of land from France, the ," she said. "President Jefferson wanted the land explored and mapped, and he wanted a water route to the Pacific." "The rivers were the highways," said Salma.

"Math to make a map," said Israel.

"On May 14, 1804, the expedition left the camp and started up the Missouri," said Ms. Novak. "Lewis wrote that the mouth of the Wood River, right here, was to be considered the point of departure." She pointed to the sign. "And here's a surprise: the rivers have moved since then. The real campsite is now on the other side of the Mississippi."

"Who went on the trip?" asked Selena. "About forty people at the start," said Ms. Novak. "Soldiers, boatmen and York, a man enslaved by Clark, who did the same hard work as everyone else but was not free. Later, they were joined by Sacagawea, a Lemhi Shoshone woman, who traveled with her baby and helped as an ."

"Many books say that Lewis and Clark explored an unknown land," said Ms. Novak. "But from the of the Native nations, it wasn't unknown. Dozens of nations lived along the route, traded with the travelers, gave them food and directions, and some of them saw the expedition as a sign of changes that would threaten their lands."

On the bus home, Elías looked out at the Mississippi. "The journey started in Illinois," he said. "And it only worked because of people who already knew the way." Sophia wrote in her notebook: Every map has a starting point. Ask who was already standing there.

Check your thinking

Clark mainly drew the rivers, where they met and where the camp was, because the expedition traveled by water and needed to know its starting point. A field sketch is quick and records what he saw on the spot; a finished map is cleaned up later, combines many sketches and measurements, and is made to share with others, like the president.

Glossary

expedition
A journey made by a group of people for a special purpose, like exploring or mapping.
expedición: Un viaje que hace un grupo de personas con un propósito especial, como explorar o hacer mapas.
Louisiana Purchase
The land the United States bought from France in 1803, west of the Mississippi River.
Compra de Luisiana: Las tierras al oeste del río Misisipi que los Estados Unidos le compraron a Francia en 1803.
latitude
A measure of how far north or south of the equator a place is.
latitud: Una medida de qué tan al norte o al sur del ecuador está un lugar.
sextant
An instrument that measures the angle between the Sun or a star and the horizon. It helps find latitude.
sextante: Un instrumento que mide el ángulo entre el Sol o una estrella y el horizonte. Sirve para hallar la latitud.
interpreter
A person who helps people who speak different languages understand each other.
intérprete: Una persona que ayuda a que se entiendan quienes hablan idiomas distintos.
perspective
A point of view: the way a person or group sees an event.
perspectiva: Un punto de vista: la manera en que una persona o un grupo ve un suceso.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Compare perspectives

Describe the day the expedition left Camp Dubois from three perspectives: William Clark, York and a member of a Native nation along the Missouri who hears that the travelers are coming. What might each one hope for, or worry about?

Compara perspectivas. Describe el día en que la expedición salió de Camp Dubois desde tres perspectivas: William Clark, York y un miembro de una nación originaria a orillas del Misuri que se entera de que vienen los viajeros. ¿Qué podría esperar o temer cada uno?

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Clark might hope for success, new maps and a route to the Pacific, and worry about danger and supplies. York did the same hard work but was not free; he might hope for respect or freedom, and worry about being treated unequally. A member of a Native nation might hope for trade and allies, and worry that the strangers were a sign that more people would come and take their land. The same event looks different from each point of view.

Check your thinking

Chicago is about 41 to 42 degrees north, so it is north of Camp Dubois, which is near 38 degrees 55 minutes. Every degree of latitude is about 69 miles, so Chicago is roughly 200 miles farther north.

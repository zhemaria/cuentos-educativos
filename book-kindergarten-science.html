Chapter 1: The dinosaur's wagon

Pushes and pulls

Kindergarten · Science · K-PS2-1

A story about pushing, pulling and how things move.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • To move something away from you.

  • To move something toward you.

  • The way something is going.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Adrián has a plush dinosaur.

He wants to take it to the park in his red wagon.

Adrián puts his hands behind the wagon and gives it a push.

The wagon rolls away from him!

Génesis takes the wagon's rope and gives it a pull.

The wagon comes toward her!

Adrián pushes the wagon gently. The wagon moves just a little.

Then he pushes it hard. The wagon goes fast and rolls far!

Oops! The wagon is rolling straight toward a puddle.

Adrián pushes it from the side, and the wagon changes direction.

"We saved the dinosaur!" Adrián laughs.

A push or a pull can move things and change the way they go.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. What happens when Génesis pulls the rope?

    When she pulls, the wagon comes toward Génesis.

  2. BQuestion B. When does the wagon go the farthest?

    With a hard push, the wagon goes fast and rolls far.

  3. CQuestion C. How did Adrián save the dinosaur from the puddle?

    He pushed it from the side, and the wagon changed direction.

Illinois standard (NGSS) K-PS2-1: compare how an object moves with pushes and pulls of different strengths and directions.

Chapter 2: The ball that would not roll

A ramp for the ball

Kindergarten · Science · K-PS2-2

A story about a ramp and a ball that loves to travel.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • A slanted board: one end is high and the other end is low.

  • The way something is going.

  • A plan we make and test to solve a problem.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Génesis and Adrián are playing with a red ball in the hallway.

Adrián puts the ball on the floor, but it hardly moves.

"Let's make a ramp!" says Génesis.

They put a board on top of a book.

Adrián lets go of the ball at the top of the ramp. The ball rolls down!

"What if the ramp is higher?" asks Adrián. They use three books.

Now the ball rolls down faster and goes farther, all the way to the door!

Now they want the ball to go into the toy box, off to one side.

Génesis puts a block in the way. The ball bumps it, changes direction and rolls into the box.

"Our design works!" they both shout.

To know if it worked, they tried it many times and watched how far the ball went.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. What happened when the ramp was higher?

  2. BQuestion B. How did they get the ball into the box?

    The block changed the ball's direction, and the ball rolled into the box.

  3. CQuestion C. How did they know their design worked?

    They tried it many times and watched how far the ball went.

Illinois standard (NGSS) K-PS2-2: find out whether a design works to change the speed or direction of an object with a push or a pull.

Chapter 3: The hot sidewalk

The sun warms things

Kindergarten · Science · K-PS3-1

A story about the sun, the shade and two pairs of bare feet.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • The star that gives us light and heat during the day.

  • A darker, cooler place where something blocks the sunlight.

  • To make something hotter.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

It is a summer day. Adrián and Génesis go out to the yard barefoot.

"Ouch, ouch, ouch!" Adrián hops. "The sidewalk is hot!"

The sidewalk out in the sun is very hot.

Génesis walks to the shade of the tree. "It's cool here," she says.

They touch the sidewalk with their hands. In the sun, hot. In the shade, cool.

Adrián leaves his dinosaur in the sun. Génesis leaves her teddy bear in the shade.

After a while, the dinosaur is warm and the teddy bear is still cool.

"Why?" asks Adrián.

"Because the sun warms the things it shines on," says Génesis.

The sun warms the sidewalk, the sand, the water and even your dinosaur.

Adrián puts on his sandals. Now it doesn't hurt!

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. How was the sidewalk out in the sun?

    The sidewalk out in the sun was very hot.

  2. BQuestion B. How was the dinosaur that stayed in the sun?

    The sun warmed the dinosaur. The teddy bear, in the shade, stayed cool.

  3. CQuestion C. What things does the sun warm?

    The sun warms the sidewalk, the sand, the water and everything it shines on.

Chapter 4: Shade for Pelusa

Making shade to stay cool

Kindergarten · Science · K-PS3-2

A story about a hot little puppy and a homemade shade.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • A darker, cooler place where something blocks the sunlight.

  • A little cold; nice when it is hot outside.

  • To make something by putting parts together, like sticks and a bedsheet.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Pelusa the puppy is playing in the yard. It is very sunny.

Pelusa pants with her tongue out. She is so hot!

"Let's make her some shade!" says Génesis.

Adrián brings four sticks. Génesis brings a thin towel and a thick bedsheet.

First they try the thin towel. The sunlight shines right through it!

Then they put the thick bedsheet over the sticks. Underneath, there is a big shade.

They touch the ground with their hands.

Under the bedsheet, the ground is cooler than in the sun.

Pelusa lies down in the shade and wags her tail.

"We built it ourselves!" says Adrián.

The bedsheet blocks the sunlight, so the ground underneath gets less warm.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. What did they use to make a big shade?

    They put the thick bedsheet over the sticks, and there was a big shade underneath.

  2. BQuestion B. How was the ground under the bedsheet?

    Under the bedsheet, the ground was cooler than in the sun.

  3. CQuestion C. Why does the shade get less warm?

    The bedsheet blocks the sunlight, so the ground underneath gets less warm.

What plants and animals need

Kindergarten · Science · K-LS1-1

A story about what living things need to live.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • What all living things need to drink or get in order to live.

  • What lets us see; sunlight helps plants grow.

  • What animals and people eat to live.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Génesis has two little bean plants. Adrián has a turtle named Tortu.

One plant is by the window. The other one is in a dark corner.

Génesis gives both plants water every day.

After a week, the plant by the window is green and big.

The plant in the corner is pale and droopy. It gets no light!

Génesis moves it to the window. Little by little, it turns green again.

"Plants need water and light to live," says Génesis.

Tortu swims slowly toward Adrián. He is hungry!

Adrián gives him his food and changes his water.

"Tortu doesn't eat light like plants do," Adrián laughs. "He eats turtle food!"

Plants need water and light. Animals need water and food.

  2. BQuestion B. What did Adrián give Tortu?

  3. CQuestion C. What do animals need to live?

    Animals need water and food. Plants need water and light.

Chapter 6: The weather calendar

Weather every day

Kindergarten · Science · K-ESS2-1

A story about sun, clouds, rain and snow.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • A day with sunshine and a blue sky.

  • A day when water falls from the clouds.

  • A very cold day when snowflakes fall.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Every morning, Adrián and Génesis look out the window.

Then they stick a picture on the weather calendar.

On Monday the sky is blue. It is a sunny day, so they stick on a sun.

On Tuesday, drops fall from the sky. It is a rainy day, so they stick on a cloud with raindrops.

On Wednesday there are gray clouds, but no rain. They stick on a cloud.

On Thursday it is very cold, and white flakes fall.

It is a snowy day! Adrián and Génesis make a snowman.

On Friday the sun comes out again. They stick on another sun.

At the end of the week, they count the pictures.

"There were two sunny days! That happened the most," says Génesis.

When we look at the calendar, we know what the weather has been like.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. What picture did they stick on Tuesday?

    It rained on Tuesday, so they stuck on a cloud with raindrops.

  2. BQuestion B. What did they do on the snowy day?

    It snowed on Thursday, and Adrián and Génesis made a snowman.

  3. CQuestion C. What weather happened the most that week?

    There were two sunny days. That happened the most.

Illinois standard (NGSS) K-ESS2-1: observe and record daily weather to describe patterns over time.

Chapter 8: Ready for the storm

Severe weather and caring for the Earth

Kindergarten · Science · K-ESS3-2 · K-ESS3-3

A story about a storm and about saving water and caring for the Earth.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • What people tell us about what the weather is going to be like.

  • A lot of rain with wind, lightning and thunder.

  • To put used things, like bottles, aside so they can be made into new things.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

In the morning, Adrián and Génesis watch the weather forecast on TV.

"This afternoon there will be a storm," says the weather lady.

So they put away the toys in the yard and close the windows.

In the afternoon, the sky gets dark. Boom! Thunder rumbles.

Adrián and Génesis are safe inside the house. They listen to the rain.

When the storm is over, a rainbow comes out.

Before bed, Adrián brushes his teeth and turns off the water.

"That way we don't waste water," he says.

Génesis puts the plastic bottles in the blue bin to recycle.

"We take care of the Earth when we save water and recycle," says Génesis.

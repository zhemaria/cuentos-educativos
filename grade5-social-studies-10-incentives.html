The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Israel found a pile of library books under his bed. Some had been there since last year. "Oh no," he groaned. "I'll have to pay a hundred dollars in fines." His older brother, Gabriel, laughed. "Not anymore. The Chicago Public Library stopped charging late in 2019."

At the library, the librarian smiled as Israel piled up the books. "When we had fines, many families stopped coming because they owed money," she said. "When we ended them, lots of people came back, and many old books were returned." Israel was amazed. "So taking away a punishment made people do the right thing?"

In class, Mr. Okafor explained it. "A fine is a : it's supposed to make people return books on time. An , like a prize or a sale price, encourages people to do something." "Both change what people do," he said. "But sometimes a fine pushes people away instead."

That week, Athena and Elías were in charge of the school bake sale. Athena designed a poster with drawings of cookies and cupcakes. At the bottom, Elías wrote: "TODAY ONLY: buy 2 cookies, get 1 FREE!"

"Why 'today only'?" asked Daniel. "Because it makes people hurry," said Elías. "If they think the deal will end soon, they'll buy now." Mr. Okafor nodded. "Every is made to convince you. Good shoppers ask: do I really need this, or is the poster just making me want it?"

The sale worked. They sold every cookie before lunch. Meanwhile, Israel had a new problem: he wanted a used bike that cost 50 dollars, but he only had 20. "Gabriel, can I 30 dollars?" he asked.

Gabriel thought about it. "OK. But you'll pay me back 33 dollars by the end of the summer." "Thirty-three? You're only giving me thirty!" "The three extra dollars are the ," said Gabriel. "It's the price you pay for using my money now, instead of waiting."

Israel told Mr. Okafor, expecting him to say it was unfair. "Your brother is acting like a bank," said the teacher. "Banks lend money and charge interest. That's how they earn money for lending it. The longer you take to pay back a loan, the more interest you usually pay."

Israel did the math. If he saved his allowance every week, he could pay Gabriel back in two months. And by the end of the summer, he had the bike and no debt. "Next time," he told Gabriel, "I'll save first and buy later. Then the interest stays in my pocket." Gabriel groaned. "There goes my business."

Check your thinking

Tricks: big bold letters and a bright red label that catch your eye; "TODAY ONLY" makes you hurry; "FREE" feels like a prize; and "Every dollar helps our class trip" makes you feel good about buying. Ads are media art made to change what people do, so it's smart to notice their tricks.

Glossary

incentive
Something that encourages people to do something, like a prize or a sale price.
incentivo: Algo que anima a las personas a hacer algo, como un premio o un precio rebajado.
negative consequence
Something unpleasant that happens because of what you did, like a fine.
consecuencia negativa: Algo desagradable que pasa por lo que hiciste, como una multa.
fine
Money a person must pay as a punishment for breaking a rule.
multa: Dinero que una persona debe pagar como castigo por romper una regla.
borrow
To take something that you must give back later.
pedir prestado: Tomar algo que debes devolver más tarde.
interest
The price a borrower pays for using someone else's money.
interés: El precio que paga quien pide prestado por usar el dinero de otra persona.
advertisement
A message, like a poster or video, made to convince people to buy or do something.
anuncio: Un mensaje, como un cartel o un video, hecho para convencer a la gente de comprar o hacer algo.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Decide with incentives

Your class wants students to bring back their permission slips on time. Design one positive incentive and one negative consequence. Which one do you think would work better, and why?

Decide con incentivos. Tu clase quiere que los alumnos devuelvan a tiempo los permisos firmados. Diseña un incentivo positivo y una consecuencia negativa. ¿Cuál crees que funcionaría mejor y por qué?

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Positive incentive: everyone who returns the slip by Friday gets five extra minutes of recess. Negative consequence: students who are late lose their place in line. Many people think the positive incentive works better, because it makes students want to help, like the library: when the fines ended, more people came back.

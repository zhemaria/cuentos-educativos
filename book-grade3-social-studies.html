The wind carried sparks from one roof to the next. The fire began to north and east. In the early morning of October 9, the flames jumped the Chicago River, burned downtown and reached the waterworks. Without water, the firefighters could hardly put it out.

Then the guide showed them a drawing of a cow. "The story of Mrs. O'Leary's cow is a ," she explained. "In 1893, a reporter named Michael Ahern admitted that he had made it up to make his article more exciting." In 1997, the Chicago City Council officially declared Mrs. O'Leary and her cow innocent.

"Poor cow!" laughed Israel. "For more than a hundred years, people blamed her for something no one could prove." The guide nodded. "Even a primary source can contain mistakes. Historians compare sources and ask how the writer knew what happened."

Before they left, they saw something else surprising: after the fire, people in England sent more than 8,000 books to Chicago. Those books helped start the Chicago Public Library, the same library where Lucía has her card!

That night, Israel wrote to Jamal: "We don't have enough evidence to blame the cow. We still don't know exactly how the fire started." And he drew a cow with a huge smile.

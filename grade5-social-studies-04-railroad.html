The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

The class met at Union Station, in downtown Chicago. On one of the long wooden benches, Mr. Okafor unrolled an old map of the railroads in the 1880s. Dozens of lines crossed it, like the strings of a giant spider web. "Look," said Elías. "All the lines meet here, in Chicago."

"That's right," said Mr. Okafor. "By the late 1800s, Chicago was the biggest railroad center in the country. Trains brought cattle, wheat, corn and lumber from the West, and they carried furniture, tools and clothes back out."

"Why didn't every town make everything for itself?" asked Sophia. "Because it's better to ," said the teacher. "Farmers on the prairie were great at growing wheat. Factories in Chicago were great at making tools. With the railroad, they could with each other."

Daniel turned on his tablet. "Look what I found." It was an old photo from May 10, 1869. Two steam engines stood face to face in the desert of Utah, and a crowd of men cheered between them. "This is the day they finished the first railroad," said Mr. Okafor. "Now trains could cross the whole country."

"Who built it?" asked Sophia. "Thousands of workers," said the teacher. "On the western part, most of them were Chinese immigrants. They dug tunnels through the mountains, in snow and heat, for less pay than other workers."

Sophia zoomed in on the photo. "But I don't see them." Mr. Okafor nodded. "Historians have noticed that too. Very few Chinese workers appear in the famous photos of that day, even though they did a huge part of the work. They were ."

"The railroad was an amazing ," Mr. Okafor continued, "but it also changed nature. Trains brought hunters and settlers onto the Great Plains. Millions of bison were killed for their hides, and by the late 1880s, fewer than a thousand were left. For the Plains nations, who depended on the bison, it was a disaster."

"So the railroad helped some people and hurt others," said Daniel. "That's why historians look at an event from many sides," said the teacher. "Who built it? Who made money? Who lost their land or their food? The of the West were changed forever."

On the way out, Sophia took a picture of the whole class in the station. Then she looked at it and frowned. "Wait. Elías isn't in it." "I was behind the camera, helping you!" said Elías. "See?" said Daniel, laughing. "Now you know how the railroad workers felt."

Check your thinking

The handshake shows East and West joining together, so the photo tells a story of unity and success. But a photo also shows what the photographer chose to include. Adding a Chinese worker would tell a more complete story about who built the railroad.

Glossary

transcontinental
Going across a whole continent, from one ocean to the other.
transcontinental: Que cruza todo un continente, de un océano al otro.
trade
Buying, selling or exchanging goods with other people or places.
comercio: Comprar, vender o intercambiar bienes con otras personas o lugares.
specialize
To focus on making just a few kinds of goods or doing a few kinds of work.
especializarse: Dedicarse a producir solo unos pocos tipos de bienes o hacer pocos tipos de trabajo.
natural resource
Something from nature that people use, like land, water, trees or animals.
recurso natural: Algo de la naturaleza que usan las personas, como la tierra, el agua, los árboles o los animales.
technology
New tools and machines that people invent to solve problems.
tecnología: Herramientas y máquinas nuevas que las personas inventan para resolver problemas.
left out
Not included or not shown, even though they were there and took part.
excluidos: Que no aparecen o no se incluyen, aunque estuvieron allí y participaron.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Look at both sides

Make a T-chart. On one side, list two ways the railroad helped people. On the other side, list two ways it hurt people or nature. Then explain whether you think it was a good change for everyone.

Mira los dos lados. Haz una tabla en T. De un lado, anota dos formas en que el ferrocarril ayudó a la gente. Del otro, dos formas en que dañó a personas o a la naturaleza. Después explica si crees que fue un buen cambio para todos.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Helped: goods could travel fast across the country; farmers and factories could trade, and Chicago grew. Hurt: Chinese workers had dangerous jobs and lower pay; the bison were almost wiped out, and the Plains nations lost their food and land. It was not a good change for everyone: it helped some groups and hurt others.

The story

When the class walked in, the desks were lined up in one long snake across the room. Each desk had a paper sign: Chang'an, Dunhuang, Samarkand, Baghdad, Antioch. "Today, each desk is a city on the Silk Road," said Ms. Haddad. "And you are ."

"The Silk Road wasn't one road," she explained. "It was a network of , thousands of miles long, that connected China with Central Asia, India, the Middle East, Africa and Europe." Merchants used these routes for more than 1,500 years, starting about 2,100 years ago, she said, and few merchants ever traveled the whole thing.

She gave Sophia, at the Chang'an desk, a red silk scarf. "In China, people knew how to make silk from silkworms, and people far away wanted it badly. Pass it west, and each merchant can sell it for a little more." The scarf traveled from desk to desk. By the time it reached Antioch, Ms. Haddad had written its price on the board five times, each one higher.

"Why didn't one person just carry it the whole way?" asked Elías. "Because the trip was brutal," said Ms. Haddad. "Deserts, high mountains, bandits." "Merchants traveled in of camels, which can walk for days with little water. They stopped at each , where they could drink, rest and trade."

Then Ms. Haddad gave Athena, at the Antioch desk, a jar of glass beads, and Salma, at Baghdad, a small bag of cinnamon. " went in both directions," she said. "Silk and paper moved west. Glass, wool, horses and silver moved east. Spices came from India and Southeast Asia."

"Now here's the real treasure," she said, and she whispered a sentence into Sophia's ear. Sophia whispered it to Elías, Elías to Salma, Salma to Athena. Athena laughed. "Camels love to dance in the moonlight?" Ms. Haddad laughed too. "I said, Camels can carry a lot in the moonlight." "Ideas traveled on the Silk Road too, and they changed as they moved from person to person, just like your message."

"Which ideas?" asked Salma. "Buddhism traveled from India to China along these routes. Papermaking traveled from China to the Middle East and then to Europe. Foods, music and art traveled too. When ideas and inventions spread from one people to another, we call it ." "Did anything bad travel?" asked Elías. "Yes. Diseases also traveled with the caravans."

At the end of class, Ms. Haddad asked everyone to look at their own things. Sophia's pencil, Salma's paper, Elías's rice for lunch. "Today our Silk Road is made of ships, planes and the internet," she said. "A video can cross the world in one second, and ideas still change along the way." Athena grinned. "Like dancing camels."

Check your thinking

Camels carried the goods that made merchants rich, so a camel was a sign of wealth and trade. Families placed figures like this in tombs to show who the person was and what was important in their life. If artists made many beautiful camels, trade on the Silk Road must have been a big part of life in Tang China.

Glossary

trade route
A path that merchants follow over and over to carry goods to sell.
ruta comercial: Un camino que los comerciantes recorren una y otra vez para llevar productos que venden.
merchant
A person who buys and sells goods to make money.
comerciante (mercader): Una persona que compra y vende productos para ganar dinero.
caravan
A group of travelers and animals, like camels, that cross a long distance together.
caravana: Un grupo de viajeros y animales, como camellos, que recorren juntos una gran distancia.
oasis
A place in the desert with water, where plants can grow and travelers can rest.
oasis: Un lugar en el desierto con agua, donde crecen plantas y los viajeros pueden descansar.
goods
Things that people make or grow to sell or trade, like silk, spices or glass.
productos (mercancías): Cosas que la gente hace o cultiva para vender o intercambiar, como seda, especias o vidrio.
cultural diffusion
When ideas, beliefs, foods or inventions spread from one group of people to another.
difusión cultural: Cuando ideas, creencias, comidas o inventos pasan de un grupo de personas a otro.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Trace the spread

Choose one good and one idea from the story. For each one, explain where it started, where it traveled and how transportation helped it spread.

Sigue el recorrido. Elige un producto y una idea del cuento. Para cada uno, explica dónde empezó, a dónde viajó y cómo el transporte ayudó a que se difundiera.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Good: silk started in China and traveled west, carried by camel caravans that stopped at oases, and its price went up at each city. Idea: Buddhism started in India and traveled to China with merchants and travelers on the trade routes. Without caravans and safe stops, neither could have traveled so far.

Go further

The internet spreads ideas much faster than the Silk Road did. Give one benefit and one problem of ideas spreading so fast, and explain how people can check whether an idea changed along the way.

Para ir más lejos. Internet difunde las ideas mucho más rápido que la Ruta de la Seda. Da un beneficio y un problema de que las ideas se difundan tan rápido, y explica cómo puede la gente comprobar si una idea cambió en el camino.

Check your thinking

Benefit: people can learn about inventions, medicine or other cultures right away. Problem: false or changed information can also spread to millions of people before anyone checks it. To check, people can look for the original source, compare several reliable sources and ask who made the message and why, just as historians do.

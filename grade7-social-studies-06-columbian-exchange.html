The star that showed the way

The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

On a clear evening, Ms. Novak took the class to the school field. Israel held up a half-circle of cardboard with a straw taped along the edge and a string with a washer hanging from the center. "It's a homemade astrolabe," said Ms. Novak. "Find the North Star through the straw."

Israel aimed the straw at a steady star. Athena read where the string crossed the cardboard. "About 42 degrees." "In the Northern Hemisphere, the angle of the North Star above the horizon is about the same as your ," said Ms. Novak. "Chicago is about 42 degrees north of the equator. You just found it with cardboard and a straw."

"Sailors did this?" asked Israel. "Yes. In the 1400s, sailors from Portugal and Spain used a metal or a quadrant to measure the angle of the North Star at night or the Sun at noon," she said. "Then they knew how far north or south they were." That kind of helped them sail farther from the coast than ever before.

Back in the classroom, Ms. Novak covered a table with foods: potatoes, corn, tomatoes, chocolate, wheat and oranges. "In 1492, Christopher Columbus crossed the Atlantic and reached islands in the Caribbean," she said. "Europeans did not know these lands existed, but millions of people had lived in the Americas for thousands of years."

"After 1492, ships carried plants, animals and people back and forth across the ocean," said Ms. Novak. "Historians call it the ." She sorted the foods into two piles. Potatoes, corn, tomatoes and chocolate came from the Americas. Wheat, oranges, horses, cows and pigs came to the Americas from Europe, Africa and Asia.

Athena was surprised. "So Italian food had no tomatoes before 1492?" "And Ireland had no potatoes," said Ms. Novak. "Corn and potatoes became important foods in Europe, Africa and Asia and helped populations grow. Horses changed life for many Native nations of the Great Plains."

Then her voice became serious. "But the exchange also carried diseases like smallpox. Indigenous peoples had never faced them before, and killed millions of people." She explained that Europeans also forced Indigenous people, and later millions of Africans who were enslaved, to work on plantations of sugar and other crops. "The exchange changed daily life on both sides of the ocean," she said, "in good ways and in terrible ways."

At home that night, Israel looked at his plate: tacos with corn tortillas, beef and tomato salsa. "Corn and tomatoes from the Americas," he told his father, "and the cow from across the ocean." His father smiled. "So your dinner is a map of history."

It was heavy so it would hang straight and stay steady on a rocking ship, and the open spaces let the wind pass through so it would not swing. A navigator's instruments could decide whether a ship came home safely, so they were made with great care and were very valuable.

Glossary

latitude
How far north or south of the equator a place is, measured in degrees.
latitud: Qué tan al norte o al sur del ecuador está un lugar, medido en grados.
astrolabe
An instrument used to measure the angle of the Sun or a star above the horizon, to find latitude.
astrolabio: Un instrumento que sirve para medir el ángulo del Sol o de una estrella sobre el horizonte y así hallar la latitud.
navigation
Finding your position and planning your route, especially at sea.
navegación: Encontrar tu posición y planear tu ruta, sobre todo en el mar.
Columbian Exchange
The transfer of plants, animals, people, ideas and diseases between the Americas and Europe, Africa and Asia after 1492.
intercambio colombino: El traspaso de plantas, animales, personas, ideas y enfermedades entre América y Europa, África y Asia después de 1492.
Indigenous
Describes the first peoples who lived in a place, and their descendants.
indígena: Describe a los primeros pueblos que vivieron en un lugar y a sus descendientes.
epidemic
A disease that spreads quickly and makes many people sick at the same time.
epidemia: Una enfermedad que se propaga rápido y enferma a muchas personas al mismo tiempo.

Questions

Elige la respuesta correcta. Si te equivocas, el cuento te muestra dónde está la respuesta.

  1. AQuestion A. How did Israel and Athena find Chicago's latitude?

    ¿Cómo encontraron Israel y Athena la latitud de Chicago?

    In the Northern Hemisphere, the angle of the North Star above the horizon is about the same as your latitude.

  2. BQuestion B. How did sailors in the 1400s use an astrolabe?

    ¿Cómo usaban el astrolabio los marineros del siglo XV?

    They measured the angle of the North Star at night or the Sun at noon to know how far north or south they were.

  3. CQuestion C. What was the Columbian Exchange?

    ¿Qué fue el intercambio colombino?

    After 1492, ships carried plants, animals and people back and forth across the Atlantic: the Columbian Exchange.

  4. DQuestion D. How did foods from the Americas affect the rest of the world?

    ¿Cómo afectaron los alimentos de América al resto del mundo?

    Corn and potatoes became important foods in Europe, Africa and Asia and helped populations grow.

  5. EQuestion E. What was one terrible effect of the exchange on Indigenous peoples?

    ¿Cuál fue un efecto terrible del intercambio para los pueblos indígenas?

    Diseases like smallpox were new to Indigenous peoples, and epidemics killed millions of people.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Sort the effects

Make a chart with two columns: from the Americas to the rest of the world, and from the rest of the world to the Americas. Write at least three things in each column. Then circle one item that brought benefits and one that caused great harm, and explain why.

Clasifica los efectos. Haz una tabla de dos columnas: de América al resto del mundo, y del resto del mundo a América. Escribe por lo menos tres cosas en cada columna. Después encierra en un círculo una cosa que trajo beneficios y otra que causó un gran daño, y explica por qué.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

From the Americas: corn, potatoes, tomatoes, chocolate (cacao), chili peppers. To the Americas: wheat, oranges, horses, cows, pigs, sugarcane, and diseases like smallpox. Benefit: potatoes fed many people in Europe and helped populations grow. Harm: smallpox caused epidemics that killed millions of Indigenous people.

Go further

Look at a meal you ate this week. Which ingredients came from the Americas and which came from other continents? What does your meal show about how the world is connected?

Para ir más lejos. Mira una comida que comiste esta semana. ¿Qué ingredientes vienen de América y cuáles de otros continentes? ¿Qué muestra tu comida sobre cómo está conectado el mundo?

Check your thinking

Example: pizza. The tomatoes come from the Americas; the wheat for the dough and the cheese from cows came from Europe and Asia. A single meal can mix ingredients from several continents, because of exchanges that began more than 500 years ago.

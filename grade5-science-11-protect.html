The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

On a Saturday in spring, the whole class joined a neighborhood cleanup at a small river in the park. Trash that falls into a river does not stay there: the water carries it downstream into bigger waters. Neighbors in gloves filled bags with bottles, wrappers and bottle caps.

"Why does this matter so much?" asked Israel, holding up a soggy chip bag. "Because clean water is a that everyone needs," said Ms. Kowalski. "Chicago gets its drinking water from Lake Michigan, and fish, birds and plants need clean water too."

Selena found a tiny piece of plastic on the shore. "Plastic doesn't rot like a banana peel," the teacher said. "Decomposers can't break it down. It breaks into smaller and smaller pieces, and that can harm fish and birds that swallow it."

"So what can we do?" asked Sophia. "Communities use science ideas to their resources," said Ms. Kowalski. "Some cities put out more recycling bins. Some ban plastic bags. And many people volunteer to clean rivers and beaches, like we're doing today."

Back at school on Monday, the teacher gave them an engineering challenge. "Design a trash catcher that stops floating trash in a stream." She wrote the on the board: it must catch floating trash, and it must let water and fish pass through. Then she wrote the : you can use only materials from the recycling bin, and you have one week.

Selena, Israel and Sophia built their first : a plastic crate with a piece of window screen tied across it. They tested it in a long tray of water with floating bottle caps. The crate caught some of the caps, but the water pushed the whole thing over.

"It failed," said Israel, disappointed. "No," said Sophia, writing in her notebook. "It taught us something." They tied two heavy rocks to the bottom of the crate and tested it again. This time it stayed in place and caught almost every cap, and the water flowed right through the screen.

On Friday, the class tested every team's design and compared the results. Some caught more trash, some were stronger, and some were easier to empty. "Engineers test, learn and improve," said Ms. Kowalski. "That's how they solve real problems for their communities."

Before going home, Israel looked at the tray full of rescued bottle caps. "We should take this to the river cleanup next year," he said. "And the fish can send us a thank-you note." "Fish can't write," said Selena. "Then they can send us a thank-you bubble," said Israel.

Glossary

natural resource
Something from nature that people use, like water, air, soil or trees.
recurso natural: Algo de la naturaleza que usan las personas, como el agua, el aire, la tierra o los árboles.
pollution
Harmful things, like trash or chemicals, that make air, water or land dirty.
contaminación: Cosas dañinas, como basura o sustancias químicas, que ensucian el aire, el agua o la tierra.
protect
To keep something safe from harm.
proteger: Mantener algo a salvo de daños.
criteria
What a design must do to be successful.
criterios: Lo que un diseño debe lograr para tener éxito.
constraint
A limit on a design, like the materials, the money or the time you have.
restricción: Un límite para un diseño, como los materiales, el dinero o el tiempo que tienes.
prototype
A first model of a design that you build to test it.
prototipo: Un primer modelo de un diseño que se construye para probarlo.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Design a solution

Your school throws away a lot of plastic water bottles at lunch. Design a plan to solve this problem. Write one criterion and one constraint, and explain how you would test whether your plan works.

Diseña una solución. En tu escuela se tiran muchas botellas de agua de plástico a la hora del almuerzo. Diseña un plan para resolver este problema. Escribe un criterio y una restricción, y explica cómo probarías si tu plan funciona.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Example: put refill stations and recycling bins in the cafeteria. Criterion: the school should throw away fewer bottles. Constraint: the plan must cost very little. Test: count the bottles in the trash for one week before the plan and one week after, and compare the numbers.

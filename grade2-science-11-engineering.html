The story

In Ms. Ríos's classroom, there was a problem. Every time someone moved the table, the pencils rolled and fell on the floor. Israel spent all day picking up pencils.

"How can we solve this?" the teacher asked. "Today we are going to think like engineers." is using what we know to invent or improve objects that solve problems.

First, each child made a , which is a simple drawing of an idea. Lucía drew a paper cup taped to the table. Israel drew a cardboard box with holes, one for each pencil.

Then they built their two . A design is the plan for what an object will be like and how it will work. They used cups, cardboard, scissors, and tape.

It was time for the . They put ten pencils in each design and shook the table hard. Four pencils fell out of Lucía's cup, because the cup tipped over. None fell out of Israel's box: each pencil was held tight in its hole.

They wrote the in a table. "The box works better," said Lucía. "But it is hard to take the pencils out quickly."

"Then we can the design," said Israel. "Let's make the holes a little bigger." They did, and they tested it again. Now the pencils did not fall out, and they were easy to take out.

The teacher put the new box in the middle of the table. "This is how engineers work," she said. "They see a problem, sketch ideas, test them, compare, and improve." From that day on, no pencil ever ran away again.

The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Elías had been training for a month for the 5K fun run along the lakefront. When the race started, Daniel, Salma and Athena stood by the path with a notebook. Ms. Rivera had given them a mission: "Watch Elías's body like scientists. Find evidence that the body works as a team."

By the first mile, Elías was breathing hard. "Look at his chest," said Salma. "His is working faster. His lungs are pulling in more air to get more oxygen, and breathing out more carbon dioxide."

Athena put two fingers on her own wrist. "When I run, I can feel my heart beat faster." "That's his ," said Daniel. "His heart pumps faster to carry oxygen from his lungs to his leg muscles. The two systems work together. One gets the oxygen in, and the other delivers it."

"But why do his muscles need so much oxygen?" asked Athena. Daniel opened his science notebook. "Muscle cells use oxygen and food to release the energy that makes them move. Lots of muscle cells together make muscle . Different tissues make an , like the heart, and organs that work together make an ."

At the second mile, Elías saw a puddle and jumped over it without slowing down. "That was his ," said Salma. "His eyes saw the puddle, his brain sent a message through his nerves, and his muscles jumped. All in less than a second."

When Elías finally crossed the finish line, his face was red and sweat was dripping down his forehead. "Even the sweat is part of the team," said Athena. "His muscles made a lot of heat, and sweating helps cool his body down."

Ms. Rivera met them at the finish line with a bottle of water. "So, scientists, what's your argument?" Daniel read from the notebook. "Our claim is that the body is a team of systems. Our evidence: when Elías ran, his breathing got faster, his heart beat faster, his brain controlled his jump and his skin sweated. No system worked alone."

Elías drank the whole bottle in one go. "That's a great argument," he panted. "And here's more evidence: my digestive system says it's time for tacos."

Glossary

tissue
A group of similar cells that work together, like muscle tissue.
tejido: Un grupo de células parecidas que trabajan juntas, como el tejido muscular.
organ
A body part made of different tissues that does a special job, like the heart or the lungs.
órgano: Una parte del cuerpo formada por distintos tejidos que hace un trabajo especial, como el corazón o los pulmones.
organ system
A group of organs that work together, like the circulatory system or the respiratory system.
sistema de órganos: Un grupo de órganos que trabajan juntos, como el sistema circulatorio o el respiratorio.
respiratory system
The lungs and airways, which take in oxygen and get rid of carbon dioxide.
sistema respiratorio: Los pulmones y las vías respiratorias, que toman oxígeno y eliminan el dióxido de carbono.
circulatory system
The heart, blood and blood vessels, which carry oxygen and food to all the cells.
sistema circulatorio: El corazón, la sangre y los vasos sanguíneos, que llevan oxígeno y alimento a todas las células.
nervous system
The brain, spinal cord and nerves, which send messages that control the body.
sistema nervioso: El cerebro, la médula espinal y los nervios, que envían mensajes que controlan el cuerpo.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Make an argument

Choose an everyday activity, like eating lunch or reading a book. Write a claim about which body systems work together during it, and give at least two pieces of evidence.

Construye un argumento. Elige una actividad de todos los días, como almorzar o leer un libro. Escribe una afirmación sobre qué sistemas del cuerpo trabajan juntos durante esa actividad y da por lo menos dos evidencias.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Example: when you eat lunch, the digestive system breaks food into nutrients, the circulatory system carries those nutrients to every cell, and the nervous system tells you when you are hungry or full. Evidence: your stomach growls before lunch, and you feel more energy after you eat. The body works as a team of systems made of cells.

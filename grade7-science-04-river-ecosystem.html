The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

The class met on the bank of the Chicago River, where a scientist in tall rubber waders was pulling a net through the water. "We count fish here every year," she said. "It tells us how healthy the river is." She held up a big green-and-white fish. "Largemouth bass!"

Israel remembered the story of the river that ran backward in 1900. "After they reversed it, was the river clean?" he asked. "Not for a long time," said the scientist. "For many years the river was so dirty that very few could live in it. By the 1970s, fewer than ten kinds of fish were found here."

"So what changed?" asked Salma. "People," she said. "New laws and better treatment plants cleaned up the water, and giant tunnels under the city now store dirty storm water so it doesn't pour into the river." "As the water got cleaner, fish came back. Today, more than 70 species of fish have been found in the river."

Mr. Mensah drew a graph in the air with his finger, low on the left and high on the right. "When one part of an ecosystem changes, like the water getting cleaner, the of living things change, too," he said. "More oxygen in the water and a better meant more fish, and more fish meant more birds and turtles that eat them." "So went up," said Israel, writing it on his clipboard.

"But there's another side to the story," said the scientist. "The canal that reversed the river connected it to the Mississippi River system. That opened a door for : living things from other places that can spread and harm the ones that were here first."

She told them about invasive carp, large fish from Asia that have spread up the Mississippi and the Illinois rivers. They eat huge amounts of plankton, the tiny food that young native fish need. "They are not in the Chicago River system," she said, "and we're working hard to keep it that way." "In the canal near Romeoville, there are electric barriers in the water that send out a pulse to scare fish away, so the carp can't swim through to the Great Lakes."

"And some invaders already got in," Mr. Mensah added. "Zebra mussels came to the Great Lakes in the water of ships' tanks in the 1980s. They filter so much plankton from the water that they changed the whole food web of Lake Michigan." Salma looked into the bucket of little fish. "So the river can get better and worse at the same time."

Before they left, the scientist let each student release one small fish back into the river. Israel watched his swim away into the green water. "In 1900, Chicago changed the river for people," he said. "Now people are changing it back for the fish." "And the fish are noticing," said the scientist, smiling.

Glossary

ecosystem
All the living things in an area and the nonliving things around them, like water, soil and air, working together.
ecosistema: Todos los seres vivos de un lugar y lo no vivo que los rodea, como el agua, la tierra y el aire, funcionando juntos.
species
A group of living things of the same kind that can have young together.
especie: Un grupo de seres vivos del mismo tipo que pueden tener crías entre sí.
population
All the members of one species that live in the same area.
población: Todos los miembros de una especie que viven en el mismo lugar.
habitat
The place where a living thing finds the food, water and shelter it needs.
hábitat: El lugar donde un ser vivo encuentra el alimento, el agua y el refugio que necesita.
biodiversity
The variety of different kinds of living things in a place.
biodiversidad: La variedad de tipos distintos de seres vivos en un lugar.
invasive species
A species brought from another place that spreads quickly and harms the living things that were already there.
especie invasora: Una especie traída de otro lugar que se extiende rápido y daña a los seres vivos que ya estaban allí.

Check your thinking

The carp eat huge amounts of plankton, so the plankton population would go down. Young native fish need plankton for food, so fewer of them would survive. With fewer native fish, birds that eat fish would have less food, and their populations could go down too. A change in one part of an ecosystem spreads through the food web.

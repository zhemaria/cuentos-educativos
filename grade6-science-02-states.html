The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

On the hottest day of July, Selena and Elías bought mango ice pops at the paletería on 18th Street. "Race you," said Elías. "The one who finishes first without a drop on the sidewalk wins." Sophia, who was in a summer science camp, sat on the bench with a thermometer and a cup of ice water. "I'll be the judge," she said.

The ice pops started dripping almost right away. "Why does it melt so fast?" asked Selena, licking her wrist. "Because the hot air is giving it ," said Sophia. "Inside the ice pop, the were packed together and only shaking in place. When they gain energy, they move faster and start sliding past each other."

"So the solid turns into a liquid," said Elías, catching a drip with his tongue. "Exactly. That's called . The ice pop doesn't disappear. It just changes its , from solid to liquid."

Sophia pointed at her cup. The ice cubes were floating in water, and the thermometer said 0 degrees Celsius. "Here's the weird part," she said. "While ice is melting, the stays the same. The energy goes into breaking the ice apart, not into making it warmer. Only when all the ice has melted does the water start to warm up."

Selena's grandmother came out of the shop carrying a pot of water for the elotes. She set it on a little camping stove, and soon bubbles rose from the bottom. "Now the water is so hot that bubbles of gas form all through the liquid, not just at the top," said Sophia. "That's . Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius at sea level."

"Can it go the other way?" asked Elías. Sophia nodded and held the cold cup against the steam. Tiny drops of water appeared on the outside of the cup. "When particles lose thermal energy, they slow down and come closer together. A gas can turn back into a liquid, and a liquid can freeze into a solid."

"So heat speeds the particles up, and cold slows them down," said Selena. "Cold just means less thermal energy," said Sophia. "Remove enough energy, and the particles almost stop wiggling."

By then, Elías had mango all the way to his elbows, and Selena had a sticky stick and a clean sidewalk. "Who wins?" they asked. Sophia checked the sidewalk carefully. "Nobody," she said. "Thermal energy wins."

Glossary

particle
A tiny piece of matter, too small to see. Particles are always moving.
partícula: Un trocito de materia, demasiado pequeño para verlo. Las partículas siempre se están moviendo.
thermal energy
The energy of moving particles. The faster the particles move, the more thermal energy there is.
energía térmica: La energía de las partículas en movimiento. Cuanto más rápido se mueven, más energía térmica hay.
temperature
A measure of how fast the particles of a substance are moving, on average.
temperatura: Una medida de qué tan rápido se mueven, en promedio, las partículas de una sustancia.
melting
When a solid gains enough thermal energy to become a liquid.
fusión (derretirse): Cuando un sólido gana suficiente energía térmica para convertirse en líquido.
boiling
When a liquid gains enough thermal energy that it turns into a gas, with bubbles all through it.
ebullición (hervir): Cuando un líquido gana tanta energía térmica que se convierte en gas, con burbujas por todas partes.
state of matter
The form a substance takes: solid, liquid or gas.
estado de la materia: La forma que tiene una sustancia: sólida, líquida o gaseosa.

Questions

Choose the correct answer. If you make a mistake, the story shows you where the answer is.

Elige la respuesta correcta. Si te equivocas, el cuento te muestra dónde está la respuesta.

  1. AQuestion A. Why did the ice pops melt so fast?

    ¿Por qué las paletas se derritieron tan rápido?

    The hot air gave the ice pops thermal energy, so their particles moved faster and started sliding past each other.

  2. BQuestion B. What happens to an ice pop when it melts?

    ¿Qué le pasa a una paleta cuando se derrite?

    Melting changes the state of matter from solid to liquid; the ice pop doesn't disappear.

  3. CQuestion C. What did Sophia notice about the temperature of the melting ice?

    ¿Qué notó Sophia sobre la temperatura del hielo que se derretía?

    While ice melts, the energy breaks the ice apart, so the temperature stays at 0 degrees Celsius until all of it has melted.

  4. DQuestion D. What happens to water particles when water boils?

    ¿Qué les pasa a las partículas del agua cuando el agua hierve?

    When water boils, the particles move so fast that some escape into the air as a gas.

  5. EQuestion E. What happens when particles lose thermal energy?

    ¿Qué pasa cuando las partículas pierden energía térmica?

    Particles that lose thermal energy slow down and come closer together, so a gas can become a liquid and a liquid can freeze.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Explain with a model

Draw three boxes: ice, liquid water and steam. In each box, draw particles as dots and use arrows to show how fast they move. Then explain what you must add or remove to go from one box to the next.

Explica con un modelo. Dibuja tres recuadros: hielo, agua líquida y vapor. En cada uno, dibuja las partículas como puntos y usa flechas para mostrar qué tan rápido se mueven. Después explica qué hay que agregar o quitar para pasar de un recuadro al siguiente.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Ice: dots packed tightly in rows, shaking in place (tiny arrows). Liquid water: dots close together but sliding around (medium arrows). Steam: dots far apart, moving fast in all directions (long arrows). To go from ice to water to steam you add thermal energy; to go back you remove it.

The story

Elías's grandma doesn't hear very well. When somebody knocks on the door, she just keeps watching her favorite TV show. Last Saturday, Aunt Rosa knocked and knocked for ten whole minutes and ended up standing in the hallway holding a cake.

"We need an invention," Elías announced on Monday. Daniel and Athena were in right away. First they wrote down the problem: Grandma can't hear the door. Next they listed the : the invention had to let her know with a light she could see from her armchair. Then the : they could only use the materials in the science box, and they couldn't spend any money.

The box held a , some wires, a small light bulb, aluminum foil and cardboard. Athena sketched the design in her notebook: a cardboard doorbell by the front door and, next to the armchair, the light bulb.

"Electricity needs a complete path to travel," Daniel reminded them. "A is like a racetrack. The current leaves the battery, goes through the wires and the bulb, and comes back to the battery."

Time for the first test. Elías pressed the doorbell... and nothing happened. From her armchair, Grandma said, "Very nice, mijo. Is it working yet?" They checked everything carefully. One wire had come loose. It was an : the path had a gap, so the current couldn't get through.

They fixed the wire, but now the bulb stayed on all the time, even when nobody touched anything. Athena had an idea and drew it: two little pieces of aluminum foil inside the cardboard doorbell, held apart by a sponge.

That made a . When you press the doorbell, the foil pieces touch and the circuit closes. When you let go, they spring apart and the circuit opens. They tested it again. Elías pressed, and the bulb lit up next to the armchair. Grandma clapped like she'd just won the lottery.

"And where does the light come from?" she asked. "The battery stores energy," Athena explained. "The current carries it to the bulb, and the bulb it into light. Plus a tiny bit of heat!"

On Sunday, Aunt Rosa came back with another cake. She pressed the cardboard doorbell, the bulb glowed, and Grandma opened the door in a flash. "Finally!" said Aunt Rosa. "This cake was getting tired of waiting."

Glossary

circuit
The complete loop that an electric current travels along: from the battery, through the wires and the device, and back to the battery.
battery
An object that stores energy and gives it out as electricity.
open circuit
A circuit with a gap in it. The current can't get through.
switch
A part that opens or closes a circuit to turn a device on or off.
convert
To change something from one form into another. A light bulb converts electrical energy into light and heat.
criteria
The things a good design must do to solve the problem.
constraints
The limits on a design, like the materials, money or time you have.

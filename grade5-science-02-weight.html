The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Ms. Kowalski set three things on the lab table: a zip-top plastic bag, a small cup of baking soda and a bottle of vinegar. "Today, two are going to meet," she said. "And we're going to weigh what happens."

Daniel put the bag, the cup of baking soda and the vinegar on the digital scale together. Salma wrote down the number in her notebook. Then Daniel poured the baking soda into the bag, and Israel added the vinegar. Daniel zipped the bag shut, fast.

Fssssss! The started to fizz and bubble like a soda that someone had shaken. The bag began to puff up like a pillow. "It's alive!" Israel shouted, jumping back.

"It's not alive," Ms. Kowalski said, "but something new is happening. The baking soda and the vinegar are reacting, and they are making a : a called carbon dioxide." That gas is what's filling up the bag.

Salma touched the bag. "It feels cold," she said. "And the bubbles weren't there before." "Exactly," said the teacher. "When two substances mix and you see bubbles, a change in temperature or a new color, it can be a sign that a new substance formed."

Israel made a prediction. "Gas is light," he said. "So now the bag will weigh less." Daniel put the puffy bag back on the scale, next to the empty cup and the empty bottle. Everyone held their breath.

The scale showed exactly the same number as before. "No way," said Israel. Salma checked her notebook twice. "The same," she said. "Not one gram more, and not one gram less."

"In a sealed bag, nothing can get in and nothing can get out," Ms. Kowalski explained. "The particles just changed partners and formed new substances. So the total is : it stays the same." Then she opened the bag. Pfff! The gas rushed out.

Daniel weighed the open bag again. This time the number was a little smaller, because the gas had escaped into the air of the classroom. Israel wrinkled his nose. "So now we're breathing the experiment?" "A tiny bit," said Ms. Kowalski. "Don't worry. Carbon dioxide is also in the air you breathe out." Israel took a giant breath, just to be sure.

Glossary

substance
A kind of matter with its own properties, like water, sugar or salt.
sustancia: Un tipo de materia con sus propias propiedades, como el agua, el azúcar o la sal.
mixture
Two or more substances put together. Sometimes they just mix, and sometimes they form a new substance.
mezcla: Dos o más sustancias juntas. A veces solo se mezclan, y a veces forman una sustancia nueva.
new substance
A substance that was not there before, made when two substances react. It has different properties.
sustancia nueva: Una sustancia que no estaba antes y que se forma cuando dos sustancias reaccionan. Tiene propiedades distintas.
gas
Matter that has no shape of its own and spreads out to fill any space.
gas: Materia que no tiene forma propia y se esparce hasta llenar todo el espacio.
weight
How heavy something is. We measure it with a scale.
peso: Cuánto pesa algo. Se mide con una báscula.
conserved
Kept the same. In a sealed bag, the total weight is conserved, even when substances change.
se conserva: Se mantiene igual. En una bolsa cerrada, el peso total se conserva aunque las sustancias cambien.

Questions

  1. AQuestion A. What filled up the bag and made it puff up?

    ¿Qué llenó la bolsa y la hizo inflarse?

    The baking soda and the vinegar reacted and made a new substance, a gas called carbon dioxide.

  2. BQuestion B. What can be a sign that a new substance formed?

    ¿Qué puede ser una señal de que se formó una sustancia nueva?

    Bubbles, a change in temperature or a new color can be signs that a new substance formed.

  3. CQuestion C. What did the scale show when the sealed bag puffed up?

    ¿Qué mostró la báscula cuando la bolsa cerrada se infló?

    The scale showed exactly the same number as before.

  4. DQuestion D. Why did the weight stay the same in the sealed bag?

    ¿Por qué el peso no cambió en la bolsa cerrada?

    The particles only formed new substances, and nothing got in or out, so the total weight was conserved.

  5. EQuestion E. Why did the open bag weigh a little less?

    ¿Por qué la bolsa abierta pesó un poco menos?

    When the bag was opened, the gas escaped into the air, so the bag weighed less.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Predict and explain

You put an ice cube in a jar, close the lid and weigh it. You wait until the ice melts and weigh it again. Will the weight be more, less or the same? Explain why.

Predice y explica. Pones un cubo de hielo en un frasco, lo tapas y lo pesas. Esperas a que el hielo se derrita y lo vuelves a pesar. ¿El peso será mayor, menor o igual? Explica por qué.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

The weight will be the same. The ice changed from a solid to a liquid, but it is the same water, and the closed jar does not let anything in or out. When matter changes, the total weight is conserved.

The story

One Friday, Ms. Ríos took the class to a forest preserve near Chicago. "Today we are going to observe three ," she said. "A habitat is the place where a living thing lives and finds food, water, and shelter."

Each child had a notebook to count the they found. A living thing is something that is born, grows, and needs food, like a plant or an animal.

First, they visited the forest. There were tall trees, mushrooms on the logs, and a squirrel on a branch. Israel saw a woodpecker looking for insects in the bark. The trees gave shade and to many animals.

Next, they came to a , a small, calm lake. Lucía counted frogs, little fish, a turtle, and many plants floating on the water. "Animals that need a lot of water live here!" she said.

Last, they walked through the . There were almost no trees there, but there was tall grass and flowers. They saw butterflies, bees, grasshoppers, and a rabbit that ran off to hide.

Back on the bus, the children compared their notebooks. In each habitat, they had found different living things. "The pond had more water animals," Israel said. "And the meadow had more insects."

"That is called ," the teacher explained. "It is the variety of living things in a place." Each habitat has its own diversity, because it offers different things.

That night, Lucía told Grandpa all about it. "Each animal lives where it finds what it needs," she said. "A frog would not do well in the dry meadow!"

Glossary

habitat
The place where a living thing lives and finds food, water, and shelter.
living thing
Something that is born, grows, eats, and can have young or seeds, like a plant or an animal.
shelter
A place where an animal is safe from cold, rain, or other animals.
pond
A small lake of calm water, where frogs, fish, and water plants live.
meadow
A wide, almost flat field covered with grass and flowers, with few trees.
diversity
The variety of different living things in a place.

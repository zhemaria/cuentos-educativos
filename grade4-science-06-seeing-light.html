Salma's dark box

A story about light, reflections and how we see things.

The story

Salma showed up at school with a giant box, the one her family's new refrigerator came in. She had covered every crack with black tape and poked two small holes in it: one to look through and one for a flashlight beam. "It's a dark box," she announced. "And there's a mystery inside."

Selena peeked through the hole. "I can't see anything. It's all black." "Of course," said Ms. Patel. "There is inside the box, and without light we can't see a thing, even with our eyes wide open."

Then Israel switched on the flashlight and pointed it into the other hole. "An apple!" Selena shouted. "There's a red apple in there!"

"The flashlight is a ," the teacher explained. "It makes its own light. But the apple doesn't glow by itself. Light from the flashlight hits the apple, the apple it, and that light bounces into your . That's how you see it."

Salma drew a on the whiteboard: a flashlight, an apple and an eye. She used arrows to show the path of the light, first from the flashlight to the apple and then from the apple to the eye. Light travels in a straight line, like a .

"So why don't eyes send light out to the apple?" Israel asked, opening his eyes really wide to see if anything came out. "Because eyes aren't flashlights," Selena laughed. "If they were, you could read under the covers without batteries."

Next, Ms. Patel handed Selena a small . Selena held it up to the window, and a little spot of light danced across the wall. "A mirror is very smooth and shiny, and it reflects almost all the light that hits it," the teacher said. "That's why we can aim the reflection wherever we want."

Salma moved the apple into a corner of the box, far from the flashlight. Now it was hard to see. "Less light reaches it, so it reflects less light to your eye," Israel explained, very seriously, just like a real scientist.

At the end, everyone wrote down the big idea of the day. To see an object, light has to reach the object, reflect off it and go into our eyes. Salma put the apple back in her backpack. "The experiment is over," she said. "Now it's time for lunch."

Glossary

reflect
When light hits something and bounces off, like a ball bouncing off a wall.
light source
Something that makes its own light, like the sun, a flashlight or a light bulb.
eye
The body part that takes in light so we can see.
ray of light
A straight path that light travels along.
mirror
A very smooth, shiny surface that reflects almost all the light that hits it.
darkness
A place where there is no light.
model
A drawing, object or idea that helps us understand how something works.

Questions

  1. AQuestion A. Why couldn't Selena see anything at first?

    There was darkness inside the box, and without light we can't see anything.

  2. BQuestion B. Why could Selena see the apple with the flashlight?

    Light hits the apple, the apple reflects it, and that light bounces into the eye.

  3. CQuestion C. What did the arrows in Salma's model show?

    The arrows showed the path of light: from the flashlight to the apple, and from the apple to the eye.

  4. DQuestion D. What is special about a mirror?

Challenge: explain your thinking

Make a model

Draw a model that shows how you can see a book in your room when the lamp is on. Use arrows for the light. Then explain why you can't see the book when the lamp is off.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Check your thinking

One arrow goes from the lamp to the book, and another goes from the book to your eye. The book does not make its own light: it reflects the lamp's light into your eyes. When the lamp is off, no light reaches the book, so no light reflects into your eyes.

The story

There was a huge pothole on Israel's street. Every time a car drove by, splash! Water sprayed everyone.

"Who fixes the streets?" asked Israel. "The city ," answered Great-Grandpa. "A government is the group of people who organize and run a place, and it provides for everyone."

"And who pays for those services?" asked Lucía. "All the grown-ups, with ," Great-Grandpa explained. "That's the money people give the government to pay for schools, firefighters, libraries, parks and streets."

Israel and Lucía decided to write a letter to the 's office. The mayor runs the city government and takes care of things close to home, like garbage, streets and parks.

At school, Mrs. Kim explained that there are more levels of government. "The runs the government of the state of Illinois, from Springfield, the capital. The runs the government of the whole country, from Washington, D.C."

"The state takes care of things like highways between cities and state parks. The country takes care of the mail, money and national parks."

Two weeks later, a city truck stopped in front of Israel's house. The workers filled the pothole with hot asphalt. Israel and Lucía ran outside to clap.

"You acted like good ," said Great-Grandpa. "You saw a problem in the community and asked for help the right way."

That afternoon, the children listed all the government services they used in just one day: school, the bus, the library, tap water and the park. There were so many!

Glossary

government
The group of people who organize and run a city, a state or a country.
public service
Work the government does for everyone, like schools, firefighters or garbage pickup.
taxes
Money that people and businesses pay to the government so it can provide services.
mayor
The person who runs the government of a city.
governor
The person who runs the government of a state.
president
The person who runs the government of a country.
citizen
A person who belongs to a community or a country, with rights and responsibilities.

The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

"Every ten years, the government counts everyone who lives in the United States," said Ms. Novak. "It's called the , and the Constitution requires it. The first one began on August 2, 1790." She held up a drawing of a man on horseback with a notebook.

"There were no census offices yet," she said. "The count was done by U.S. marshals and their assistants, who rode from farm to farm and town to town. When they finished, they had counted 3,929,214 people." "Almost four million," said Israel. "Today Chicago and its suburbs have more than that."

"Why count everyone?" asked Selena. "Because the census decides ," said Ms. Novak. "The number of seats each state gets in the House of Representatives depends on its population. That's called . More people means more seats, and more power in Congress."

"So who was counted?" asked Ms. Novak. She wrote a phrase from the Constitution on the board: three fifths of all other persons. "The 'other persons' were people," she said. "In the Three-Fifths , each enslaved person counted as three fifths of a person when deciding a state's seats."

Daniel frowned. "But enslaved people couldn't vote." "Exactly," said Ms. Novak. "They had no rights and no voice, but counting them, even as a fraction, gave more seats and more power to the states that held them in slavery. The fraction was a deal about power, not about fairness." The class was quiet.

"Was anyone left out completely?" asked Salma. "Yes," said Ms. Novak. "The Constitution said 'Indians not taxed' were not counted. Most Native people belonged to their own nations and were left out of the count."

"The Three-Fifths rule ended after the Civil War," said Ms. Novak. "The Fourteenth , ratified in 1868, said that seats would be divided by counting the whole number of persons in each state." "So the count changed when the rights changed," said Elías.

"And the census still matters," said Ms. Novak. "It decides seats in Congress and how money for schools, roads and hospitals is shared." Sophia wrote in her notebook: A count is never just numbers. Ask who counted, who was counted, and why.

Check your thinking

Most people lived near the Atlantic coast and along rivers, where the colonies had begun. Because Native nations were left out, the western land can look empty on the map, but it wasn't: many Native nations lived there. A map shows only what its makers decided to count.

Glossary

census
An official count of all the people who live in a country.
censo: Un recuento oficial de todas las personas que viven en un país.
representation
Having people in the government who speak and vote for you.
representación: Tener personas en el gobierno que hablen y voten por ti.
apportionment
Dividing the seats in the House of Representatives among the states according to their population.
distribución de escaños: El reparto de los escaños de la Cámara de Representantes entre los estados según su población.
compromise
An agreement in which each side gives up part of what it wants.
acuerdo (compromiso): Un acuerdo en el que cada parte cede algo de lo que quiere.
enslaved
Forced into slavery: treated as property and denied freedom and rights.
esclavizado: Obligado a vivir en esclavitud: tratado como propiedad y privado de libertad y de derechos.
amendment
A change or addition to the Constitution.
enmienda: Un cambio o un añadido a la Constitución.

    ¿Qué fue el Compromiso de las Tres Quintas Partes?

Check your thinking

Apportionment gives each state seats in the House according to its population. Under the Three-Fifths Compromise, enslaved people were counted as three fifths of a person, which increased the population count, and the representation, of the states that held them in slavery. But enslaved people could not vote and had no rights, so the extra seats represented the slaveholders, not them. One group gained power from the very people it denied rights to.

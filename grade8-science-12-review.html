The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

On the last science night before high school, the gym was full of families. Each table held one big idea from eighth grade, and the students were the guides. "Tonight, you're the teachers," said Mr. Mensah. "Tell the story, show the and explain the idea."

At the first table, Israel let a steel ball roll down one ramp and up another. "Galileo found that a ball would keep rolling forever if nothing stopped it," he told a group of parents. "It stops because of friction. A changes motion; nothing stops on its own." Next to him, Athena sent a balloon rocket flying across the gym. "And every push comes back," she said. "That's how rockets fly in space."

Daniel lowered a paper crown into a bowl of water on a balance, and its side rose. "Density tells the truth about what something is made of," he said. At the next table, Selena held a prism up to a lamp, and a rainbow appeared on the wall. "White light is all the colors together," she said. "A red apple reflects red light and absorbs the rest."

Elías tapped a message in Morse code on a brass key, and a grandmother decoded it on a card: H-I. "Dots and dashes are digital," he said. "Even a weak signal can be read perfectly." Beside him, Sophia walked a small ball around a globe in front of a lamp. "When the Moon passes exactly between the Sun and the Earth, its shadow makes a total eclipse," she said. "Carbondale saw it twice in seven years."

Salma had the long paper strip from the hallway rolled up on her table, with a peppercorn on a card next to it. "The Earth is 4.6 billion years old," she told a little boy. "We're the last few millimeters of the roll. And the solar system is mostly empty space." At the last table, paper moths covered two pictures of bark. "When the trees changed, the moths changed," said Israel's cousin, who had come to help.

When the families had left, Mr. Mensah gathered the class in the middle of the gym. "One last question," he said. "What do all these stories have in common?" "They all found a ," said Athena. "The phases of the Moon, the rock layers, the moths going up and down on the graph."

"And they all built a to explain it," said Selena. "The ramps, the lamp and the globe, the hallway solar system, the paper moths. Then they tested a with evidence." "And a lot of them weren't believed at first," said Daniel. "Goddard, Hutton, even Newton."

Mr. Mensah smiled. "Next year, in high school, you'll be the people don't believe at first. Keep your evidence ready." Sophia closed her notebook, full from cover to cover. On the last page, she wrote: Ask. Measure. Model. Explain. Then ask again.

Glossary

evidence
Observations, measurements and data that support a claim.
evidencia: Observaciones, mediciones y datos que apoyan una afirmación.
claim
A statement that answers a question and that evidence can support.
afirmación: Un enunciado que responde a una pregunta y que la evidencia puede apoyar.
force
A push or a pull on an object.
fuerza: Un empujón o un tirón sobre un objeto.
pattern
Something that repeats in a way you can predict, like the phases of the Moon.
patrón: Algo que se repite de una manera que se puede predecir, como las fases de la Luna.
model
A drawing, object or idea that represents something too big, too small or too far away to study directly.
modelo: Un dibujo, un objeto o una idea que representa algo demasiado grande, pequeño o lejano para estudiarlo directamente.
scientist
A person who asks questions about the world and answers them with evidence.
científico: Una persona que hace preguntas sobre el mundo y las responde con evidencia.

  1. AQuestion A. According to Israel, why does a rolling ball stop?

    Según Israel, ¿por qué se detiene una bola que rueda?

    A force changes motion. The ball stops because friction slows it down; nothing stops on its own.

  2. BQuestion B. Why does a red apple look red?

    ¿Por qué una manzana roja se ve roja?

    White light has all the colors. The apple reflects the red and absorbs the rest.

  3. CQuestion C. What causes a total solar eclipse?

    ¿Qué causa un eclipse total de Sol?

    When the Moon is exactly between the Sun and the Earth, its shadow makes a total eclipse.

  4. DQuestion D. What did Salma's paper strip show?

    ¿Qué mostraba la tira de papel de Salma?

    Earth is 4.6 billion years old, and humans are only the last few millimeters of the roll.

  5. EQuestion E. According to Selena, what did all the discoveries do?

    Según Selena, ¿qué hicieron todos los descubrimientos?

    They found patterns, built models to explain them and tested claims with evidence.

Check your thinking

Example: the eclipse story and the peppered moth story both use a model to explain a pattern. The lamp, globe and ball show why eclipses and moon phases repeat, and the paper moths on two kinds of bark show why the percentage of dark moths went up and then down. In both, scientists used the model to make predictions and then compared them with real data. Another example: Cavendish and Leavitt both measured something too big to reach, the mass of the Earth and the distance to other galaxies, by measuring something small very carefully.

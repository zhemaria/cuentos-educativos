The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Ms. Haddad held up a picture of an eagle standing on a cactus. "Have you seen this before?" "It's on the flag of Mexico!" said Selena. "Right," said Ms. Haddad. "The Mexica, sometimes called the Aztecs, said that their god told them to build a city where they saw an eagle on a cactus. They said they saw it on a small, swampy island in Lake Texcoco, and around 1325, they began building Tenochtitlan there."

Israel frowned. "Why build a city in a lake? Seems like a bad idea." "It had problems," said Ms. Haddad, "but also advantages. The water protected them from enemies, and the lake was full of fish, birds and plants." "The Mexica didn't move somewhere easier. They chose to to the lake, and to change it."

There wasn't enough land to grow food, so the Mexica made more. "They built ," said Ms. Haddad. "Farmers marked out rectangles in the shallow water, piled up layers of mud and plants, and planted trees along the edges so their roots would hold everything in place." The chinampas were so rich that farmers could grow corn, beans, squash and flowers several times a year. Some chinampas are still farmed in Xochimilco, in Mexico City, today.

Israel and Selena built a clay model on a tray of water. "How did people get to the shore?" asked Selena. "On ," said Ms. Haddad, "long raised roads across the water, with bridges that could be lifted if enemies came." And because the lake water was salty in many places, the Mexica built an that carried fresh water from springs at Chapultepec, a hill on the shore.

By 1519, when Spanish soldiers arrived, Tenochtitlan had about 200,000 people, more than almost any city in Europe at the time. The Spanish conquered the city in 1521 and built Mexico City on top of it. Over the next few hundred years, people drained most of the lake to stop the floods.

Then Ms. Haddad showed a photo of an old church in Mexico City, leaning to one side. "Today, parts of Mexico City sink up to 20 inches a year." Daniel, at the next table, said, "That's because the city got so big and heavy." Ms. Haddad smiled. "Good thinking, but careful. The city grew bigger, and the city sank more. Those two things happened together. That's a . It doesn't prove one caused the other."

"So what's the real ?" asked Selena. "The city is built on the soft clay of the old lake," said Ms. Haddad. "For many years, the city has pumped water out from under the ground to drink. As the water is pumped out, the clay dries and squeezes together, like a sponge, and the ground sinks." "So more people did matter," said Daniel, "because more people needed more water." "Yes! That's a cause with more than one step."

Israel looked at their model city floating on the tray. "The Mexica adapted to the lake, and then the lake was taken away," he said. "And now the lake's old clay is still changing the city." Selena put one last tiny canoe in the water. "Seven hundred years later," she said, "the lake is still in charge."

Check your thinking

The eagle on the cactus is the sign of the founding, so the artists put it in the center to show it was the heart of the story and of the city. The water lines remind us that the city was built on a lake. Because the book was made for Spanish readers, the artists may have chosen to explain Mexica history clearly and proudly. Historians always ask who made a source and for whom.

Glossary

adapt
To change the way you live so you can survive in a place.
adaptarse: Cambiar la forma de vivir para poder sobrevivir en un lugar.
chinampa
A rectangle of farmland built in shallow lake water with layers of mud and plants.
chinampa: Un rectángulo de tierra de cultivo construido en el agua poco profunda de un lago, con capas de lodo y plantas.
causeway
A raised road built across water or wet land.
calzada: Un camino elevado construido sobre el agua o sobre tierra mojada.
aqueduct
A channel or pipe that carries fresh water from far away to a city.
acueducto: Un canal o tubo que lleva agua dulce desde lejos hasta una ciudad.
cause
Something that makes another thing happen.
causa: Algo que hace que otra cosa pase.
correlation
When two things happen at the same time or change together, even if one does not cause the other.
correlación: Cuando dos cosas pasan al mismo tiempo o cambian juntas, aunque una no cause la otra.

  1. AQuestion A. Why did the Mexica build Tenochtitlan on an island?

    ¿Por qué los mexicas construyeron Tenochtitlan en una isla?

    The Mexica said that their god told them to build a city where they saw an eagle on a cactus, and they saw it on an island in Lake Texcoco.

  2. BQuestion B. What did the Mexica do instead of moving somewhere easier?

    ¿Qué hicieron los mexicas en lugar de ir a un lugar más fácil?

    The Mexica chose to adapt to the lake and to change it.

  3. CQuestion C. How did farmers build chinampas?

    ¿Cómo construían las chinampas los campesinos?

    Farmers marked rectangles in the shallow water, piled up mud and plants, and planted trees along the edges.

  4. DQuestion D. Why did Ms. Haddad say Daniel's idea showed only a correlation?

    ¿Por qué la maestra dijo que la idea de Daniel solo mostraba una correlación?

    The city grew and the city sank at the same time, but two things happening together doesn't prove one caused the other.

  5. EQuestion E. What is the real cause of Mexico City sinking?

    ¿Cuál es la causa real de que la Ciudad de México se hunda?

    The city pumps water from under the ground, and the soft clay of the old lake dries and squeezes together, so the ground sinks.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Cause or correlation?

Explain the difference between a cause and a correlation, using Mexico City as an example. Then make up your own example of two things that happen together without one causing the other.

¿Causa o correlación?. Explica la diferencia entre una causa y una correlación, usando como ejemplo la Ciudad de México. Después inventa tu propio ejemplo de dos cosas que pasan juntas sin que una cause la otra.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

A correlation: the city grew and the city sank at the same time. The cause: pumping water from under the ground dries the clay, so the ground sinks. Your own example could be: "In summer, people eat more ice cream and more people go swimming." Ice cream does not cause swimming; hot weather causes both.

Go further

The Mexica changed the lake by building chinampas, and the Spanish changed it by draining it. Compare the two changes: what was the goal of each one, and what were the effects, short-term and long-term?

Para ir más lejos. Los mexicas cambiaron el lago construyendo chinampas, y los españoles lo cambiaron secándolo. Compara los dos cambios: ¿cuál era el objetivo de cada uno y cuáles fueron sus efectos a corto y a largo plazo?

Check your thinking

Chinampas: the goal was to make farmland; the short-term effect was lots of food, and the long-term effect is that some are still farmed today. Draining: the goal was to stop floods; the short-term effect was more dry land for the city, but the long-term effect is that the city sits on soft clay that sinks when water is pumped out. Both groups changed the environment, but one change worked with the lake and the other worked against it.

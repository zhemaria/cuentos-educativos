Mr. Mensah showed a photo on the screen: a black circle in a dark sky, surrounded by a white, glowing ring. "This was taken in Carbondale, Illinois, on April 8, 2024, at about two in the afternoon," he said. "Seven years earlier, on August 21, 2017, the same town also saw a total solar . That's why people call it the Eclipse Crossroads of America."

"My cousin was there in 2024," said Salma. "She said it got cold, the birds stopped singing and the streetlights turned on." "In 2024, Carbondale had about four minutes of ," said Mr. Mensah, "the minutes when the Moon covers the whole Sun and only its outer glow can be seen."

He closed the blinds and turned on a bright lamp. "This is the Sun. Selena, you are holding the Earth." He gave Elías a small white ball on a stick. "And this is the Moon. Let's make a ." Elías walked the Moon slowly around Selena's globe, in its .

When the Moon was on the far side of the Earth, the lamp lit up its whole face: a full moon. When it was between the Earth and the lamp, the side facing the Earth was dark: a new moon. "So the Moon doesn't change shape," said Israel. "We just see different amounts of its lit half." "Exactly," said Mr. Mensah. "Those are the , and the whole cycle takes about a month."

Then Elías held the Moon exactly between the lamp and the globe. A tiny dark shadow fell on the globe. "Only the people inside that small shadow see a total eclipse," said Mr. Mensah. "That's why the path of totality is narrow, and why most places wait hundreds of years between total eclipses."

"Then why isn't there an eclipse every new moon?" asked Daniel. "Because the Moon's orbit is tilted a little," said Mr. Mensah. He tilted Elías's stick. "Most months, the Moon passes a little above or below the line between the Sun and the Earth, and its shadow misses us."

Before the bell, Mr. Mensah tilted the globe. "One more pattern. The Earth's is tilted." He walked Selena around the lamp. When the North Pole leaned toward the lamp, the light hit Illinois more directly, and it was summer. Half a year later, Illinois leaned away, the light spread out, and it was winter. "So seasons aren't about being closer to the Sun," said Athena. "They're about the tilt."

"When's the next one in Illinois?" asked Sophia. "Not for a long time," said Mr. Mensah, "so you'd better understand the model." Sophia wrote in her notebook: The Sun, the Earth and the Moon keep dancing. If you know the steps, you can predict the shadows.

Glossary

eclipse
When one object in space blocks the light of another, as seen from Earth.
eclipse: Cuando un objeto del espacio tapa la luz de otro, visto desde la Tierra.
totality
The minutes during a total solar eclipse when the Moon covers the whole Sun.
totalidad: Los minutos de un eclipse total de Sol en los que la Luna tapa todo el Sol.
lunar phase
The shape of the lit part of the Moon that we see from Earth, like full moon or crescent.
fase lunar: La forma de la parte iluminada de la Luna que vemos desde la Tierra, como la luna llena o la creciente.
orbit
The path of one object around another, like the Moon around the Earth.
órbita: El camino de un objeto alrededor de otro, como la Luna alrededor de la Tierra.
axis
The imaginary line through the North and South Poles that the Earth spins around. It is tilted.
eje: La línea imaginaria que pasa por los polos Norte y Sur y alrededor de la cual gira la Tierra. Está inclinado.
model
A drawing, object or idea that represents something too big, too small or too far away to study directly.
modelo: Un dibujo, un objeto o una idea que representa algo demasiado grande, pequeño o lejano para estudiarlo directamente.

  1. AQuestion A. What is totality?

    ¿Qué es la totalidad?

    Totality is the time during a total solar eclipse when the Moon covers the whole Sun. In Carbondale in 2024, it lasted about four minutes.

  2. BQuestion B. In the model, when did the Moon look full?

    En el modelo, ¿cuándo se veía llena la Luna?

    On the far side of the Earth, the lamp lit up the whole face we see: a full moon.

  3. CQuestion C. Why is the path of a total eclipse so narrow?

    ¿Por qué es tan estrecho el camino de un eclipse total?

    Only the people inside the Moon's small shadow see a total eclipse.

  4. DQuestion D. Why isn't there an eclipse every month at new moon?

    ¿Por qué no hay un eclipse cada mes en la luna nueva?

    The Moon's orbit is tilted a little, so most months its shadow passes above or below the Earth.

  5. EQuestion E. What causes the seasons?

    ¿Qué causa las estaciones?

    When Illinois leans toward the Sun, the light hits it more directly: summer. When it leans away: winter.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Use a model

Draw a model of the Sun, the Earth and the Moon during a total solar eclipse, and another one during a full moon. Label the shadow. Then explain why a solar eclipse can only happen at new moon.

Usa un modelo. Dibuja un modelo del Sol, la Tierra y la Luna durante un eclipse total de Sol, y otro durante la luna llena. Rotula la sombra. Después explica por qué un eclipse de Sol solo puede ocurrir en luna nueva.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Solar eclipse: Sun, then Moon, then Earth, in a straight line; the Moon's small shadow falls on the Earth. Full moon: Sun, then Earth, then Moon; the Moon's whole face is lit. A solar eclipse needs the Moon between the Sun and the Earth, and that is exactly the position of new moon. It doesn't happen every new moon because the Moon's orbit is tilted.

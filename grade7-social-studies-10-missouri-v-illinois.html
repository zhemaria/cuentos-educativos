The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Ms. Novak held up a jar of murky water. "Remember the Chicago River that ran backward in 1900? Today, you are going to decide a famous case about it." Nine students put on black paper robes and sat in a row. "You are the of the Supreme Court," she said. Selena sat in the middle and tapped a wooden gavel.

"In January 1900, right after Chicago opened its canal, the state of Missouri filed a against the state of Illinois," said Ms. Novak. "Missouri said Chicago's sewage was flowing down the Illinois River into the Mississippi, where St. Louis got its drinking water, and that it was spreading typhoid fever."

"Why didn't they just go to a regular court?" asked Israel. "Because of ," said Ms. Novak. "In our system, power is shared between the national government and the states. When one state has a dispute with another, a court of either state might not be fair. So the gives the U.S. Supreme Court : it hears cases between states first."

Elías argued for Missouri. He held up the jar. "Before the canal, Chicago's sewage went into Lake Michigan. Now it comes down to us. Typhoid deaths in St. Louis are a serious danger. Chicago is harming our people." Sophia argued for Illinois. "Chicago needed clean drinking water too. And St. Louis and other towns on the river dump their own sewage into it. Missouri can't prove that the sickness comes from Chicago."

The justices whispered. "What did the real scientists say?" asked Selena. "Both states hired experts," said Ms. Novak. "They tested the water, studied the bacteria and counted cases of typhoid. But the experts disagreed, and the river carried sewage from many cities, not only Chicago."

"In February 1906, the Supreme Court decided in favor of Illinois," said Ms. Novak. "Justice Oliver Wendell Holmes wrote that Missouri had not proved, with clear , that Chicago's sewage was causing the harm. A state must show real proof before the Court will stop another state's project."

"So Missouri lost because of the evidence, not because it was wrong to worry," said Selena. "That's a good way to say it," said Ms. Novak. "The case shows how the Constitution helps states settle conflicts peacefully. Instead of fighting, two states argued with lawyers and experts, and nine justices decided." Later, other cases about the canal and the water of Lake Michigan went back to the Supreme Court for many years.

The class justices voted. Six of them agreed with the real Court, and three wrote a dissent saying that a state should not have to wait for people to get sick. "That's democracy too," said Ms. Novak. "Justices can disagree, and they explain their reasons in writing." Elías poured out the murky water. "I lost the case," he said, "but I won the argument about clean water."

Check your thinking

The photographs show an enormous project: tons of rock removed, huge machines and many workers over years. A lawyer for Illinois could use them to show how important and expensive the canal was for the city's health. A lawyer for Missouri could use them to show how much water the canal could send toward the Mississippi. The same source can support different arguments.

Glossary

federalism
A system in which power is shared between a national government and state governments.
federalismo: Un sistema en el que el poder se reparte entre un gobierno nacional y los gobiernos de los estados.
lawsuit
A case brought to a court by one side against another, asking the court to decide a dispute.
demanda: Un caso que una parte presenta ante un tribunal contra otra, para que el tribunal resuelva un conflicto.
original jurisdiction
The power of a court to hear a case first. The U.S. Supreme Court has it for cases between states.
jurisdicción original: El poder de un tribunal de ver un caso en primer lugar. La Corte Suprema de EE. UU. lo tiene en los casos entre estados.
Constitution
The highest law of the United States. It sets up the government and divides its powers.
Constitución: La ley más alta de Estados Unidos. Organiza el gobierno y reparte sus poderes.
evidence
Facts, data or observations that help prove whether a claim is true.
evidencia: Hechos, datos u observaciones que ayudan a demostrar si una afirmación es verdadera.
justice
A judge of the Supreme Court. There are nine justices.
magistrado: Un juez de la Corte Suprema. Hay nueve magistrados.

Check your thinking

Missouri: Claim: Chicago's canal harms our drinking water. Evidence: before the canal, the sewage went into Lake Michigan; now it flows toward the Mississippi; typhoid is a deadly disease. Answer: even if other cities pollute too, Chicago adds a huge amount. Illinois: Claim: Missouri has not proved the harm. Evidence: experts disagreed; St. Louis and other cities dump their own sewage into the river. Answer: Chicago also had a right to protect its own drinking water.

Go further

Federalism means power is shared. Give one example of something your state decides and one thing the national government decides, and explain why it makes sense for each level to decide it.

Para ir más lejos. El federalismo significa que el poder se comparte. Da un ejemplo de algo que decide tu estado y algo que decide el gobierno nacional, y explica por qué tiene sentido que lo decida cada nivel.

Check your thinking

State: the rules for getting a driver's license or the school calendar, because conditions are different in each state. National: printing money or relations with other countries, because the whole country needs one rule. Disputes between states go to the national courts, because neither state should judge its own case.

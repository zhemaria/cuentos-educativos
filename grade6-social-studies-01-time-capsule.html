The story

For the school's 100th birthday, the principal brought a dusty metal box to the library. "This time capsule was sealed by sixth graders in 1976," she said, "and today, sixth graders get to open it." Ms. Haddad smiled at her class. "Today, you are not students. You are ."

The lid creaked open. On top was a letter in neat cursive. Selena read it aloud: "Dear future students, today our class planted a tree for America's 200th birthday. I hope you still have recess." "This letter is a ," said Ms. Haddad. "It was written by someone who was there, at the time."

Daniel pulled out a small plastic rectangle with two holes and a brown ribbon inside. "What is this?" "A cassette tape," laughed Ms. Haddad. "People recorded music and voices on it." "It's also an , an object people used. Artifacts tell us how people lived, even when nobody wrote about them."

Next came a black-and-white photo of children standing in front of a brick school. Athena held it next to the window and gasped. "That's our school! But there's no gym, and the street has no trees." Ms. Haddad nodded. "A photo is a primary source too. It shows us what changed and what stayed the same."

At the bottom of the box was a thin booklet called The History of Our Neighborhood, written by a teacher in 1975. It told stories from the 1920s and 1930s. "This is a ," said Ms. Haddad. "For our question about the neighborhood in the 1920s, it's a secondary source. The teacher wrote it in 1975, and she explained those years by studying old newspapers and interviews."

Israel frowned. "So secondary sources are worse?" "Not worse, just different," said Ms. Haddad. "A secondary source can explain the big picture. A primary source gives you the voice of one person. But be careful: a primary source can contain mistakes or bias, too. Being there doesn't make someone always right." Then she held up two more letters from the capsule. One boy wrote that 1976 was the best year ever. A girl wrote that her family had just moved and she missed her old friends. "Same school, same year, two different ."

"So which one is true?" asked Sophia. "Both can be true," said Ms. Haddad. "That's why historians never trust just one source. They compare many pieces of , they ask who made each one and why, and then they build an explanation."

Before the bell rang, the class decided to make their own time capsule for 2076. Daniel wanted to put in his phone. "You can't," said Selena. "You need it." So he wrote a letter instead: Dear future students, we also hope you still have recess.

Glossary

historian
A person who studies the past and explains what happened, using evidence.
historiador o historiadora: Una persona que estudia el pasado y explica lo que pasó usando evidencia.
primary source
Something made by a person who was there at the time, like a letter, a photo or a diary.
fuente primaria: Algo hecho por una persona que estuvo allí en ese momento, como una carta, una foto o un diario.
secondary source
Something made later that explains or interprets the past by studying other sources, like a history book.
fuente secundaria: Algo hecho después que explica o interpreta el pasado a partir de otras fuentes, como un libro de historia.
artifact
An object made or used by people in the past, like a tool, a toy or a coin.
artefacto: Un objeto hecho o usado por personas en el pasado, como una herramienta, un juguete o una moneda.
perspective
The way one person sees an event, based on who they are and what they experienced.
perspectiva (punto de vista): La manera en que una persona ve un suceso, según quién es y lo que vivió.
evidence
Facts or objects that help prove an idea about the past is true.
evidencia: Datos u objetos que ayudan a demostrar que una idea sobre el pasado es verdadera.

Questions

Choose the correct answer. If you make a mistake, the story shows you where the answer is.

Elige la respuesta correcta. Si te equivocas, el cuento te muestra dónde está la respuesta.

  1. AQuestion A. Why is the letter from 1976 a primary source?

    ¿Por qué la carta de 1976 es una fuente primaria?

    The letter is a primary source because it was written by a student who was there, at the time.

  2. BQuestion B. What can an artifact like the cassette tape tell historians?

    ¿Qué les puede decir a los historiadores un artefacto como el casete?

    Artifacts are objects people used, and they show how people lived, even when nobody wrote about it.

  3. CQuestion C. Why is the 1975 neighborhood booklet a secondary source?

    ¿Por qué el folleto del barrio de 1975 es una fuente secundaria?

    For a question about the 1920s, the booklet is secondary: the teacher wrote it in 1975 by studying old newspapers and interviews and explaining them.

  4. DQuestion D. What did the two letters from the same year show?

    ¿Qué mostraron las dos cartas del mismo año?

    Same school, same year, but two different perspectives: one boy loved the year, and a girl missed her old friends.

  5. EQuestion E. Why do historians compare many sources?

    ¿Por qué los historiadores comparan muchas fuentes?

    Historians compare many pieces of evidence and ask who made each one and why, because one source shows only part of the story.

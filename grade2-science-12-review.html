The story

At the end of the year, the school held a science fair. Lucía and Israel decided to make a poster about everything they had learned. "There is so much to tell!" said Israel. "Where do we start?"

"Let's start with materials," said Lucía. They glued on a little piece of plastic and a piece of towel. Underneath they wrote: "Each material has its own . Plastic is waterproof, and the towel absorbs water."

Next, they drew an ice cream and a fried egg. "Melted ice cream can freeze again: it is a ," Israel explained. "But a fried egg can never become raw again."

Then it was time for plants. Lucía drew the healthy bean plant by the window and wrote that plants need light and water to grow. Next to it, she drew the hummingbird from the garden. "The hummingbird carries pollen from one flower to another: it helps to ," she remembered.

Israel drew the forest, the pond, and the meadow. "Each has different living things," he wrote. "A frog lives in the pond, not in the dry meadow!"

In the Earth section, they put the canyon, the volcano, and the hill in the garden. They wrote that is a slow change, that an eruption is a fast change, and that plant roots hold the soil. They also painted Lake Michigan, with solid ice on the shore and liquid water farther out.

Last, they glued on the pencil box. "This is ," Israel explained to the visitors. "We saw a problem, drew ideas, tested them, and improved the best one."

On the day of the fair, Grandpa and Ms. Ríos came to see their poster. "You have learned so much this year!" said the teacher. Lucía and Israel looked at each other and smiled. They were already thinking about the questions they would investigate next year.

Glossary

property
Something about a material that we can observe, like its color, its texture, or whether it lets water through.
reversible change
A change that can be undone, like ice cream that melts and then freezes again.
pollinate
To carry pollen from one flower to another flower, as bees, butterflies, and hummingbirds do.
habitat
The place where a living thing lives and finds food, water, and shelter.
erosion
When water or wind slowly carry away soil or rock from a place.
engineering
Using science and creativity to invent or improve objects that solve problems.

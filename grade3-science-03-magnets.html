Whiskers' cat door

The story

Whiskers, an orange cat, lives at Adrián's house. Whiskers has a little pet door in the kitchen door, but the wind blows it open and the cold Chicago air comes in.

"We need the little door to stay shut, but Whiskers still has to be able to push it," said Israel. Alejandro, who was visiting, had an idea: "Let's use a !"

The cousins tried the magnets from Israel's science kit. A magnet can things made of iron, like paper clips and metal lids: it pulls them toward it without touching them. But it doesn't attract paper, plastic or wood.

Then they brought two magnets close together. One way, they snapped together. When they flipped one, the magnets pushed apart and wouldn't touch at all. "Every magnet has two ," Israel explained. "Opposite poles attract and like poles ."

Alejandro slowly moved a magnet away from a paper clip. When the magnet was close, the clip jumped to it; when it was far, the clip didn't move. changes the magnet's force: it is stronger up close.

Meanwhile, Adrián rubbed a balloon on his hair. His hair stood up like a hedgehog! Then the balloon picked up little bits of paper from the table. "That's ," Alejandro laughed. "It pushes and pulls without touching too, like magnets."

At last, the cousins began to a solution. They drew a plan: a small magnet on the door frame and a metal plate on the little door. That way, the magnet would hold it shut.

They tested their design. The wind no longer blew the door open, but Whiskers pushed it with his head and walked right through. At first the magnet was too strong and Whiskers couldn't open it, so they swapped it for a weaker one.

That night, Whiskers slept warm by the stove. "Engineers test, make mistakes and improve," said Israel. "Just like us!"

Glossary

magnet
An object that attracts iron and some other metals without touching them.
attract
To pull something closer. A magnet attracts paper clips.
repel
To push something away. Two like poles of magnets repel each other.
pole
One of the two ends of a magnet, where its force is strongest. They are called north and south.
distance
How far apart or how close two things are.
static electricity
An electric charge that builds up when you rub some objects, like a balloon on your hair. It can attract light things.
design
To think up and draw a plan to solve a problem or build something.

Questions

  1. AQuestion A. What things can a magnet attract?

    A magnet attracts things made of iron, like paper clips and metal lids, but not paper or wood.

  2. BQuestion B. What happens when two like poles come close?

    Opposite poles attract and like poles repel.

  3. CQuestion C. How does a magnet's force change with distance?

    When the magnet was close, the clip jumped; when it was far, the clip didn't move.

  4. DQuestion D. What made the balloon pick up the bits of paper?

    Rubbing the balloon on his hair made static electricity, which pulls without touching.

  5. EQuestion E. Did the cousins' design work?

    The wind no longer opened the door, but Whiskers pushed it and walked right through.

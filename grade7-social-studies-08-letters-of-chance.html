The letters that measured luck

A story about an unfinished game, two men who solved it by mail without ever meeting, and how the world began to think differently about chance.

The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Selena and Elías were playing a game: each coin flip gave one point, and the first to reach 3 points would win ten candies. Selena had 2 points and Elías had 1 when the bell rang. "Split it five and five," said Elías. "No way," said Selena. "I'm winning!" Ms. Novak smiled. "You two just found a problem that bothered people for more than a hundred years."

"In 1654, a French writer who loved games asked the scientist Blaise Pascal this exact question," said Ms. Novak. "If a game stops before it ends, how should the players fairly divide the prize?" Pascal lived in Paris. To think it through, he wrote to Pierre de Fermat, a lawyer and mathematician who lived in Toulouse, in the south of France.

"That summer, the two men wrote letters back and forth," Ms. Novak said. "They never met in person. Their traveled for days on the roads of France." "So they did math by mail," said Elías. "Like texting, but really slow."

Sophia drew a tree on the board. "Selena needs one more point, and Elías needs two. So the game would end in at most two more flips. Let's imagine both flips, even if the game ends early: Selena-Selena, Selena-Elías, Elías-Selena, Elías-Elías." "That's their method," said Ms. Novak. "Pascal and Fermat counted the ways the game could end. Selena wins in three of the four equally likely cases, so she should get three fourths of the prize." Selena took seven candies and a half. Elías took two and a half and sighed.

"Before this, many people believed that games of were controlled by luck or ," said Ms. Novak. "Pascal and Fermat showed that chance could be measured with numbers. Their letters helped start a new branch of mathematics: ."

"How did other people find out?" asked Sophia. "Scientists in Europe had a of letters," said Ms. Novak. "They copied letters, sent them to friends in other countries and met in small groups to discuss them. A few years later, a Dutch scientist named Christiaan Huygens heard about the problem in Paris and wrote one of the first books on probability."

Then she showed how the new idea changed daily life. Over time, people used probability to plan for ships and houses, to study how long people live and, much later, to forecast the weather. "When the weather report says there's a 70 percent chance of rain," she said, "that's Pascal and Fermat's idea at work."

Elías unwrapped one of his candies. "So in 1654, people stopped thinking it was all luck?" "Not everyone, and not all at once," said Ms. Novak. "Perspectives change slowly. But two letters started to change how people saw an uncertain world." Selena held up her seven and a half candies. "I like this perspective."

Check your thinking

The painter uses hands and eyes to tell the story: the cheater's hand hides an ace, and the women's glances show they are working together. The young man trusts luck, but the game is not fair. Probability helps people understand what a fair game is, so they can notice when the chances are not what they seem.

Glossary

chance
How likely something is to happen when the result is not certain.
azar, probabilidad: Qué tan probable es que algo pase cuando el resultado no es seguro.
probability
A number from 0 to 1 that tells how likely an event is. The study of chance.
probabilidad: Un número del 0 al 1 que indica qué tan probable es un suceso. El estudio del azar.
correspondence
Letters that people write to each other over time.
correspondencia: Las cartas que las personas se escriben a lo largo del tiempo.
network
A group of people or places connected to one another, so information can travel between them.
red: Un grupo de personas o lugares conectados entre sí, por donde puede viajar la información.
fate
The idea that events are decided by a power beyond our control.
destino: La idea de que los sucesos los decide un poder que no podemos controlar.
insurance
Paying a small amount regularly so that you get help paying for a big loss if it happens.
seguro: Pagar una cantidad pequeña con regularidad para recibir ayuda si ocurre una pérdida grande.

Challenge: explain your thinking

How perspectives change

Explain how people's perspective on chance changed after 1654. Describe the old view and the new view, and give one example from today that shows the new view.

Cómo cambian las perspectivas. Explica cómo cambió después de 1654 la perspectiva de la gente sobre el azar. Describe la idea antigua y la nueva, y da un ejemplo de hoy que muestre la idea nueva.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Old view: many people believed that games and uncertain events were controlled by luck or fate. New view: chance can be measured with numbers, using probability. Examples today: weather forecasts (a 70 percent chance of rain), insurance prices, or the chance of winning a game. The change was slow: it spread through letters, books and teaching over many years.

Go further

Suppose the game stopped when Selena needed 1 point and Elías needed 3. How many equally likely ways could the next three flips go, and in how many does Elías win? How should they divide the ten candies?

Para ir más lejos. Supón que la partida se detuvo cuando a Selena le faltaba 1 punto y a Elías 3. ¿De cuántas maneras igualmente probables podrían salir los tres lanzamientos siguientes, y en cuántas gana Elías? ¿Cómo deberían repartirse los diez dulces?

Check your thinking

Three flips can come out in 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 equally likely ways. Elías wins only if he wins all three flips: 1 way out of 8. Selena wins in the other 7. So Selena should get 7/8 of the candies (8 and 3/4) and Elías 1/8 (1 and 1/4).

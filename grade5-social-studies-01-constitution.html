The story

Mr. Okafor turned on the screen. A big painting appeared: a room with tall windows, curtains and dozens of men in long coats. A tall man stood on a platform, holding a paper. "This is the Constitutional ," said Mr. Okafor. "Philadelphia, 1787. Here, the wrote the of the United States."

"Wow," said Daniel. "So this is what it looked like." Mr. Okafor smiled. "Are you sure? Before we decide, let's ask the painting some questions. Every has a , a date and a ."

The painting was made by an artist named Junius Brutus Stearns, in the year 1856. Daniel counted on his fingers. "From 1787 to 1856... that's 69 years later!" "Exactly," said Mr. Okafor. "Stearns was not in the room. He was not even born yet. So the painting shows what he imagined, not what he saw."

Athena looked closely. "Then it's not a photo. It's like a story in pictures." "Good thinking," said the teacher. "Some details may be true, and others may be the artist's ideas. Historians have counted 39 delegates in the painting, but on the day they signed, only 38 were in the room."

"Why would he paint it like that?" asked Selena. "Look at George Washington," said Mr. Okafor. "He stands higher than everyone, with light shining on him. Stearns wanted people in 1856 to see Washington as a great leader." The painting had a purpose: to make people feel proud.

"So is the painting useless?" asked Daniel. "Not at all," said the teacher. "It's very useful for learning what Americans in 1856 thought about the Convention. But to learn what really happened in 1787, we need sources from 1787, like the notes that James Madison wrote during the meetings."

Then Mr. Okafor asked a harder question. "Look at everyone in the painting. Who is missing?" The class looked again. Selena raised her hand. "There are no women," she said. "And there are no Black people and no Native Americans."

"That's true about the real Convention too," said Mr. Okafor. "Only white men were delegates. Women, enslaved people and Native nations had no voice in writing the Constitution, even though its rules would affect them." The class was quiet for a moment.

"Over many years, people fought to change those rules," the teacher added. "We'll learn about them this year." Athena drew the painting in her sketchbook. Then she added a few more people in the doorway: a woman, a Black man and a Native American girl. "My version," she said. "Painted in the 21st century, by Athena. Purpose: to show everyone."

Check your thinking

Most people look at Washington first, because he is higher and the light falls on him. Artists use light, size and position to tell us who is important. Stearns wanted people to feel proud of the Constitution and of Washington. Art can be used to shape what people believe.

Glossary

constitution
The written plan of a government. It says how the government works and what rights people have.
constitución: El plan escrito de un gobierno. Dice cómo funciona el gobierno y qué derechos tienen las personas.
convention
A big meeting of people who come together to make decisions.
convención: Una gran reunión de personas que se juntan para tomar decisiones.
delegate
A person chosen to speak and decide for a group.
delegado: Una persona elegida para hablar y decidir en nombre de un grupo.
historical source
Anything from the past that gives us information: a letter, a photo, a painting, a newspaper.
fuente histórica: Cualquier cosa del pasado que nos da información: una carta, una foto, una pintura, un periódico.
creator
The person who made a source.
autor: La persona que hizo una fuente.
purpose
The reason why something was made.
propósito: La razón por la que se hizo algo.

    ¿Quiénes notó Selena que faltaban en la pintura?

Challenge: explain your thinking

Judge a source

You want to know what the weather was like on the day the Constitution was signed. Which source would be more useful: Stearns's painting from 1856 or a diary written by a delegate in 1787? Explain using the creator, the date and the purpose.

Juzga una fuente. Quieres saber cómo estaba el tiempo el día en que se firmó la Constitución. ¿Qué fuente sería más útil: la pintura de Stearns de 1856 o un diario escrito por un delegado en 1787? Explica usando el autor, la fecha y el propósito.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

The diary would be more useful. Its creator was in Philadelphia, it was written at the same time as the event, and its purpose was to record what happened. The painting was made 69 years later by someone who was not there, and its purpose was to make people proud.

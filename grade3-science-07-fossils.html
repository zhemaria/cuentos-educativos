The story

One Sunday, Great-Grandpa took Israel and Lucía to the Field Museum in Chicago. In the great hall, Sue was waiting for them: a T. rex skeleton longer than a school bus!

"Sue lived about 67 million years ago," Lucía read on the sign. "She is named after Sue Hendrickson, the woman who found her bones in 1990."

"Those bones are a ," Great-Grandpa explained, "the remains or marks of a living thing from a very long time ago, saved in ." The animals left in mud that later hardened are fossils too.

T. rexes don't exist anymore: they are . "So how do we know what they looked like?" asked Israel. "Thanks to fossils," said Great-Grandpa. " study fossils to learn what living things of the past were like."

Next they stopped at a case full of stone seashells and snails. "These shells were found right here in Illinois," said Great-Grandpa.

Israel thought about it. "But there's no ocean in Illinois…" "Not today," smiled Great-Grandpa. "But these fossils are : millions of years ago, a warm, shallow sea covered Illinois. Fossils tell us what the of the past was like."

In the same case was a strange animal with a long snout like a trunk. "It's the Tully monster," Lucía read, "the official state fossil of Illinois. It lived in the waters of Illinois about 300 million years ago."

"If you find fish fossils on a mountain," said Great-Grandpa, "you know there was water there once. If you find fossils of warm-climate plants in a cold place, you know that place has changed."

On the way home, Israel looked out the train window. He imagined Chicago covered by a sea full of strange animals. "Rocks keep secrets," he said, "and fossils tell them to us."

Glossary

fossil
The remains or marks of a living thing from long ago that were saved in rock, like bones, shells or tracks.
paleontologist
A scientist who studies fossils to learn about living things of the past.
extinct
No longer existing: none of that kind of living thing are left, like the dinosaurs.
evidence
Something that helps us know an idea is true.
environment
What a place was like: whether it had water or land, whether it was cold or hot, and what plants and animals lived there.
rock
Hard material from the Earth. Many fossils form inside rocks.
track
The mark a foot, paw or shell leaves when it presses into the ground.

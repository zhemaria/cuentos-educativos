The story

Tap. Tap-tap. Taaap. Mr. Mensah was tapping on an old brass key on his desk. "On May 24, 1844," he said, "Samuel Morse tapped a key like this one in the United States Capitol in Washington, and his partner, Alfred Vail, received the message in Baltimore, about 40 miles away. The words, chosen by Annie Ellsworth, the young daughter of a friend, came from the Bible: 'What hath God wrought?'"

"How did words travel through a wire?" asked Salma. "Morse's turned every letter into only two kinds of : a short one, the dot, and a long one, the dash," said Mr. Mensah. "The electric current in the wire was either on for a short time or on for a long time."

Then he lined the class up for a game. He whispered a sentence to Daniel: "The lab needs seven clean beakers by Thursday." Daniel whispered it to Selena, and so on, to the end of the line. Elías said it out loud: "The cat needs eleven green sneakers on Thursday." Everyone laughed. "A whisper is like an ," said Mr. Mensah. "Every time it passes, it changes a little, and the adds up until the message is wrong."

Next, Athena and Israel stood at the back of the room with a flashlight and sent the same sentence in Morse code: short blinks and long blinks. The flashlight's battery was weak, and some blinks were dim. But Salma could still tell a short blink from a long one, so she wrote down every letter correctly. "A only has a few possible values," said Mr. Mensah. "Even if the signal gets weaker or noisy, the receiver only has to decide: dot or dash? Then the message can be rebuilt perfectly."

"Your phone does the same thing," he said. "It turns your voice into numbers, ones and zeros, before sending it. A one or a zero is easy to recognize, even far away."

"How did Morse know the message arrived right?" asked Sophia. "A moment later, Vail tapped the same message back to Washington," said Mr. Mensah. "If the copy that came back matched the original, the line was working."

At the end of the class, Mr. Mensah tapped one more message on the brass key. Israel decoded it on the board: G-O-O-D-B-Y-E.

Sophia wrote in her notebook: Fewer choices, fewer mistakes. That's why digital wins.

Glossary

signal
A sound, light, electric current or wave that carries information from one place to another.
señal: Un sonido, una luz, una corriente eléctrica o una onda que lleva información de un lugar a otro.
code
A system of signals or symbols that stand for letters, numbers or words.
código: Un sistema de señales o símbolos que representan letras, números o palabras.
analog signal
A signal that changes smoothly, like the shape of a voice. Small changes in it change the message.
señal analógica: Una señal que cambia de forma continua, como la forma de una voz. Pequeños cambios en ella cambian el mensaje.
digital signal
A signal made of a few separate values, like dots and dashes, or ones and zeros.
señal digital: Una señal formada por unos pocos valores separados, como puntos y rayas, o unos y ceros.
noise
Unwanted changes that get mixed into a signal and can spoil the message.
ruido: Cambios no deseados que se mezclan con una señal y pueden estropear el mensaje.
binary
A way of writing information with only two symbols, 1 and 0.
binario: Una forma de escribir información con solo dos símbolos, el 1 y el 0.

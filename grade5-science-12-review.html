At the end of the year, the school gym turned into a science fair. Families walked between the tables, and Ms. Kowalski handed each visitor a card with seven boxes to stamp. "One stamp for each friend's station," she said. "They're going to show you everything they learned this year."

At the first station, Selena sprayed a little orange juice in the air. A few seconds later, a girl at the next table said, "Mmm, oranges!" "The smell is made of tiny ," Selena explained, "too small to see, that spread through the air." Stamp.

Daniel had a sealed bag with baking soda and vinegar on a scale. The bag puffed up, but the number on the scale didn't change. "A new substance formed, a gas, but nothing got in or out," he said. "So the weight was ." A grandfather shook his hand. Stamp.

Salma had four white powders and some vinegar. She showed how to identify the baking soda because it fizzed, without tasting anything. Next to her, Elías dropped a ball and pointed at a globe. " pulls everything toward the center of Earth," he said. "Even in Australia." Two more stamps.

Athena showed her drawing of a corn plant, a chicken and a runner, with the sun at the top. "Almost all the in our food started as energy from the Sun," she said. Beside it was a potted bean plant. "And the plant built its body mostly from air and water, not from the soil." Stamp.

Israel had a jar of compost with real worms. "Watch out, they're shy," he warned. " break down dead plants and animals and return their matter to the soil," he said, "so new plants can grow. Matter goes around and around." A little boy said the worms were his favorite part. Stamp.

Sophia had a globe and a flashlight. She turned the globe to show day and night, and she walked it around the flashlight to explain why the winter stars are different from the summer stars. "And the Sun looks brighter than the other only because it is much closer to us," she added. Stamp.

At the end of the table, there was a big jug of water and a tiny cup. "This is all the fresh water we can reach," said Selena, pointing at the tiny cup. "So we must protect our , like Lake Michigan." Next to it was the trash catcher that they had built with rocks tied to the bottom.

At the end of the night, every card was full of stamps. Ms. Kowalski gathered the seven friends. "You are real scientists now," she said. "You observe, you test, you use evidence and you explain." Israel raised his hand. "Does a real scientist get the leftover cookies?" "Only if he explains where their energy came from," said the teacher. "From the Sun!" all seven shouted at once.

Glossary

particle
A tiny piece of matter, much too small to see.
partícula: Un trocito de materia, demasiado pequeño para verlo.
conserved
Kept the same. When matter changes in a sealed container, its total weight is conserved.
se conserva: Se mantiene igual. Cuando la materia cambia en un recipiente cerrado, su peso total se conserva.
gravity
The force that pulls objects toward the center of Earth.
gravedad: La fuerza que jala los objetos hacia el centro de la Tierra.
energy
The ability to make something move, grow, warm up or change.
energía: La capacidad de hacer que algo se mueva, crezca, se caliente o cambie.
decomposer
A living thing that breaks down dead plants and animals and returns their matter to the soil.
descomponedor: Un ser vivo que descompone plantas y animales muertos y devuelve su materia a la tierra.
star
A giant ball of very hot gas that makes its own light. The Sun is a star.
estrella: Una bola gigante de gas muy caliente que produce su propia luz. El Sol es una estrella.
natural resource
Something from nature that people use, like water, air or soil.
recurso natural: Algo de la naturaleza que usan las personas, como el agua, el aire o la tierra.

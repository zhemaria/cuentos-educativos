The story

The class visited the Adler Planetarium, on the shore of Lake Michigan. In the dark dome of the , thousands of tiny dots of light appeared on the ceiling. "Those are ," said the guide. "And here is the most important star of all." A giant yellow ball filled the screen.

"That's not a star," said Elías. "That's the ." "The Sun is a star," said the guide. "It is a giant ball of very hot gas, just like the stars you see at night." Elías frowned. "But the Sun is enormous and super bright, and the stars are tiny little dots."

"Let's find out why," said Ms. Kowalski. After the show, she took the class to a long hallway with the lights turned down. She gave Israel and Daniel two flashlights that were exactly the same. Israel stayed close to the class. Daniel walked all the way to the end of the hallway.

"Now, turn them on," said the teacher. Israel's flashlight was so bright that everyone squinted. Daniel's flashlight, far away, looked like a tiny dot, much less bright. "Daniel, your flashlight is broken," said Elías. "It's not broken!" Daniel shouted from far away.

"The two flashlights are the same," Ms. Kowalski explained. "The only difference is the . The closer a light is, the brighter and bigger it looks." Athena drew both flashlights in her sketchbook: one big circle of light, and one little dot.

"So the Sun is like Israel's flashlight," said Athena, "and the other stars are like Daniel's." "Exactly," said the guide. "The Sun looks so bright because it is much closer to Earth than any other star."

"How far are the other stars?" asked Elías. "The Sun is about 93 million miles away," said the guide. "The next closest star is more than four away. Its light travels for more than four years to reach us!" Elías whistled. "That's farther than my grandma's house."

"In fact," said the guide, "some stars are much bigger and brighter than our Sun. They only look like little dots because they are so far away." The that we see depends on two things: how much light the star really gives off and how far away it is.

When the class went outside, the afternoon Sun was shining on the lake. Elías put on his sunglasses. "Sorry, other stars," he said to the sky. "Our star is closer, so it wins." Daniel came running out, still holding his flashlight. "And I'm closer now," he said, "so I win too."

Glossary

star
A giant ball of very hot gas that makes its own light and heat.
estrella: Una bola gigante de gas muy caliente que produce su propia luz y calor.
Sun
The star closest to Earth. It gives us light and heat.
Sol: La estrella más cercana a la Tierra. Nos da luz y calor.
distance
How far one thing is from another.
distancia: Qué tan lejos está una cosa de otra.
brightness
How much light something seems to give. From far away, a light looks less bright.
brillo: Cuánta luz parece dar algo. Desde lejos, una luz se ve menos brillante.
light-year
The distance light travels in one year: almost six trillion miles.
año luz: La distancia que recorre la luz en un año: casi seis billones de millas.
planetarium
A building with a round ceiling that shows the stars and planets.
planetario: Un edificio con un techo redondo donde se ven las estrellas y los planetas.

  1. AQuestion A. What is the Sun?

    ¿Qué es el Sol?

    The Sun is a star: a giant ball of very hot gas, like the stars we see at night.

  2. BQuestion B. How did Daniel's flashlight look from far away?

    ¿Cómo se veía la linterna de Daniel desde lejos?

    Far away, Daniel's flashlight looked like a tiny dot, much less bright.

  3. CQuestion C. Why did the two flashlights look so different?

    ¿Por qué las dos linternas se veían tan distintas?

    The flashlights were the same; the only difference was the distance.

  4. DQuestion D. Why does the Sun look brighter than other stars?

    ¿Por qué el Sol se ve más brillante que otras estrellas?

    The Sun looks so bright because it is much closer to Earth than any other star.

  5. EQuestion E. How long does light from the next closest star take to reach us?

    ¿Cuánto tarda en llegarnos la luz de la siguiente estrella más cercana?

    The next closest star is more than four light-years away, so its light travels more than four years.

Israel's grandfather pulled his old bicycle out of the garage. Twenty years ago it had been shiny and silver. Now it was covered in an orange, crusty coat. "It turned orange all by itself," said Abuelo. "Like magic."

Salma, who had come over for breakfast, shook her head. "It's not magic. It's , and it's chemistry." She took two nails from her backpack, one new and shiny, one old and orange. "Ms. Rivera gave us these for homework. We have to compare their ."

They made a table of . The new nail was gray, smooth and hard, and a magnet grabbed it right away. The rusty part was orange, rough and crumbly, and the magnet barely pulled it. "Different color, different texture, different magnetism," said Salma. "That means the rust is a new substance."

"Where did it come from?" asked Israel. "The iron in the bike reacted with oxygen from the air and with water from the rain," said Salma. "Their atoms rearranged and made iron oxide, which we call rust. That's a ."

Abuelo scratched his head. "But when I bent the handlebar years ago, it was still iron." "Right," said Salma. "Bending changed the shape, but it was still the same substance. That's a . No new substance was made."

Inside, Israel's mom was making pancakes. Israel watched the batter bubble on the hot pan. "Is this a chemical reaction too?" "Yes!" said Salma. "The baking powder reacts and makes that puff the pancake up. And the cooked pancake has a new color, a new smell and a new texture that the batter didn't have."

"So how do I know when a reaction happened?" asked Israel, with his mouth full. "Compare the properties before and after," said Salma. "New colors, new smells, gas bubbles or a change in temperature are clues. But clues alone don't prove it. Boiling water makes bubbles too, and it's still water. The real test is evidence that a new substance formed, with different properties from what you started with."

After breakfast, Abuelo found a can of paint. "If air and water make rust," he said, "then paint should keep them away from the iron." Israel grinned. "Abuelo, you're a chemist." "No," said Abuelo, picking up a brush. "I'm a man who wants his bike back."

Glossary

property
A trait that describes a substance, like its color, smell, hardness or whether a magnet attracts it.
propiedad: Una característica que describe una sustancia, como su color, su olor, su dureza o si la atrae un imán.
chemical reaction
A change in which atoms rearrange to make one or more new substances with new properties.
reacción química: Un cambio en el que los átomos se reorganizan para formar una o más sustancias nuevas con propiedades nuevas.
rust
A reddish-orange substance that forms when iron reacts with oxygen and water.
óxido (herrumbre): Una sustancia anaranjada rojiza que se forma cuando el hierro reacciona con el oxígeno y el agua.
physical change
A change in size, shape or state that does not make a new substance, like cutting paper or melting ice.
cambio físico: Un cambio de tamaño, forma o estado que no produce una sustancia nueva, como cortar papel o derretir hielo.
gas bubbles
Bubbles of gas. They can be a clue that a reaction made a new gas, but boiling water also makes bubbles without making a new substance.
burbujas de gas: Burbujas de gas. Pueden ser una pista de que una reacción produjo un gas nuevo, pero el agua hirviendo también hace burbujas sin formar una sustancia nueva.
data
Information, like measurements and observations, that scientists collect and study.
datos: Información, como medidas y observaciones, que los científicos reúnen y estudian.

Check your thinking

It is a chemical reaction. The new color, the new smell and the smoke are clues. Smoke is not just one gas: it is a mix of gases and tiny bits of solid floating in the air. The strongest evidence is that the ash has properties the paper never had: it is black and crumbly, and it doesn't bend like paper. New properties show that a new substance formed.

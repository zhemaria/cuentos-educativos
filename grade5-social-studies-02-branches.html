The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

On a field trip downtown, Mr. Okafor stopped the class in Federal Plaza, next to a giant red sculpture. "Look at these buildings," he said. "Inside them, people work for the national government: judges, officials and members of Congress have offices here."

Elías unrolled a drawing he had made in class: a tree with three thick branches. "The government is like a tree," he said. "The makes the laws. The carries them out. And the decides what the laws mean."

"Why three?" asked Israel. "Wouldn't one boss be faster?" "Faster, maybe," said Mr. Okafor, "but dangerous. The people who wrote the Constitution didn't want a king. So they used : each branch can limit the others. Congress makes a law, the president can veto it, and the courts can decide if it follows the Constitution."

"And it's not only in Washington, D.C.," the teacher continued. "There are three . In Chicago, the mayor leads the city and the City Council makes city laws. In Springfield, the governor leads Illinois and the General Assembly makes state laws. And in Washington, D.C., there are the president and Congress."

Sophia noticed a round bronze on the building, with an eagle in the middle. "What's that bird holding?" she asked. "That's the Great Seal of the United States," said Mr. Okafor. "The eagle holds an olive branch in one claw and 13 arrows in the other."

"The olive branch stands for peace, and the arrows stand for being ready to defend the country," the teacher explained. "And look: the eagle's head is turned toward the olive branch. It means the country prefers peace." Sophia looked again. "So a picture can say something without words."

Back on the bus, Israel asked, "Has the government always been the same?" "No," said Mr. Okafor. "In 1789, the Supreme Court had six justices. Today it has nine. And the House of Representatives started with 65 members. Now it has 435, because the country grew."

"The jobs of the branches have changed too," he added. "Today the president leads a huge executive branch with millions of workers, from mail carriers to park rangers. In 1789, it was very small." Elías grabbed a pencil and added many little leaves to his tree. "It's still growing," he said.

At home that night, Israel's little cousin wanted to play. "I'm the president," she said, "and I make all the rules." Israel shook his head seriously. "Sorry. Congress makes the rules, you carry them out, and Grandma is the Supreme Court." Grandma, from the kitchen, agreed.

Check your thinking

"Out of many, one" means that many states, and many people, come together to form one nation. A seal uses symbols instead of words: each object stands for an idea. Your school seal could hold a book (learning) and a heart (kindness), for example.

Glossary

legislative branch
The part of government that makes the laws. In the nation, it is Congress.
rama legislativa: La parte del gobierno que hace las leyes. En la nación, es el Congreso.
executive branch
The part of government that carries out the laws. In the nation, it is led by the president.
rama ejecutiva: La parte del gobierno que hace cumplir las leyes. En la nación, la dirige el presidente.
judicial branch
The part of government that decides what the laws mean, with courts and judges.
rama judicial: La parte del gobierno que decide qué significan las leyes, con tribunales y jueces.
checks and balances
The way each branch can limit the power of the others, so no branch becomes too strong.
controles y equilibrios: La forma en que cada rama puede limitar el poder de las otras, para que ninguna sea demasiado fuerte.
level of government
Local (city), state (Illinois) or national (the United States).
nivel de gobierno: Local (la ciudad), estatal (Illinois) o nacional (Estados Unidos).
seal
An official picture or symbol that stands for a government.
sello: Una imagen o símbolo oficial que representa a un gobierno.

Questions

  1. AQuestion A. What does each branch of government do?

    ¿Qué hace cada rama del gobierno?

    The legislative branch makes laws, the executive carries them out and the judicial decides what they mean.

  2. BQuestion B. Why did the writers of the Constitution use checks and balances?

    ¿Por qué los autores de la Constitución usaron controles y equilibrios?

    Each branch can limit the others, so no branch becomes too powerful, like a king.

  3. CQuestion C. Who makes the laws for the state of Illinois?

    ¿Quién hace las leyes del estado de Illinois?

    In Springfield, the General Assembly makes state laws, and the governor leads Illinois.

  4. DQuestion D. What does the eagle's head turned toward the olive branch mean?

    ¿Qué significa que la cabeza del águila mire hacia la rama de olivo?

    The olive branch stands for peace, and the eagle looks toward it: the country prefers peace.

  5. EQuestion E. How has the Supreme Court changed since 1789?

    ¿Cómo ha cambiado la Corte Suprema desde 1789?

    In 1789 the Supreme Court had six justices; today it has nine.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Explain how it changed

The House of Representatives grew from 65 members to 435. Explain why it grew, and why it matters that each state has more or fewer representatives.

Explica cómo cambió. La Cámara de Representantes creció de 65 miembros a 435. Explica por qué creció y por qué importa que cada estado tenga más o menos representantes.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

It grew because the country added new states and many more people. In the House, states with more people have more representatives, so the number of representatives decides how strong each state's voice is when Congress makes laws.

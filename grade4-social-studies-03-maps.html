The story

For the geography project, Ms. Rivera gave each team a giant sheet of paper. "Make a map of Illinois," she said, "but make it a map that can help you find any place." Athena opened her sketchbook and grinned. This was her dream project.

First she drew the shape of the state, tall and narrow. Salma marked the . To the north was Wisconsin. To the east were Lake Michigan and Indiana. To the west was the Mississippi River, which separates Illinois from Iowa and Missouri. And to the south was the Ohio River, which separates it from Kentucky.

"Now comes the hard part," said Athena. "The ." With a ruler she drew some lines across the map and others up and down. "Lines of run east and west, and they tell how far north a place is," she explained. "Lines of run north and south, and they tell how far west or east it is."

Israel found Chicago with his finger. "We're close to 42 degrees north and 88 degrees west!" he shouted. "I want number 42 on my soccer jersey." "And 88 on the back?" Salma giggled.

Using coordinates, they found Springfield, close to 40 degrees north and 90 degrees west, and the southern tip of the state, close to 37 degrees north, where the Ohio River joins the Mississippi.

Next, Salma painted the three of Illinois, each in its own color. In the north are Chicago and its suburbs, where most people live. In the middle are flat fields of corn and soybeans. And in the south are hills and forests.

"We still need a ," Israel reminded them. A scale tells how much real distance a length on the map stands for. On their map, each centimeter stood for 50 kilometers. They measured the state from top to bottom: almost 13 centimeters. That's about 630 real kilometers, or roughly 390 miles!

Last of all, Athena wanted to draw the . Illinois has 102 of them. She got tired at number 30 and decided to mark only Cook County, where Chicago is, with a tiny star.

When the team presented the map, Ms. Rivera asked, "What's near 40 north and 90 west?" The whole class answered at once: "Springfield!" Athena signed her work in the corner, just like a famous artist.

Glossary

border
The line where one state or country ends and another begins.
coordinates
A pair of numbers, latitude and longitude, that tell exactly where a place is.
latitude
Map lines that run east and west. They tell how far north or south a place is.
longitude
Map lines that run north and south. They tell how far east or west a place is.
region
An area with features in common, such as its land, climate or way of life.
scale
The part of a map that tells how much real distance a length on the paper stands for.
county
One of the parts a state is divided into. Illinois has 102.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Map it yourself

Draw your own map of Illinois. Add one natural feature, such as a river, Lake Michigan or the southern forests, and give it a symbol in a key. Then write a coordinates question for a classmate, like Ms. Rivera's.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Check your thinking

Example: a blue line for the Illinois River and a small tree for the southern forests, both shown in the key. Question: "What city is close to 42 degrees north and 88 degrees west?" Answer: Chicago.

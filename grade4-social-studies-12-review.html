The story

To celebrate the end of fourth grade, Ms. Rivera's class took the train from Chicago to Springfield. They paid for part of their tickets with the money they had saved from the pumpkin stand. "Our is taking us on a trip!" Salma said proudly.

At the station, Daniel looked down the platforms. "My great-grandma came to Chicago from Mississippi on a train like this one," he said. "Her family chose to north in the Great Migration." "Chicago grew really fast because of the canal of 1848, the trains and the people who came from all over to work," Sophia added.

Near Joliet, the train crossed the Des Plaines River. Elías pressed his face to the glass. "The Jolliet and Marquette traveled on this river in 1673!" he said. "The Native Illinois peoples showed them the way." "And the city of Joliet is named after Jolliet," Ms. Rivera added.

After that, all they could see out the window were green fields, flat and endless. "All of this used to be ," said Israel. "Its fertile soil is a natural resource. That's why Illinois grows so much corn and so many soybeans." Daniel looked for grass taller than his dad, but all he saw was corn.

Athena unfolded her map with its . "We're heading southwest," she said, "from almost 42 degrees north to almost 40." Salma pointed farther south on the map. "And down there, near Collinsville, is Cahokia, the city of mounds. The Native peoples of Illinois are still here today."

Selena counted the years on her fingers. "Illinois has been a state since December 3, 1818, and Springfield has been the capital since 1839. And because they moved the border, Chicago ended up in Illinois!"

In Springfield they walked to the Capitol. "This is where the makes the laws," Sophia remembered. "The governor carries them out, and the Supreme Court, right nearby, explains them." "And people have the to ask for laws to change," Selena added, "like we did with the corner."

In the afternoon they visited Lincoln's home. "He lived here for seventeen years before he became president," said Elías, who by now knew everything.

On the ride home, after dark, almost everyone fell asleep. Israel watched the lights of the towns go by. Illinois was like the class's string map: lots of places, lots of stories and one home.

Glossary

profit
The money left over after paying the costs.
migrate
To move from one place to another to live, within the same country or to a different one.
explorer
A person who travels to unfamiliar places to learn about them and make maps.
prairie
A large, mostly flat area covered with tall grasses and wildflowers and very few trees.
coordinates
A pair of numbers, latitude and longitude, that tell exactly where a place is.
legislative branch
The part of government that writes laws and votes on them. In Illinois it is called the General Assembly.
right
Something all people are free to do or have, which the law protects.

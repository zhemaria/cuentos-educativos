The story

One morning, Israel looked at his breakfast: a banana, orange juice, hot chocolate, corn cereal and toast. "Grandma, where does all this come from?" he asked.

"Let's find out," said Grandma, and they took out the world map. Israel read the banana's sticker. "It comes from Guatemala!" he said. "Bananas don't grow in Illinois because it's too cold."

When one country buys from another country, we say it them. The oranges in the juice came from Florida, and the cacao in the chocolate came from a country in West Africa.

"And we sell things too," said Grandma. "Illinois is one of the states that the most corn and soybeans. Many of those are to other countries." Israel's cereal was made with Illinois corn.

With colored strings, they connected each food on the map to the place it came from. The strings reached many countries!

"Every time we buy something, we connect with people in faraway places," said Grandma. "We are : we buy and use the products other people worked to make."

That afternoon, Israel checked the labels on his clothes and toys. His t-shirt came from Honduras, and his ball from a country far away. His dad, on the other hand, worked at a Chicago factory that made candy sold all over the world.

"The world is full of invisible strings," Israel thought. "And my breakfast traveled around the world to reach my table!"

Glossary

product
Something that is made or grown to be sold, like a fruit, a toy or a t-shirt.
produce
To make or grow products.
import
To buy products that were made in another country.
export
To sell products to another country.
transportation
The way people or things are moved from one place to another, like by ship, train, truck or plane.
consumer
A person who buys and uses products or services.
crop
Plants grown in fields to be harvested, like corn or soybeans.

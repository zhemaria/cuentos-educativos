The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

On Saturday morning, Salma's grandmother washed her four kitchen jars, and all the paper labels slid off in the sink. "Ay, no," she said. "Now I don't know which one is sugar, which is salt, which is flour and which is baking soda." Salma and Selena looked at each other. This was a job for scientists.

"First rule," said Salma, who remembered what Ms. Kowalski always said. "Scientists never taste a mystery powder. It could be dangerous." Her grandmother nodded, impressed. "Then how will you know?" "We'll their ," Selena said.

Salma looked at each powder with a magnifying glass. Two of the powders were made of tiny , with flat sides that sparkled in the light. The other two were soft and fine, like dust. Selena wrote in her notebook: "Jars 1 and 2: crystals. Jars 3 and 4: fine powder."

For the next test, they put one spoonful of each powder into four cups of warm water and stirred the same number of times. The crystals in jars 1 and 2 disappeared. Jar 4 also mixed in. But the water in cup 3 turned cloudy and white, and the powder settled at the bottom. "Jar 3 is not ," said Selena. "It's the flour!"

Then they put a little of each powder in four small dishes and added a few drops of vinegar. Three dishes did nothing. But the powder from jar 4 started to fizz and bubble right away. "It with vinegar," Salma said. "That's the baking soda." Her grandmother clapped.

Two jars were left: sugar and salt. Both were white crystals, and both dissolved in water. "They look like twins," said Selena. "How can we tell them apart without tasting?"

Salma's grandmother had an idea. She put a pinch from jar 1 in one old spoon and a pinch from jar 2 in another, and she held them over the stove for a moment. Only an adult could do this part. The crystals in jar 2 melted and turned into brown, sticky caramel. The crystals in jar 1 did not change at all.

"Jar 2 is sugar, because sugar melts and browns with heat," said Salma. "And jar 1 is salt." Selena wrote new labels in big letters, and they taped them on the jars.

"You didn't guess once," said the grandmother. "You used properties to every powder." Then she made pancakes with the flour, the baking soda and the sugar. "And the salt?" asked Selena. "A pinch," said her grandmother, "for the scientists who earned it."

Glossary

property
Something we can observe or measure about a material, like color, hardness or whether it dissolves.
propiedad: Algo que podemos observar o medir de un material, como el color, la dureza o si se disuelve.
identify
To find out exactly what something is.
identificar: Averiguar exactamente qué es algo.
crystal
A solid piece with flat sides and a regular shape, like a grain of salt or sugar.
cristal: Un pedacito sólido con caras planas y forma regular, como un grano de sal o de azúcar.
soluble
Able to dissolve in a liquid. Salt is soluble in water; flour is not.
soluble: Que se puede disolver en un líquido. La sal es soluble en agua; la harina no.
react
To change when touching another substance, often with bubbles or heat.
reaccionar: Cambiar al tocar otra sustancia, muchas veces con burbujas o calor.
test
An action that we do to find out a property, always the same way each time.
prueba: Una acción que hacemos para conocer una propiedad, siempre de la misma manera.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Plan the tests

You find a box with a paper clip, a penny, a plastic button and a rubber band all mixed together. Choose two properties you could test to sort them. Tell what each test would show.

Planea las pruebas. Encuentras una caja con un clip, un centavo, un botón de plástico y una liga, todos mezclados. Elige dos propiedades que podrías probar para clasificarlos. Di qué mostraría cada prueba.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

You could test whether each object is magnetic: only the steel paper clip would stick to a magnet. You could also test whether each one stretches: only the rubber band would. Other good tests are whether it bends, whether it shines, or whether it conducts electricity in a circuit with a bulb (the metal objects do).

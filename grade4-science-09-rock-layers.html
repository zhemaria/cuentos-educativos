The story

Sophia's birthday was on Saturday, and her mom baked her a four-layer cake: chocolate, vanilla, strawberry and chocolate again. When they cut it, Elías stared at it very seriously. "This cake looks like a rock," he said. "Just eat it!" Israel laughed.

The next day, Uncle Ken, who is a geologist, took Sophia, Israel and Elías to an Illinois state park. They hiked through a narrow with a stream at the bottom. The walls were super tall and striped with colors: light brown, dark brown and gray.

"It's just like the cake!" Elías shouted. Uncle Ken smiled. "Each stripe is a . It formed when water or wind dropped , like sand or mud, over a very long time. Over the years, the sediment got pressed together and turned into rock."

"So which layer came first?" Sophia asked. "Think about your cake," said her uncle. "Which layer did your mom put down first?" "The bottom one!" said Sophia. "So the bottom layers are the most , and the top layers are the newest." "Exactly," said Uncle Ken. "That's almost always true, as long as nothing has moved them."

Elías found a gray rock next to the stream. It had the shape of a seashell pressed into it. "That's a of a sea animal," said his uncle. "If a rock layer has seashell fossils in it, that's that this place was covered by water a very long time ago."

"There was a sea here?" asked Israel, looking around at the trees. "But we're in Illinois!" "That's right. And other layers in Illinois have fossils of ferns and swamp plants," his uncle said. "That shows there were swamps here at another time."

Then Uncle Ken pointed at the stream. "How do you think this canyon formed?" Israel watched the water trickling between the rocks. "Water wore away the rock, little by little, for thousands of years," said his uncle. "That's how it cut the canyon and showed us the layers, like slicing a cake."

Sophia drew the canyon walls: the layers, the fossils and the stream at the bottom. "The rocks tell a story," she said. "First there was a sea, then other things, and at the end the water cut the canyon. The has changed a lot over time."

Once, at the city museum, Uncle Ken had shown them the Tully monster, the official state fossil of Illinois: a strange, squishy animal that lived in the water about 300 million years ago. That night, Elías dreamed the Tully monster ate his slice of birthday cake. Luckily, it only ate the strawberry layers.

Glossary

rock layer
A band of rock that formed as sand, mud or other bits piled up over a very long time.
sediment
Bits of sand, mud and rock that water or wind carry away and drop somewhere else.
fossil
The remains or prints of a living thing from long, long ago, saved in rock.
evidence
Clues or facts that show something is true.
ancient
From a very long time ago.
canyon
A deep, narrow valley with very steep rock walls.
landscape
The shape of the land in a place: its hills, valleys, rivers and plains.

Questions

Choose the correct answer. If you make a mistake, the story shows you where the answer is.

  1. AQuestion A. How does a rock layer form?

    A layer forms when water or wind drops sediment over a very long time.

  2. BQuestion B. Which layers are the oldest?

    The bottom layers formed first, so they are the oldest.

  3. CQuestion C. What does a seashell fossil in a rock layer show?

    Seashell fossils are evidence that this place was covered by water long ago.

  4. DQuestion D. How did the canyon form?

    Water wore away the rock, little by little, for thousands of years, and cut the canyon.

  5. EQuestion E. What story do the canyon rocks tell?

    The rocks show that the landscape has changed a lot over time.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Look for evidence

Check your thinking

The seashell fossil of a sea animal is evidence, because sea animals live in water. The layers themselves also formed from sediment dropped by water or wind. To find the oldest fossils, look in the bottom layers: they formed first, as long as nothing has moved them.

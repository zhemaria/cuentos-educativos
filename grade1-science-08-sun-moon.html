The story

One winter day, Adrián left school and it was almost dark.

“I want to play outside!” he said. “Where did the sun go?”

His dad explained, “Every day the sun comes up in the morning. That’s sunrise.”

“And every evening it goes down. That’s sunset.”

The moon appeared in the dark sky. That night it looked like a fingernail.

Every night, Adrián drew the moon in his notebook.

Over several weeks, he saw more and more of the moon’s sunlit half, until it looked like a circle. A full moon!

Then he saw less of it again. This pattern repeats.

“In winter there are fewer hours of daylight,” said Dad. “In summer there are more.”

Each season has its pattern.

In summer, Adrián and Israel played outside until late.

The sun, the moon and the stars have patterns that repeat.

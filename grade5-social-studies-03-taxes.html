The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Salma's mother bought a notebook, a box of crayons and a bottle of juice. Salma had added up the prices in her head. But when the receipt came out, the total was higher. "Mom, the machine made a mistake!" she whispered.

Her mother laughed and pointed at a line on the receipt. "No mistake. That's the ." Selena, who had come with them, looked confused. "Who gets that money?" "The government," said Salma's mother. "The city, the county and the state."

The next day, Salma told the story in class. "Why does the government take our money?" she asked. "Because the government pays for that everyone uses," said Mr. Okafor: "schools, parks, libraries, firefighters, police and roads."

"The government gets that money in different ways," he continued. "People pay on what they buy, on what they earn and on the houses they own. Some services also charge fees, like a ticket to a city pool. And sometimes the government borrows money and pays it back later."

"But people haven't always agreed about taxes," Mr. Okafor said. He showed them a picture of a ship at night. "In the 1770s, Great Britain ruled 13 colonies in America. The British government made the pay taxes on things like paper and tea."

"The colonists were angry because they had no ," said the teacher. "Nobody they had chosen was in the British government to vote for them. Their slogan was: No taxation without representation."

"In December 1773, in Boston, a group of colonists climbed onto three ships and threw 342 chests of tea into the harbor." It was a that became famous as the Boston Tea Party. "And that made Britain even angrier," said Mr. Okafor. "A few years later, the Revolutionary War began."

Salma thought about her receipt. "So the difference is that today we choose who decides the taxes?" "Exactly," said Mr. Okafor. "Today, people vote for the mayor, the governor, members of Congress and other officials. If people don't agree with a tax, they can vote for someone else or ask their leaders to change it."

That afternoon, Salma walked past the public library, the fire station and the park with the new swings. "Hey," she said to Selena. "My juice tax helped pay for those swings." Selena grinned. "Then I'm going to swing extra high. I want to get my money's worth."

Pause to connect

🎵 Music · MU:Cn11.1.5

Before the Revolutionary War, British soldiers sang this old song to make fun of the colonists. They thought the colonists were silly country people. Read the verse aloud.

Antes de la Guerra de Independencia, los soldados británicos cantaban esta vieja canción para burlarse de los colonos. Pensaban que eran campesinos tontos. Lee la estrofa en voz alta.

Yankee Doodle went to town
A-riding on a pony,
Stuck a feather in his cap
And called it macaroni.

“Yankee Doodle,” traditional song from the 1700s (public domain).

Good to know: In those days, a "macaroni" was a man who dressed in very fancy, fashionable clothes. The joke is that Yankee Doodle thinks one feather makes him fancy.

Para saber más: En aquella época, un «macaroni» era un hombre que se vestía con ropa muy elegante y a la moda. El chiste es que Yankee Doodle cree que una sola pluma lo hace elegante.

  2. Can you think of a word or a song that people use today to make fun of someone, that the group later used with pride?¿Se te ocurre una palabra o una canción que se usó para burlarse de un grupo y que después ese grupo usó con orgullo?

Think about it, then answer in your notebook or talk it over with someone at home.

Check your thinking

By singing the song themselves, the colonists turned the joke around: they showed they were proud to be simple Americans. A song can change meaning depending on who sings it and why. Music is connected to the history and the feelings of a time.

Glossary

tax
Money that people and businesses pay to the government.
impuesto: Dinero que las personas y los negocios pagan al gobierno.
sales tax
A tax added to the price of many things you buy at a store.
impuesto sobre las ventas: Un impuesto que se suma al precio de muchas cosas que compras en una tienda.
public service
Something the government provides for everyone, like schools, parks, libraries or firefighters.
servicio público: Algo que el gobierno ofrece para todos, como escuelas, parques, bibliotecas o bomberos.
representation
Having people you choose to speak and vote for you in the government.
representación: Tener personas que tú eliges para que hablen y voten por ti en el gobierno.
colonist
A person who lived in one of the 13 colonies that Great Britain ruled in America.
colono: Una persona que vivía en una de las 13 colonias que Gran Bretaña gobernaba en América.
protest
An action to show that people disagree with something.
protesta: Una acción para mostrar que las personas no están de acuerdo con algo.

Questions

Choose the correct answer. If you make a mistake, the story shows you where the answer is.

Elige la respuesta correcta. Si te equivocas, el cuento te muestra dónde está la respuesta.

  1. AQuestion A. Why does the government collect taxes?

    ¿Por qué el gobierno cobra impuestos?

    The government uses taxes to pay for public services like schools, parks, libraries, firefighters and roads.

  2. BQuestion B. What is one way the government gets money besides sales tax?

    ¿Cuál es una manera en que el gobierno obtiene dinero además del impuesto sobre las ventas?

    Besides taxes on what people buy, earn and own, the government charges fees and sometimes borrows.

  3. CQuestion C. Why were the colonists angry about British taxes?

    ¿Por qué estaban enojados los colonos con los impuestos británicos?

    The colonists had no one they had chosen in the British government: no taxation without representation.

  4. DQuestion D. What happened in Boston in December 1773?

    ¿Qué pasó en Boston en diciembre de 1773?

    In December 1773, colonists threw 342 chests of tea into the harbor: the Boston Tea Party.

  5. EQuestion E. What can people do today if they disagree with a tax?

    ¿Qué pueden hacer hoy las personas si no están de acuerdo con un impuesto?

    Today people vote for their officials, and they can vote for someone else or ask leaders to change the tax.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Follow the money

Choose two public services in your neighborhood. For each one, tell which level of government probably pays for it (city, state or national) and one way that government gets the money.

Sigue el dinero. Elige dos servicios públicos de tu barrio. En cada uno, di qué nivel de gobierno probablemente lo paga (la ciudad, el estado o la nación) y una forma en que ese gobierno consigue el dinero.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Example: the park is paid for by the city's park district, with property taxes and fees. The interstate highway is paid for partly by the national government, with taxes on gasoline and on what people earn. Any answer works if you name the service, the level and a way of paying: taxes, fees or borrowing.

The story

When Great-Grandpa was young, the neighborhood park had a pond full of frogs. "At night they sang so loudly I couldn't sleep," he would say, laughing.

That pond was a : a place with shallow water and tall plants. It was the of frogs, ducks and dragonflies: the place where they found water, food and shelter.

Each living thing can well in some habitats, less well in others, and not at all in some. A frog lives very well in a wetland, but it couldn't live in a desert.

One year, to build a parking lot, the pond was drained and covered with cement. It was a big . Some animals, like the ducks, were able to somewhere else. But many frogs couldn't travel far, and they disappeared from the park.

Not long ago, the neighbors met with the park ranger to look for a . Israel and Lucía went with Adrián. They suggested several ideas and compared them: which one would help the frogs most?

They chose to make a new wetland in one corner of the park and plant plants: plants that grow naturally in Illinois, like rushes and prairie flowers. Native plants give food and shelter to the animals that live here.

They worked for many Saturdays. They dug, filled the pond with rainwater and planted flowers. Adrián planted his very first flower and named it "Little Red."

The next spring, one night, Israel heard a familiar song: ribbit, ribbit! The frogs had come back. Red-winged blackbirds and dragonflies came too.

Great-Grandpa closed his eyes to listen. "Just like when I was a boy," he said. "When we take care of a habitat, living things come back."

Glossary

habitat
The place where a living thing finds everything it needs: water, food and shelter.
wetland
A place with shallow water most of the year, like a pond or a marsh, with lots of plants.
survive
To stay alive in spite of dangers.
change
Something that makes a place different from how it was before.
migrate
To move to live in another place.
solution
A way to solve a problem.
native
Living or growing naturally in a place, without being brought from somewhere else.

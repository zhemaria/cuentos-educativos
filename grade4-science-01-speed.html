The story

On Monday morning, Ms. Patel set a long board, a stack of books and a box of toy cars on the table. "Today we're investigating the energy of motion," she said. "Your challenge is to knock down a tower of paper cups."

Israel, Daniel and Elías teamed up. They propped the board on a single book to make a low ramp. Israel let go of his blue car. It rolled down slowly, tapped the tower and barely nudged one cup. "What a polite car," Elías laughed. "It said excuse me before it crashed."

"Everything that moves has ," Ms. Patel explained. "The faster an object moves, the more energy it has. How can you give your car more ?"

Daniel pushed up his glasses and thought about it. "If the ramp is taller, the car will go faster at the bottom." They slid four books under the board. This time the car zoomed down.

Bam! Cups flew all over the table. The car was moving faster, so it had more energy, and it pushed the cups much farther. One cup landed right next to the class fish tank, and the goldfish opened its mouth like it was shocked.

Then Ms. Patel asked a new question: what happens when a moving car has a with a car that is standing still? Daniel parked his orange car at the bottom of the ramp. Before they let go of the blue car, everyone made a . Elías said the orange car wouldn't budge. Daniel said it would roll away.

Clack! The blue car hit the orange car and almost stopped, and the orange car rolled away. In the collision, energy passed from one car to the other. "Called it!" said Daniel with a big grin.

"But why did it go clack?" Elías asked. "Because in a collision, some of the energy turns into ," Ms. Patel said. "And some turns into a tiny bit of , so little that you can't even feel it."

At the end, the team wrote their conclusion in their notebook: "A faster object has more energy. When it crashes into another object, it can pass some of that energy along and change the other object's ." Elías added in big letters: "If you crash into the fish tank, the experiment is over." Everyone laughed, except the fish.

Glossary

energy
The ability to make something move or change. Everything that moves has energy.
speed
How fast an object moves: how far it goes in a certain amount of time.
collision
When one object crashes into another. In a collision, energy can pass from one object to the other.
prediction
What we think will happen, based on what we already know.
motion
A change in an object's position. An object can start moving, stop or change direction.
sound
Energy that travels through the air as vibrations and that we can hear.
heat
Energy that moves from warmer things to cooler things.

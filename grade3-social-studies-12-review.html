The story

To celebrate the end of the school year, Great-Grandpa invited all the cousins on a boat ride on the Chicago River. Alejandro came from Texas, and even Adrián and Génesis wore sailor caps.

As they passed City Hall, Israel remembered the pothole letter. "That's where the city works," he said. "With taxes, it provides services like schools, parks and firefighters." And the governor works in Springfield, the capital.

There was a sign on the boat: "Do not throw trash in the river." "It's a ," Lucía explained, "a rule the government makes to protect everyone, and if people break it, there are consequences."

They passed a community center. " help there," said Adrián proudly, "like we did at the food bank." Génesis clapped.

Alejandro unfolded a of Illinois. "The Chicago River used to flow into the lake," he said, "but in 1900 people made it run backward with a canal. People modify the environment!"

On the shore, a boat was unloading boxes of fruit. "That fruit comes from other countries," said Israel. "Chicago it. And Illinois exports corn and soybeans all over the world."

"I bought my bike with my savings and a from Grandma," Alejandro said, "and I've paid it all back." The cousins cheered.

At the end, the boat passed near where the Great Fire began. Great-Grandpa pointed to the Chicago flag. "One of those red stars stands for the fire of 1871," he said. "And we know about it from : letters, photos and newspapers from that time. And no, it wasn't the cow's fault!"

Everyone laughed. As the sun set between the skyscrapers, Israel thought that his city had a very long history… and that now they were part of it too.

Glossary

government
The group of people who organize and run a city, a state or a country.
law
A rule made by the government that everyone in a place must follow.
volunteer
A person who helps without being paid.
map
A drawing of a place seen from above that shows where things are.
import
To buy products that were made in another country.
loan
Money or something valuable that someone gives you now and that you promise to pay back later.
primary source
A source made by someone who lived through an event, like a letter, a diary or a photo from that time.

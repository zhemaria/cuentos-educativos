The story

Over spring break, Israel's great-grandpa took Israel, Elías and Selena to central Illinois. "We're going to follow in the footsteps of a very tall man," he said mysteriously. Lincoln stood about six feet four inches tall.

Their first stop was a little village of log cabins by the Sangamon River: New Salem. "Abraham Lincoln was born in Kentucky in 1809," Great-Grandpa said. "His family moved to Indiana and then to Illinois in 1830." The next year, Lincoln came to live right here in New Salem. He was twenty-two years old.

In New Salem, Lincoln worked in a store, ran the post office and surveyed land. At night he studied law from borrowed books. "Borrowed books? Just like me and the library!" said Selena.

The cabins they saw were a , copies built to show what the village looked like back then. A guide in old-fashioned clothes was showing visitors how to make candles. Elías gave it a try and ended up with a candle as crooked as a noodle.

"In 1834, the people here elected Lincoln to the Illinois General Assembly," Great-Grandpa continued. "And in 1837 he moved to Springfield to work as a ."

In Springfield they visited a two-story wooden house painted light brown with green shutters. It was the home of Lincoln and his family, who lived there from 1844 to 1861. It was the only house Lincoln ever owned.

"In 1858, Lincoln had seven with Stephen Douglas in towns all over Illinois," Great-Grandpa explained. "They argued about whether should be allowed to spread into new territories." Lincoln lost that , but people across the country heard his ideas. Two years later, in 1860, he was elected president of the United States.

As president, he led the country through the Civil War and worked to end slavery. Illinois was the first state to approve the to the Constitution that ended slavery across the whole country. After Lincoln died in 1865, he was buried in Springfield.

Before heading home, they passed a car with a license plate that said "Land of Lincoln." "Now I get it," said Elías. "This is where he learned, worked and got his start." "That's his ," said Great-Grandpa, "what he left for the future."

Glossary

reconstruction
A copy of an old building or place, made to show what it used to look like.
lawyer
A person who has studied the law and helps people with court cases.
debate
A discussion in which two people present different ideas about a topic in front of an audience.
slavery
A terrible injustice: forcing people to work without pay or freedom, as if they were someone's property.
election
A time when people vote to choose who will lead or represent them.
amendment
A change or a new part added to a constitution.
legacy
What a person leaves for the future: their ideas, their example or their work.

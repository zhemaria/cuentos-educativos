The story

One Tuesday, the alarm went off at Israel's school. It wasn't a fire: it was a drill. Everyone walked in line to the inner hallway, away from the windows, and crouched down covering their heads.

"Illinois can have tornadoes in spring and summer," Mrs. Kim explained. "A tornado is a column of wind that spins very fast. It is a weather ." That's why we practice where to go.

"No one can stop a storm," said the teacher, "but we can the damage it causes." Today you will be engineers.

Each team chose a hazard and designed a solution. To test their ideas, they built a : a small cardboard house that stood for a real house.

Israel's team thought about tornadoes and strong winds. They gave their little house a tightly tied-down roof and stronger walls, and they built a in the basement: a small, safe room with no windows.

Mei's team thought about lightning. They put a on the roof: a metal bar that carries the lightning's electricity down into the ground so it won't damage the house.

Jamal's team thought about , like the ones that sometimes happen in Texas, where Alejandro lives. They put their little house on stilts, higher than the water, and surrounded it with a wall of sandbags.

Then came the test: a fan for the wind and a tray of water for the flood. Israel's house lost a window, so the team improved it with a shutter and tested it again. This time it held up!

That night, Israel told his family all about it. Together they checked the weather radio and packed a backpack with a flashlight, water and batteries. "Now we're ready," said Israel, "even though I hope we never need it."

Glossary

hazard
Something that can hurt people or damage things.
tornado
A column of wind that spins very fast and reaches from a storm cloud down to the ground.
flood
When water covers a place that is usually dry, because of heavy rain or a river overflowing.
lightning rod
A metal bar on top of a building that carries the electricity from lightning down into the ground.
shelter
A safe place to protect yourself from danger.
model
A small copy of something that is used to study it or test it.
reduce
To make something smaller or less strong.

On Monday, the science lab looked different. Twelve stood on the tables like a row of shiny robots. "Today," said Ms. Rivera, "you're going to see something nobody saw until about 360 years ago."

She held up a picture of an old drawing. "In 1665, a scientist named Robert Hooke looked at a thin slice of cork through a microscope. He saw tiny boxes, like the rooms in a building, and he called them ." "Cork is just tree bark," said Israel. "Exactly," said Ms. Rivera. "Let's see what else is made of cells."

Israel peeled a paper-thin layer from an onion with tweezers. Ms. Rivera added a drop of purple stain, and Sophia looked first. "Whoa," she whispered. "It looks like a brick wall. The cells are shaped like rectangles, and each one has a dark dot." "The dot is the ," said Ms. Rivera. "It controls what the cell does."

"Why are they shaped like bricks?" asked Selena. "Plant cells have a stiff around them," said Ms. Rivera. "It helps plants stand up straight without bones."

Next, Selena put a drop of water from the park pond onto a slide. Everyone crowded around. Something tiny and oval zoomed across the circle of light. "That's a whole living thing made of just one cell," said Ms. Rivera. "It's . It finds food, moves and makes copies of itself, all inside one cell."

Then she showed them a slide of human skin cells. They were rounder, with no stiff wall, but each one had a nucleus too. "You are ," she said. "Your body has trillions of cells, and there are many types: skin cells, muscle cells, nerve cells and blood cells, each with a different job."

Sophia wrote in her notebook: Onion: many cells with walls. Pond creature: one cell. Me: trillions of cells. "So everything alive is made of cells?" she asked. "Every living thing we have ever found," said Ms. Rivera. "That's one of the most important ideas in biology."

At the end of class, Israel looked at his hand for a long time. "What are you doing?" asked Selena. "Saying hello," said Israel, "to trillions of my closest friends."

Glossary

cell
The smallest unit of life. All living things are made of one or more cells.
célula: La unidad más pequeña de la vida. Todos los seres vivos están formados por una o más células.
microscope
A tool with lenses that makes tiny things look much bigger.
microscopio: Un instrumento con lentes que hace que las cosas diminutas se vean mucho más grandes.
single-celled
Made of only one cell, like the tiny living things in pond water.
unicelular: Formado por una sola célula, como los seres diminutos del agua de un estanque.
multicellular
Made of many cells, like a person, a dog or an oak tree.
multicelular: Formado por muchas células, como una persona, un perro o un roble.
cell wall
A stiff layer around plant cells that helps them keep their shape.
pared celular: Una capa rígida alrededor de las células de las plantas que les ayuda a mantener su forma.
nucleus
The part of a cell that controls what the cell does.
núcleo: La parte de la célula que controla lo que hace la célula.

Questions

  1. AQuestion A. What did Robert Hooke see when he looked at cork?

    ¿Qué vio Robert Hooke cuando miró el corcho?

    In 1665, Robert Hooke saw tiny boxes in a slice of cork, like rooms in a building, and he called them cells.

  2. BQuestion B. What did the onion cells look like?

    ¿Cómo se veían las células de la cebolla?

    The onion cells looked like a brick wall: rectangles, each with a dark dot, the nucleus.

  3. CQuestion C. Why are plant cells shaped like bricks?

    ¿Por qué las células de las plantas tienen forma de ladrillo?

    Plant cells have a stiff cell wall that helps them keep their shape and helps plants stand up.

  4. DQuestion D. What was special about the creature in the pond water?

    ¿Qué tenía de especial el ser que estaba en el agua del estanque?

    It was single-celled: a whole living thing that finds food, moves and reproduces inside just one cell.

  5. EQuestion E. What does it mean that people are multicellular?

    ¿Qué significa que las personas son multicelulares?

    The human body has trillions of cells of many types, like skin, muscle, nerve and blood cells.

Check your thinking

Put a very thin slice of tomato on a slide, add a drop of water or stain, and look at it under a microscope. You would see many cells with walls and nuclei, packed together. That evidence shows the tomato is made of cells, because all living things, and parts of living things, are made of cells, even soft and juicy ones.

The story

In June, Selena rode along with Israel and his mom to visit relatives on a farm in Illinois. Out the car window there was nothing but cornfields... until giant white windmills appeared, with huge blades turning slowly. "They look like fans for giants!" said Selena.

"It's the other way around," laughed Israel's mom. "They don't make wind. They use the wind to make electricity. Wind is a . It doesn't run out, and when the blades spin, the wind's energy turns into electricity."

That afternoon, Uncle Beto told them there are coal mines in southern Illinois. "Coal is a that some power plants burn to make electricity," he explained. "But it's . It takes millions of years to form, and someday it could run out."

"So why don't we just use wind?" Israel asked. "Every resource has good points and problems," said his uncle. "Burning coal makes the air dirty and changes the . Wind turbines and solar panels don't dirty the air, but they only work when there's wind or sun." That's why we use several resources, and more and more renewable ones.

That night, the sky turned dark green and the wind started howling. The weather radio beeped: there was a warning for the county. Selena got scared, but Aunt Lupe knew exactly what to do.

Everyone went down to the basement, the lowest part of the house, away from the windows. They kept an emergency kit down there: a flashlight, batteries, water, a first aid kit and a radio. They sat together on the rug. Outside, the wind roared like a train. Israel held Selena's hand.

After a while, the radio said the danger had passed. Luckily, the tornado never reached the house, but it rained buckets. By the next morning, the creek had overflowed and there was a across the road. "Never walk or drive through floodwater," said Aunt Lupe. "It can be deeper and faster than it looks." They waited at home until the water went down.

"We can't stop tornadoes or rain," said Uncle Beto, "but we can protect ourselves." He told Selena about sirens that warn people about tornadoes, houses with safe rooms and walls of earth called levees along rivers to hold back the water. And in Chicago there are enormous tunnels underground that store storm water so the streets don't flood.

On the way back to Chicago, Selena saw the windmills spinning in the sunshine again. "Yesterday the wind was scary," she said. "Today it's working for us."

Glossary

natural resource
Something from nature that people use, like water, wind, coal or sunlight.
renewable resource
A resource that doesn't run out or that nature replaces quickly, like wind and sunlight.
nonrenewable resource
A resource that takes millions of years to form and can run out, like coal and oil.
fuel
A material that is burned to get energy, like coal, oil or gas.
environment
Everything around us: the air, water, land and living things.
tornado
A column of very fast spinning wind that reaches down from a storm cloud to the ground.
flood
When water covers places that are usually dry.

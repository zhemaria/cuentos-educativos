The story

For Family Week, Ms. Rivera hung a world map on the wall and handed out colorful strings. "Each of you will interview someone in your family," she said. "Then we'll connect the place they came from to Chicago with a string."

"A person who leaves their country to live in another country is an ," Ms. Rivera explained. "And when someone moves to another part of their own country, we say they ."

Daniel interviewed his great-grandmother, who is ninety-two and tells the best stories in the family. She came to Chicago by train from Mississippi when she was a little girl. Her family was part of the : between 1916 and 1970, about six million African Americans moved from the South to cities in the North and West.

"Why did you move?" Daniel asked her. "We were looking for jobs in the factories, and we wanted to get away from unfair laws that kept people apart because of the color of their skin," she told him. "There was unfairness in the North too, but there were more ."

His great-grandmother also told him that people brought their music with them. Blues and gospel filled the clubs and churches of Chicago's South Side. She still sings when she cooks, and Daniel says nobody on the whole block sings louder.

Elías interviewed his grandmother, who came from Puerto Rico many years ago. "My grandma wasn't an immigrant," Elías told the class. "Puerto Ricans are of the United States, so she migrated within her own country."

Sophia's grandparents came from South Korea, and Israel's great-grandpa came from Latin America. Ms. Rivera explained that for almost two hundred years, people have come to Illinois from Ireland, Germany, Poland, Sweden, Italy, Mexico, China, India and many more places. Each group brought its food, its language and its , and helped build the state.

By the end, the map looked like a colorful spiderweb. Every string led to the same spot: Chicago. "We come from lots of places," said Israel, "but now we share the same city." "That makes Illinois strong," said Ms. Rivera.

On the day of the fair, Daniel's great-grandmother came to school with sweet potato pie for everyone. It was the sweetest string on the map.

Glossary

immigrant
A person who leaves their country to live in another one.
migrate
To move from one place to another to live, within the same country or to a different one.
Great Migration
The move of millions of African Americans from the South to cities in the North and West between 1916 and 1970.
opportunity
A good chance to do something, like getting a job or going to school.
citizen
A person who belongs to a country, with rights and responsibilities.
custom
Something a group of people always does, like a special food, a holiday or a way of greeting.
diversity
When a place has people of many backgrounds, cultures and ideas.

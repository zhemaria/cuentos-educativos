The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Mr. Mensah held up a picture of a moth with white wings sprinkled with black dots, like pepper on salt. "This is a peppered moth," he said. "For hundreds of years in England, almost all of them looked like this. They rest on tree trunks during the day, on pale bark covered with lichens. Can you find the one in this picture?" It took the class almost a minute.

"In 1848, in Manchester, a collector found a dark peppered moth," said Mr. Mensah. "By 1895, about 98 percent of the peppered moths around Manchester were dark." "Ninety-eight percent!" said Israel. "What happened?"

"Factories," said Mr. Mensah. "Manchester was full of coal-burning factories. Their killed the pale lichens and covered the tree trunks with black soot." He showed a second picture: a dark tree trunk, and on it, a white moth that stood out like a light bulb.

Then they played a game. Mr. Mensah taped 20 white paper moths and 20 dark ones on two big pictures of bark: one pale, one black with soot. Elías and Selena were the hungry birds. On the pale bark, the birds found the dark moths quickly and missed many white ones. On the sooty bark, it was the opposite: the white moths were eaten first.

"Birds are the ," said Mr. Mensah. "On sooty trees, dark moths had better , so more of them survived and laid eggs. The dark color is a that is passed from parents to offspring, so each generation had a higher percentage of dark moths. That's ."

"Did the moths decide to turn dark?" asked Daniel. "No," said Mr. Mensah. "No single moth changed color. The changed, because some moths survived more than others."

"Then, in the 1950s, Britain passed laws for cleaner air," he said. "The soot slowly washed off, the lichens grew back, and the pale moths became common again." Salma drew the graph: the percentage of dark moths climbed for years, then came back down.

Athena looked at the graph. "So the environment changed twice, and the moths changed twice." Sophia wrote in her notebook: Nature doesn't pick the best moth. It picks the moth that fits.

Glossary

trait
A feature of a living thing, like the color of a moth's wings, that can be passed from parents to offspring.
rasgo: Una característica de un ser vivo, como el color de las alas de una polilla, que puede pasar de los padres a la cría.
population
All the members of one species living in one area.
población: Todos los individuos de una especie que viven en un lugar.
predator
An animal that hunts and eats other animals.
depredador: Un animal que caza y come a otros animales.
camouflage
Colors or patterns that help a living thing blend into its surroundings.
camuflaje: Colores o dibujos que ayudan a un ser vivo a confundirse con lo que lo rodea.
natural selection
The process in which living things with helpful traits survive and reproduce more, so those traits become more common over generations.
selección natural: El proceso por el que los seres vivos con rasgos útiles sobreviven y se reproducen más, de modo que esos rasgos se vuelven más comunes con las generaciones.
pollution
Harmful things, like smoke and soot, that get into the air, water or soil.
contaminación: Cosas dañinas, como el humo y el hollín, que llegan al aire, al agua o al suelo.

  1. AQuestion A. What percentage of peppered moths around Manchester were dark by 1895?

  2. BQuestion B. How did the factories change the trees?

    Coal-burning factories killed the lichens and blackened the tree trunks with soot.

  3. CQuestion C. In the game, which moths were eaten first on the sooty bark?

    En el juego, ¿qué polillas se comieron primero en la corteza con hollín?

    On the black bark, the white moths stood out, so the birds found them first.

  4. DQuestion D. Why did the percentage of dark moths go up?

    ¿Por qué subió el porcentaje de polillas oscuras?

    ¿Qué pasó después de que el Reino Unido aprobó leyes para limpiar el aire?

    The soot washed off, the lichens grew back, and pale moths had the better camouflage again.

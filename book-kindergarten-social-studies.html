Chapter 1: Our classroom rules

Why we have rules

Kindergarten · Social studies · SS.K.CV.2

A story about the rules that keep us safe at school.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • Something we all must do to stay safe and get along.

  • To let someone else use or play with something of yours.

  • A job that is yours to do, and others count on you to do it.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

It is the first day of kindergarten. Adrián runs into the classroom with his dinosaur.

Bump! He crashes into a table, and Génesis's crayons fall on the floor.

Ms. Luna helps them pick them up. "Let's make our classroom rules," she says.

"A rule tells us how to act so we can stay safe," she explains.

Rule number one: inside the classroom, we walk. That way nobody gets hurt.

Rule number two: we raise our hand to talk. That way we can all listen.

Rule number three: we share the toys. That way everyone can play.

Then Ms. Luna gives each child a job.

Adrián waters the plant. Génesis hands out the papers. It is their responsibility.

Now Adrián walks slowly. And so does his dinosaur!

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. What is a rule for?

    A rule tells us how to act so we can stay safe.

  2. BQuestion B. Why do we share the toys?

    We share the toys so everyone can play.

  3. CQuestion C. What is Adrián's responsibility?

    Adrián waters the plant. It is his job in the classroom.

Illinois Social Science standard SS.K.CV.2: identify the reasons for rules and explain how they set up responsibilities and roles at school, at home and in the community.

Rules outside of school

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • The light on the street that tells us when to stop and when to cross.

  • The time when it is your chance, after you wait.

  • A place with lots of books we can read and borrow.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

On Saturday, Grandma takes Adrián and Génesis to the park and to the library.

To get there, they have to cross the street.

"We wait until the traffic light shows the little green person," says Grandma.

They hold hands, look both ways and cross together.

At the park, lots of children want to use the slide.

"We stand in line and wait for our turn," says Génesis.

Then they go into the library. There are books everywhere!

"Here we talk quietly, so everyone can read," Grandma whispers.

Adrián picks a book about dinosaurs. Of course!

At school, on the street and at the library, rules keep all of us safe.

The story

Every morning, Adrián and Génesis walk to school.

On the corner is Mrs. Rosa, the crossing guard. She holds up his stop sign, and the cars stop.

"Thank you, Mrs. Rosa!" the children say, and they cross safely.

At the door, Mr. Morales is waiting. She is the school principal.

He makes sure the school is a safe place where everyone can learn.

Today there is a special visit: a red truck comes to the school!

A firefighter climbs down with her helmet. Her name is Carmen.

"My job is to put out fires and help people who are in danger," she says.

Adrián tries on the helmet. It is way too big!

Everyone has a job, and together we take care of our community.

Chapter 4: A name for the fish

Deciding together by voting

Kindergarten · Social studies · SS.K-2.IS.7

A story about how a class decides something all together.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • To pick one choice so a group of people can decide something.

  • To say numbers in order to find out how many there are.

  • The choice that more people pick.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

A little orange fish came to the classroom. But it has no name!

Everyone wants to choose the name. How will they decide?

"Let's vote!" says Ms. Luna. "Each of you picks the name you like best."

There are three ideas: Bubbles, Sunny and Rex.

Adrián wants Rex, like a dinosaur. Génesis wants Bubbles.

Each child puts a sticker under the name they choose.

Then Ms. Luna counts the stickers with everyone.

Bubbles has the most stickers. It is the majority! The fish will be called Bubbles.

Adrián smiles. "That's okay. We decided all together."

And Bubbles swims a happy circle in the fishbowl.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. How did they decide the fish's name?

    They decided by voting: each child picked the name they liked best.

  2. BQuestion B. How did each child vote?

    Each child put a sticker under the name they chose.

  3. CQuestion C. Why is the fish called Bubbles?

    Bubbles had the most stickers: the majority picked it.

Illinois Social Science standard SS.K-2.IS.7: talk about different points of view to draw conclusions and decide on next steps.

Chapter 5: One dollar to choose

We can't have everything

Kindergarten · Social studies · SS.K.EC.1

A story about choosing when we can't buy everything.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • Paper money we use to buy things.

  • To decide which thing you want when you can't have them all.

  • To keep money so you can buy something later.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Grandma gives Adrián a dollar, and she gives Génesis one too.

"You can each buy one thing at the store," she says.

Adrián sees dinosaur stickers. And he sees ice cream too!

But one dollar is only enough for one thing. He can't buy both!

Adrián thinks and thinks. He has to choose.

He chooses the stickers, because they will last longer than an ice cream.

Génesis wants a ball and some crayons. She doesn't have enough for both either.

"Today I'll buy the crayons, and I'll save up for the ball," says Génesis.

When they get home, Génesis puts some coins in her piggy bank.

We can't have everything. That's why we choose what we want most.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. Why couldn't Adrián buy the stickers and the ice cream?

    One dollar was only enough for one thing, so he had to choose.

  2. BQuestion B. What did Adrián choose?

    Adrián chose the stickers, because they will last longer than an ice cream.

  3. CQuestion C. What will Génesis do to get the ball?

    Génesis bought the crayons, and she will save up for the ball.

Illinois Social Science standard SS.K.EC.1: explain that people make choices because of scarcity: they cannot have everything they want.

The story

In Chicago, winter is very cold. Today it is snowing.

Adrián and Génesis put on coats, hats and mittens to go play in the snow.

But in the summer, they wear shorts and go to the pool.

The weather changes what we do and what we wear.

When they come back in, they eat a red apple. "Where does it come from?" asks Adrián.

Grandma draws a map on a piece of paper.

Apples grow in an orchard, far away from the city.

A truck carries them down the road to the store.

On the map, a dotted line shows the way.

That's how apples travel from the orchard to our table.

Three new words

  • At this moment, today.

  • Everything that happened a long time ago.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Grandpa takes out a very old photo album.

"This is me when I was five, just like you," he says.

In the photo, Grandpa is playing marbles on the street.

"Long ago there were no tablets. We played with marbles, spinning tops and balls."

"And how did you talk to your grandma?" asks Génesis.

"With a telephone that had a cord and a little wheel to dial," Grandpa laughs.

Now Adrián talks to his aunt on the tablet, and he can even see her!

Some things changed. Others are the same: children still love to play ball.

Grandpa's photos and stories teach us about the past.

That afternoon, Grandpa teaches them how to play marbles.

A story about three holidays in our country and what we celebrate.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • A special day when we celebrate or remember something important.

  • To say we are happy for what we have.

  • To think about something or someone from the past.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

There is a big calendar in the classroom. Some days have special drawings.

"These are our country's holidays," says Ms. Luna.

In November comes Thanksgiving. Families eat together and give thanks for what they have.

In January, we remember Martin Luther King Jr.

He worked so that all people would be treated fairly, no matter the color of their skin.

"Just like in our class, where we all play together!" says Génesis.

The Fourth of July is our country's birthday. At night there are fireworks.

Adrián draws red fireworks. Génesis draws pink ones.

On holidays, we remember important people and times.

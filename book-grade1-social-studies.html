Chapter 1: The library rules

Rules inside and outside of school

Grade 1 · Social studies · SS.1.CV.2

A story about the rules in different places.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • Something we all agree to do so everyone is OK.

  • A place where you can borrow books.

  • Out of danger.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Lucía and Génesis’s class went on a trip to the library.

On the bus, the teacher said, “We stay in our seats with our seat belts on.”

“That rule keeps us safe,” said Lucía.

In the library there was a sign: “Use a quiet voice.”

“Here we talk softly so everyone can read,” Génesis whispered.

The girls chose books and handled them with care.

Then they went to the park to eat.

There was another rule there: “Put trash in the trash can.”

Génesis threw her sandwich wrapper in the trash can.

On the way back, Lucía thought that every place has its own rules.

“We can shout in the park, but not in the library,” she laughed.

Rules help us stay safe and get along in every place.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. Why do we have the bus rule?

    Staying seated with our seat belts on keeps us safe.

  2. BQuestion B. Why do people talk softly in the library?

    Talking softly lets other people read.

  3. CQuestion C. What did Génesis do with her wrapper at the park?

    She followed the park rule: trash goes in the trash can.

Illinois Social Science standard SS.1.CV.2: identify and explain how rules function in various settings, inside and outside of the school.

Chapter 3: The treasure map

Reading and making maps

Grade 1 · Social studies · SS.1.G.1

A story about using a map to find a place.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • A drawing of a place seen from above.

  • A little drawing that stands for something on a map.

  • The list that tells what each symbol means.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

The teacher hid a treasure in the classroom and gave them a map.

The map was a drawing of the classroom seen from above, the way a bird would see it.

Each table was a square, and the rug was a circle. They were symbols.

In one corner was the map key: it told what each symbol meant.

A red X marked the treasure.

Génesis found the door on the map. “We start here!”

Following the arrow, they walked toward the window.

Adrián turned when they got to the bookshelf.

Next to the plant, under the rug, there was the X!

The treasure was a box of star stickers.

Later, each child drew a map of their bedroom with their own map key.

A map helps us know where things are and how to get to a place.

  2. BQuestion B. Where did they walk, following the arrow?

  3. CQuestion C. Where was the treasure?

    The X was next to the plant, under the rug.

Chapter 4: Israel's dollar

Choosing one thing and giving up another

Grade 1 · Social studies · SS.1.EC.1

A story about choosing when we can’t have everything.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • To pick one thing from several.

  • What you pick after you think.

  • To keep money for later.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Grandma gave Israel one dollar.

At the store, Israel saw a strawberry lollipop and a dinosaur pencil.

Each one cost a dollar. He could only choose one!

“If I buy the lollipop, I can’t buy the pencil,” he thought.

Lucía said, “Think about which one is more useful.”

A lollipop is gone in a little while. A pencil lasts a long time.

Israel made his decision: he bought the pencil.

The lollipop stayed at the store. That was what he gave up.

Days later, Israel wanted a ball that cost five dollars.

This time he decided to save his money in a piggy bank.

In the meantime, he used his pencil to draw a dinosaur for Adrián.

When we choose one thing, we give up another. That’s why we think before we decide.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. Why could Israel choose only one thing?

    Each one cost a dollar, and he had only one.

  2. BQuestion B. What did Israel buy?

    He chose the pencil and gave up the lollipop.

  3. CQuestion C. What did he do to buy the ball?

    He kept his money little by little in the piggy bank.

Illinois Social Science standard SS.1.EC.1: explain and give examples of when choices are made that something else is given up.

A story about what you need to know to make bread and to cut hair.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • Things that are made and can be bought, like bread.

  • Work someone does to help you, like cutting hair.

  • Something you do well because you learned and practiced it.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Génesis and Lucía visited Mrs. Carmen’s bakery.

She gets up very early to make bread.

She knows how to measure flour, knead dough and use the oven without getting burned.

“I learned from my grandmother, and I practiced for many years,” she told them.

Making bread is her skill.

Bread is one of the goods: things that are made and bought.

Next door was Mr. Luis’s barbershop.

Mr. Luis cut a boy’s hair very carefully.

Cutting hair is a service: work that helps another person.

“To cut hair, you have to know how to use scissors and a comb,” said Mr. Luis.

Génesis bought sweet bread for her whole family.

To make goods and provide services, people learn and practice.

    To cut hair, you need to know how to use scissors and a comb.

Chapter 7: Génesis's photo album

Putting events in order

Grade 1 · Social studies · SS.1.H.1

A story about how to make a timeline.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • At a time that already happened.

  • At a time that comes later.

  • A row of events in order, from first to last.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Génesis found a photo album with her mom.

In one photo, Génesis was a baby in a pink blanket.

In another, she was taking her first steps.

In another, she was blowing out three candles on her cake.

And in another, she was going to her first day of first grade with her teddy bear.

The photos were all mixed up, so Génesis put them in order.

First she put the baby photo, because it happened before everything else.

After that she put the first steps, then the candles, and last, school.

That is how she made a timeline.

Adrián brought a photo of himself as a baby, hugging his green dinosaur.

“I was born after you,” he told Génesis. “My baby photo goes later on the timeline.”

A timeline puts things in order, from the first one to the last one.

Questions

  1. AQuestion A. Which photo did Génesis put first?

    The baby photo goes first, because it happened before everything else.

  2. BQuestion B. Which photo did she put last?

    School happened last, so it goes at the end.

  3. CQuestion C. Why does Adrián’s baby photo go later?

    Adrián was born later, so his photo goes later on the timeline.

Chapter 8: When Grandpa was a boy

The past and the present

Grade 1 · Social studies · SS.1.H.3

A story about how things were then and how they are now.

Open this story alone

Three new words

  • The time that already happened.

  • The time that is now.

  • Something that happened that you still keep in your mind.

Tap a picture to hear the word and what it means.

The story

Lucía’s grandpa showed Lucía and Israel a box of memories.

Inside there was a spinning top, some marbles and a black-and-white photo.

“In the past, when I was a boy, there were no tablets,” said Grandpa.

“We played with tops and marbles in the street.”

“To talk with my aunt, I wrote her letters, and they took many days to arrive.”

Israel thought about the present.

“Now I talk with my cousin by video, and I see him right away!”

Lucía compared the old photo with a photo on her tablet.

“Long ago, many photos had no colors,” she said.

But Grandpa also had things just like theirs: friends, games and a school.

That afternoon, the three of them played marbles in the backyard.

Things change over time, but children long ago and children today are alike in many ways.

Questions

Listen to the question and tap the right picture.

  1. AQuestion A. What did Grandpa play with when he was a boy?

    As a boy, Grandpa played with tops and marbles.

  2. BQuestion B. How did Grandpa talk with his aunt?

    He wrote her letters that took many days.

  3. CQuestion C. How does Israel talk with his cousin now?

    In the present, Israel talks with his cousin by video.

Illinois Social Science standard SS.1.H.3: compare perspectives of people in the past to those of people in the present.

The duck that wouldn't come back

A story about waves and their patterns.

The story

In July, Sophia, Selena and Israel went to a Lake Michigan beach with their families. Selena's little cousin tossed his yellow rubber duck into the water. "Don't worry, it'll come right back," said Israel. "The waves will carry it to shore."

But the duck didn't come back. It went up and down, up and down... and stayed almost in the same spot. "Look," said Sophia. "Each passes under the duck and lifts it, but the water doesn't travel along with it. What travels is the energy."

"Then how do the waves get to the beach?" Selena asked. "Let's make a ," said Sophia, and she pulled a jump rope out of her backpack.

Selena held one end and Sophia held the other. They stretched the rope out on the sand, and Sophia shook her end up and down. Waves rippled along the rope, one after another, all the way to Selena's hands. "That tickles!" she giggled.

Israel drew the waves in the sand with a stick. "This top part is the ," he said, pointing. "And this low part is the ." Then he drew a straight line through the middle, like the water when it's calm.

Sophia shook the rope harder. The waves got much taller. "That's the ," said Israel. "It's how tall the wave is, from the middle line up to the crest. If you shake with more energy, the amplitude gets bigger."

Next, Sophia shook the rope faster, but just as hard as before. Now the waves came out closer together. "The changed," Selena wrote down. "That's the distance from one crest to the next. When you shake faster, the crests get closer together."

They looked out at the lake again. The waves rolled in one after another, the same distance apart. "It's a ," said Israel. "Waves repeat the same way, so we can measure them and describe them."

Just then, a big wave with lots of amplitude lifted the duck and nudged it a little toward the beach. Waves can make things move, even though the water itself mostly stays in place. Another wave came, and another, until the duck washed up on shore. Selena's cousin scooped it up. "It swam back!" he cheered. Nobody had the heart to explain amplitude to him.

Glossary

wave
A repeating movement that carries energy from one place to another, like ripples on water.
crest
The highest part of a wave.
trough
The lowest part of a wave.
amplitude
How tall a wave is: the distance from the level of calm water up to the crest.
wavelength
The distance from one crest to the next crest.
pattern
Something that repeats the same way again and again.
model
Something that represents an idea or object and helps us understand what is hard to see or study.

The story

For his birthday, Israel got ten dollars from his grandma. "How exciting!" he said, and he ran with Lucía to the corner store.

At the store, there were candies, balloons, and little toys. Israel wanted to all his money right away. To spend is to use money to buy something.

Then he saw a soccer ball in the window, just like the one at recess. It cost twenty dollars. "I do not have enough," he said sadly. "I only have ten."

Lucía had an idea. "You can ," she said. "To save is to keep money now so you can use it later." "If you keep your ten dollars and collect ten more, you can buy the ball."

Israel thought about it for a while. The ball was his , something he wanted to reach. The candies were only a : something he liked but did not need. He decided not to spend.

At home, Israel put his money in a , a little box with a slot for coins and bills. Every week, he helped his mom water the plants, and he put the dollar she gave him into the piggy bank.

One day, his mom explained the difference between a want and a . "Food, clothes, and a home are needs: we need them to live. Candies and toys are wants."

After ten weeks, Israel opened the piggy bank and counted the money: twenty dollars! He went to the store and bought the soccer ball. "It was worth the wait," he said. And the next day he took it to school, so there would be two balls at recess.

Glossary

spend
To use money to buy something.
save
To keep money now so you can use it later.
goal
Something a person wants to reach and works toward.
want
Something we like to have but do not need to live, like candy or toys.
piggy bank
A little box or container with a slot for keeping coins and bills.
need
Something we need to live, like food, clothes, or a home.

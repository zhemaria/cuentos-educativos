The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Ms. Novak unrolled a copy of an old map with straight streets and wide diagonal avenues. "This is a plan of the city of Washington from 1792," she said. "But before anyone could draw the streets, someone had to measure where the federal district began and ended. One of the people who helped was Benjamin Banneker."

"Banneker was a free African American born in Maryland in 1731," she said. "He spent most of his life on his family's farm, and he taught himself mathematics and astronomy with borrowed books and instruments." "Self-taught?" said Daniel. "Completely," said Ms. Novak.

"In 1791, Major Andrew Ellicott was hired to survey the borders of the new federal district, a square ten miles on each side," she said. "He chose Banneker as an assistant. At night, Banneker used the stars to help find exact positions, because a needed the sky to know where he stood on the Earth."

After the survey, Banneker finished his own , with the positions of the Sun, the Moon and the planets for a whole year, all calculated by hand. "On August 19, 1791, he sent a copy to Thomas Jefferson, the Secretary of State and the author of the Declaration of Independence," said Ms. Novak. "With it, he wrote a long letter. He reminded Jefferson that the Declaration said that all men are created equal, and he pointed out the of holding people in slavery."

"What did Jefferson say?" asked Salma. "He answered on August 30, politely, and thanked Banneker for the almanac," said Ms. Novak. "The next year, both letters were printed together in Philadelphia, so many more people could read them."

Then she gave each pair a copy of Banneker's letter, a , and three questions to write on the top: Who wrote it? Why? For whom? "The author is Banneker, a free Black scientist," said Selena. "His was to persuade Jefferson that Black people were equal in ability, and his almanac was the proof. His first was Jefferson, but when the letters were printed, the audience became the whole country."

"So asking who, why and for whom changes how you read it," said Israel. "Exactly," said Ms. Novak. "Every source has a point of view. Knowing it doesn't make the source less valuable. It helps you understand it."

Before the bell, Elías looked again at the map of Washington. "The capital was measured with the help of a man who couldn't vote and who had to remind the government what it had promised," he said. Sophia wrote in her notebook: A letter can be a calculation, too. Banneker proved his point with numbers.

The straight streets and wide avenues show a planned capital, orderly and grand, made to impress. The map was made by the planners and surveyors, to guide the building of the city and to show the government and the public what the capital would become. Like a letter, a map shows what its makers valued.

Glossary

surveyor
A person who measures land and marks its exact borders, often using the stars and special instruments.
agrimensor: Una persona que mide terrenos y marca sus límites exactos, a menudo con ayuda de las estrellas e instrumentos especiales.
almanac
A yearly book with calendars, tides, the positions of the Sun, the Moon and the stars, and weather predictions.
almanaque: Un libro anual con calendarios, mareas, posiciones del Sol, la Luna y las estrellas, y predicciones del tiempo.
primary source
A document or object made by someone who lived at the time, like a letter, a diary or a map.
fuente primaria: Un documento u objeto hecho por alguien que vivió en esa época, como una carta, un diario o un mapa.
audience
The people a writer wants to reach.
público (destinatario): Las personas a las que un autor quiere llegar.
purpose
The reason why an author creates a source: to inform, to persuade, to remember, and so on.
propósito: La razón por la que un autor crea una fuente: informar, convencer, recordar, etc.
contradiction
When two ideas or actions don't fit together, like defending liberty while keeping people enslaved.
contradicción: Cuando dos ideas o acciones no encajan entre sí, como defender la libertad y al mismo tiempo mantener a personas esclavizadas.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Analyze a source

Imagine you find Jefferson's polite reply to Banneker. Write three questions a historian should ask about it: one about the author, one about the purpose and one about the audience. Then explain why a polite answer might not tell us everything the author thought.

Analiza una fuente. Imagina que encuentras la respuesta amable de Jefferson a Banneker. Escribe tres preguntas que debería hacerse un historiador: una sobre el autor, una sobre el propósito y una sobre el público. Después explica por qué una respuesta amable podría no decirnos todo lo que pensaba su autor.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Author: Jefferson was the Secretary of State and owned enslaved people; how might that shape what he wrote? Purpose: was he trying to be polite, to avoid an argument, or to share his true opinion? Audience: did he write only for Banneker, or did he think others might read it? A polite letter may hide disagreement or doubts, so historians compare it with other sources, like the author's private writings, to understand the whole picture.

Go further

Banneker's almanacs were printed for several years. Find out what kinds of information an almanac of that time included and why farmers and sailors needed them.

Para ir más lejos. Los almanaques de Banneker se imprimieron durante varios años. Averigua qué tipo de información traía un almanaque de esa época y por qué lo necesitaban los agricultores y los marinos.

Check your thinking

Almanacs included calendars, the times of sunrise and sunset, the phases of the Moon, eclipses, tide tables and weather predictions. Farmers used them to plan planting and harvests, and sailors used the tides and the positions of the stars to navigate. Making one required many careful calculations.

The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

"Last night the moon was a skinny crescent," said Daniel. "Three weeks ago it was full. Does part of it break off?" Ms. Rivera smiled, closed the blinds and turned off the lights. In the middle of the room, she switched on a bare lamp. "This is the sun," she said. "Athena, you're Earth."

She gave Athena a white ball on a stick. "Hold the moon out in front of you and turn slowly in a circle. Your head is Earth, and the ball is the moon traveling in its ." Athena turned. "The ball is always half lit by the lamp," Daniel noticed. "But the part of the lit half that Athena can see keeps changing."

"That's the secret of the ," said Ms. Rivera. "The moon doesn't make its own light. It reflects sunlight. As it goes around Earth, we see more or less of its sunny side: new moon, crescent, quarter, gibbous, full and back again, in about a month."

When the ball was exactly between Athena's head and the lamp, a small round shadow fell on Athena's face. "That's a ," said Ms. Rivera. "The moon blocks the sun. On April 8, 2024, the moon covered the sun completely in southern Illinois, and in Chicago most of the sun was covered." "My grandma watched it with special glasses," said Daniel.

Then Athena turned so that her head was between the lamp and the ball. The ball went dark. "Now Earth's shadow is falling on the moon," said Ms. Rivera. "That's a . It can only happen at full moon. During a total lunar eclipse, the moon often looks reddish, because a little sunlight bends through Earth's air and reaches it."

"If the moon goes around us every month," asked Daniel, "why don't we have eclipses every month?" "Because the moon's orbit is a little tilted," said Ms. Rivera. "Most months, the moon passes a little above or below the shadow."

Next, she picked up a globe and walked it slowly around the lamp. The globe's always leaned the same way. "Earth's causes the seasons," she said. "In June, the northern half leans toward the sun, so the sunlight hits Chicago more directly and the days are longer. In December, it leans away, so we get less direct light and colder weather."

"So it's not because Earth gets closer to the sun?" asked Daniel. "Nope. We're actually a bit closer to the sun in January!" Ms. Rivera turned the lights back on. Daniel blinked. "I came in thinking the moon was breaking," he said. "Now I know it's just playing hide-and-seek with the sunlight."

Glossary

orbit
The curved path an object follows as it travels around another object, like the moon around Earth.
órbita: El camino curvo que sigue un objeto cuando gira alrededor de otro, como la Luna alrededor de la Tierra.
phase
The shape of the lit part of the moon that we see from Earth, like a crescent or a full moon.
fase: La forma de la parte iluminada de la Luna que vemos desde la Tierra, como la luna en forma de hoz o la luna llena.
solar eclipse
When the moon passes between the sun and Earth and blocks the sun's light.
eclipse solar: Cuando la Luna pasa entre el Sol y la Tierra y tapa la luz del Sol.
lunar eclipse
When Earth passes between the sun and the moon, and Earth's shadow falls on the moon.
eclipse lunar: Cuando la Tierra pasa entre el Sol y la Luna, y la sombra de la Tierra cae sobre la Luna.
axis
An imaginary line through Earth from the North Pole to the South Pole. Earth spins around it.
eje: Una línea imaginaria que atraviesa la Tierra del Polo Norte al Polo Sur. La Tierra gira alrededor de ella.
tilt
The way Earth's axis leans to one side as Earth travels around the sun.
inclinación: La manera en que el eje de la Tierra está ladeado mientras la Tierra viaja alrededor del Sol.

  1. AQuestion A. What did Daniel notice about the ball in the model?

    ¿Qué notó Daniel sobre la pelota del modelo?

    The ball was always half lit by the lamp, but the part of the lit half that Athena could see kept changing.

  2. BQuestion B. Why does the moon have phases?

    ¿Por qué la Luna tiene fases?

    The moon reflects sunlight, and as it goes around Earth, we see more or less of its sunny side.

  3. CQuestion C. What happens during a solar eclipse?

    ¿Qué pasa durante un eclipse solar?

    In a solar eclipse, the moon passes between the sun and Earth and blocks the sun.

  4. DQuestion D. What happens during a lunar eclipse?

    ¿Qué pasa durante un eclipse lunar?

    In a lunar eclipse, Earth passes between the sun and the moon, and Earth's shadow falls on the moon at full moon.

  5. EQuestion E. What causes the seasons?

    ¿Qué causa las estaciones?

    Earth's tilt makes the northern half lean toward the sun in June and away from it in December.

Check your thinking

Full moon: sun, Earth, moon in a line, with Earth in the middle, so we see the whole sunny side. New moon: sun, moon, Earth, with the moon in the middle, so its sunny side faces away from us. A lunar eclipse needs Earth between the sun and the moon so Earth's shadow can fall on the moon, and that is the full moon position.

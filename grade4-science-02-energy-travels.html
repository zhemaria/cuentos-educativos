The dancing rice

A story about how energy travels with sound, light, heat and electricity.

The story

It was a freezing January day in Chicago, and Ms. Patel had a surprise waiting in the music room. On the table sat a big drum, a bowl with plastic wrap stretched tight across the top and a handful of rice. "Today we're going to watch travel," she said.

Salma sprinkled a few grains of rice on the plastic wrap. Sophia grabbed the mallet and gave the drum a hard whack, one step away from the bowl. The rice grains jumped like popcorn! "The rice is dancing!" Israel shouted.

"But nobody touched the bowl," said Salma, her eyes wide. "Hitting the drum makes its skin ," Ms. Patel explained. "That vibration travels through the air as , reaches the plastic and makes it vibrate too." That was why the rice jumped: energy had traveled from the drum to the bowl.

Sophia tapped the drum again, very softly this time. The grains barely trembled. "A louder sound carries more energy," Salma said, and she wrote it down in her notebook.

Meanwhile, Israel had plopped down in the seat by the window, where the sun was pouring in. "This chair is nice and warm!" he said. "That's because from the sun travels a very long way and brings energy with it," said Ms. Patel. "When it reaches your chair, it warms it up."

To celebrate, Ms. Patel poured cups of hot chocolate. Israel wrapped both of his icy hands around his mug. Little by little his fingers warmed up: was moving from the hot chocolate to the mug, and from the mug to his cold hands. "Heat always moves from hot to cold," the teacher said. "That's why your cocoa will get cold if you don't drink it soon." Israel drank it in one gulp.

Last of all, Ms. Patel connected a small light bulb to a battery with two wires. The bulb lit up. "An carries energy from the battery, through the wires, to the bulb," she explained. Then the bulb changes that energy into light and heat.

Sophia raised her hand. "So energy is like a mail carrier. It's always delivering something from one place to another."

"Great idea," said Ms. Patel. "Today we saw four ways to energy: with sound, with light, with heat and with electric current." Israel looked at his empty mug and sighed. "Well, my hot chocolate just transferred all its energy. Straight into my stomach."

Glossary

energy
The ability to make something move or change. It can travel from one place to another.
vibration
A very fast back-and-forth movement. Sound is made by vibrations.
sound
Energy that travels as vibrations through air, water or solid objects.
light
Energy that travels from a source, like the sun or a light bulb, and lets us see.
heat
Energy that moves from warmer things to cooler things.
electric current
Electrical energy that travels along a path of wires, from a battery or an outlet.
transfer
To move something from one place or object to another.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Find it at home

Find one example at home or at school for each way energy travels: sound, light, heat and electric current. For one of them, tell where the energy starts and where it ends up.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Check your thinking

Some ideas: a doorbell you can hear (sound), a lamp that lights your book (light), a warm stove that heats a pot (heat) and a toaster plugged into the wall (electric current). In the toaster, the energy travels through the cord from the outlet and ends up as heat that browns the bread.

The story

After learning about the Great Fire, Israel had a question: "Is it true that a cow started it by kicking over a lantern?" His friend Jamal had told him that story.

Great-Grandpa took them to the Chicago history museum. There, a guide told them, "Let's be history detectives. To find out what happened, study : documents and objects that give us information about the past."

In one case there was a handwritten letter from 1871 by a girl who saw the fire, a newspaper from those days and a photo of the burned buildings. "These are ," the guide explained. "They were made by people who lived through the events, at that time."

There was also a lump of glass marbles that had melted and stuck together in the heat of the fire. "An object made and used by people in the past is called an ," said the guide. "This artifact is of how hot the fire was."

Great-Grandpa held up a modern history book. "And this book?" he asked. "That's a ," Lucía answered. "Someone wrote it later, by studying the primary sources."

Then the guide showed them a drawing of a cow. "The story of Mrs. O'Leary's cow is a ," she explained. "In 1893, a reporter named Michael Ahern admitted that he had made it up to make his article more exciting." In 1997, the Chicago City Council officially declared Mrs. O'Leary and her cow innocent.

"Poor cow!" laughed Israel. "For more than a hundred years, people blamed her for something no one could prove." The guide nodded. "Even a primary source can contain mistakes. Historians compare sources and ask how the writer knew what happened."

Before they left, they saw something else surprising: after the fire, people in England sent more than 8,000 books to Chicago. Those books helped start the Chicago Public Library, the same library where Lucía has her card!

That night, Israel wrote to Jamal: "We don't have enough evidence to blame the cow. We still don't know exactly how the fire started." And he drew a cow with a huge smile.

Glossary

historical source
A document or object that gives us information about the past.
primary source
A source made by someone who lived through an event, at that time, like a letter, a diary or a photo.
secondary source
A source written later by someone who studied the event, like a textbook or an encyclopedia.
artifact
An object made or used by people in the past, like a tool or a marble.
historian
A person who studies the past by using sources to find out what happened.
legend
A story that people tell a lot but that can't be proven or isn't true.
evidence
Something that helps us know whether an idea is true.

Ms. Novak hung an old railroad clock next to the map. "Before 1883, every town in America set its clocks by the Sun," she said. "Noon was the moment when the Sun was highest in the sky, right there. That's called ."

"So what's the problem?" asked Daniel. "The Sun is highest a little earlier in places to the east and a little later in places to the west," said Ms. Novak. "So when it was noon in Chicago, it was a few minutes past noon in Detroit and a few minutes before noon in St. Louis. There were more than a hundred different local times across the country."

"For the railroads, that was a nightmare," she said. "Each railroad used its own time, and a station might have several clocks showing different times. Reading a was confusing, and when trains ran on the same tracks, mistakes about time could be dangerous."

"On October 11, 1883, the railroad leaders met at the Grand Pacific Hotel right here in Chicago and voted for his plan."

"They chose Sunday, November 18, 1883, to switch," said Ms. Novak. "At noon, railroad clocks were reset to the new time. In many cities, the clocks struck noon once by the old local time and again by the new standard time. People called it the Day of Two Noons." Salma laughed. "Two lunches!"

Daniel and Salma each held a cardboard clock. "Chicago is in the Central zone," said Ms. Novak. "New York is one zone east. When it's noon in Chicago, what time is it in New York?" Daniel turned his clock. "One o'clock."

Glossary

local time
The time based on the position of the Sun in one place. Noon is when the Sun is highest there.
hora local: La hora según la posición del Sol en un lugar. El mediodía es cuando el Sol está más alto allí.
timetable
A schedule that shows when trains, buses or other transportation arrive and leave.
horario (itinerario): Una tabla que muestra cuándo llegan y salen los trenes, autobuses u otros transportes.
standard time
One official time for a whole region, used by everyone there.
hora estándar (hora oficial): Una hora oficial para toda una región, que usan todos allí.
time zone
A region of the Earth that uses the same standard time.
huso horario (zona horaria): Una región de la Tierra que usa la misma hora estándar.
meridian
An imaginary line from the North Pole to the South Pole. Time zones are based on meridians.
meridiano: Una línea imaginaria del polo Norte al polo Sur. Los husos horarios se basan en meridianos.
cause and effect
A cause is why something happens; an effect is what happens as a result.
causa y efecto: La causa es por qué ocurre algo; el efecto es lo que ocurre como resultado.

  1. AQuestion A. Why were there more than a hundred local times?

    ¿Por qué había más de cien horas locales?

    The Sun is highest at different moments in different places, so every town had its own local time.

  2. BQuestion B. Why were local times a problem for the railroads?

    ¿Por qué las horas locales eran un problema para los ferrocarriles?

    Each railroad had its own time, timetables were confusing, and mistakes about time on shared tracks could be dangerous.

  3. CQuestion C. What did William F. Allen propose?

    ¿Qué propuso William F. Allen?

    He proposed time zones, each one hour apart, in which everyone used the same standard time.

  4. DQuestion D. What was the Day of Two Noons?

    ¿Qué fue el día de los dos mediodías?

    When the railroads switched on November 18, 1883, many clocks struck noon twice.

  5. EQuestion E. When did Congress make time zones official law?

    ¿Cuándo hizo el Congreso que los husos horarios fueran ley oficial?

    The railroads used standard time from 1883, but Congress made it official law in 1918.

On a cold March morning, Mr. Mensah held up an old photo: a thin man in a heavy coat stood in the snow next to a tall, skinny metal frame. "This is Robert Goddard," he said, "on March 16, 1926, at his Aunt Effie's farm in Auburn, Massachusetts. A few minutes later, his rocket flew for 2.5 seconds, rose 41 feet and landed 184 feet away, in a cabbage patch."

"That's not very far," said Daniel. "It was the first rocket in history to fly with liquid fuel," said Mr. Mensah. "And a few years earlier, in 1920, a newspaper had made fun of Goddard. It said a rocket could never work in space, because in a there is nothing to push against."

He asked Elías and Selena to stand on two skateboards, face to face, with their palms together. Only Elías pushed, but both of them rolled apart. "When Elías pushed on Selena, Selena's hands pushed back on Elías with a force of the same size, in the opposite direction," said Mr. Mensah. "That is . The two forces always come together, as a ."

Next, Salma blew up a long balloon, taped it to a straw on a string that crossed the gym and let it go. It zoomed to the other side. "The balloon pushes the air out backward, and the air pushes the balloon forward," said Mr. Mensah. "A rocket works the same way. It doesn't need air to push against: it pushes on its own hot gases, and the gases push it forward. That push is called ."

Then he put a squeezed spring between two carts, one empty and one full of textbooks, and cut the string that held the spring. The carts shot apart. The two forces were equal, but the empty cart rolled much farther, because it had less mass.

"Now, a problem for engineers," said Mr. Mensah. Each team got a cart with a raw egg in it. The carts would roll toward each other and crash. The : the egg must not break, and the bumper must fit on the front of the cart. "In a , the two carts push on each other with equal forces, so you can't make the forces disappear," said Mr. Mensah. "But a soft bumper makes the crash last longer, so the push at each moment is smaller."

Israel's team used a paper cup. Crack! Athena's team used a sponge and two rubber bands, and their egg survived without a scratch. "The sponge squished slowly," said Athena. "It spread the push out over more time."

At the end of class, Mr. Mensah told one more fact. Goddard died in 1945. In July 1969, while Apollo 11 was flying to the Moon, the same newspaper printed a correction: a rocket can work in a vacuum. "He was right for almost fifty years," said Selena. Sophia wrote in her notebook: Every push comes back. Design for it.

Glossary

Newton's third law
When one object pushes or pulls on another, the second object pushes or pulls back with a force of the same size in the opposite direction.
tercera ley de Newton: Cuando un objeto empuja o tira de otro, el segundo empuja o tira del primero con una fuerza del mismo tamaño en dirección contraria.
force pair
The two equal and opposite forces that two objects put on each other at the same time.
par de fuerzas: Las dos fuerzas iguales y opuestas que dos objetos se hacen el uno al otro al mismo tiempo.
thrust
The forward push on a rocket, made when it pushes hot gases out backward.
empuje: El empujón hacia adelante que recibe un cohete cuando expulsa gases calientes hacia atrás.
vacuum
A space with no air, like outer space.
vacío: Un espacio sin aire, como el espacio exterior.
collision
A crash in which two moving objects hit each other.
choque (colisión): Un golpe en el que dos objetos en movimiento se encuentran.
criteria
The things a design must do to be successful.
criterios: Lo que un diseño tiene que lograr para tener éxito.

Questions

  1. AQuestion A. When Elías pushed Selena, why did both of them roll apart?

    Cuando Elías empujó a Selena, ¿por qué se separaron los dos?

    By Newton's third law, Selena's hands pushed back on Elías with an equal force in the opposite direction.

  2. BQuestion B. How can a rocket move in space, where there is no air?

    ¿Cómo puede moverse un cohete en el espacio, donde no hay aire?

    The rocket pushes its gases out backward, and the gases push the rocket forward: that is thrust.

  3. CQuestion C. Why did the empty cart roll farther than the cart full of books?

    ¿Por qué el carrito vacío rodó más lejos que el carrito lleno de libros?

    The force pair was equal, but the same force changes the motion of an object with less mass more.

  4. DQuestion D. How does a soft bumper help in a collision?

    ¿Cómo ayuda un parachoques blando en un choque?

    The forces are still equal, but a soft bumper spreads the push over more time.

  5. EQuestion E. What did the newspaper print in July 1969?

    ¿Qué publicó el periódico en julio de 1969?

    While Apollo 11 flew to the Moon, the newspaper corrected its 1920 article: a rocket can work in a vacuum.

On a gray November day, the class rode the bus to the Garfield Park Conservatory. Inside, under the glass, it felt like summer. Sophia stopped in front of a palm tree so tall that its leaves touched the roof.

"This tree weighs more than our whole class together," she said. "Where did all that come from? It must have eaten a lot of dirt." Salma looked down at the pot. "But the is still there. The pot isn't empty."

Ms. Kowalski smiled. "A scientist asked that exact question almost four hundred years ago. His name was Jan Baptist van Helmont." She held up a drawing of a small willow tree in a big pot.

"Van Helmont weighed the soil, planted a small willow tree in it and gave it only rainwater for five years," she explained. "He covered the pot so no dust could fall in. It was a careful ."

"After five years, the little tree had grown into a big tree," she continued. "It had gained about 164 pounds. Then van Helmont dried and weighed the soil again. It had lost only about two ounces." Elías's eyes went wide. "Two ounces? That's like a small cookie!"

"So the tree didn't grow from the soil," said Sophia. "Then where did it come from?" Van Helmont thought it was only the water. "He was partly right," said Ms. Kowalski. "Plants take in water through their ."

"But later, other scientists discovered the missing piece." She pointed up at the giant leaves shining in the light. "Plants take in a gas from the air called through their . With the energy of sunlight, they use that gas and water to build their stems, leaves and trunks."

"So this tree is made mostly of air and water?" asked Salma. "Mostly, yes," said the teacher. "Plants take a small amount of minerals from the soil, but most of their matter comes from air and water." Sophia looked up at the palm tree again, amazed. "A tree made of air," she whispered.

On the bus ride home, Elías breathed out loudly on the window. "What are you doing?" asked Salma. "Giving the trees some carbon dioxide," he said. "I want them to grow big and strong." Ms. Kowalski laughed. "That's very generous of you, Elías."

Glossary

matter
Anything that takes up space and has weight.
materia: Todo lo que ocupa espacio y tiene peso.
soil
The top layer of the ground, where plants grow. It is made of bits of rock and old plants.
tierra: La capa de arriba del suelo, donde crecen las plantas. Está hecha de trocitos de roca y restos de plantas.
roots
The parts of a plant under the ground. They take in water and hold the plant in place.
raíces: Las partes de la planta que están bajo tierra. Absorben agua y sostienen la planta.
leaves
The parts of a plant that catch sunlight and take in air.
hojas: Las partes de la planta que captan la luz del sol y toman el aire.
carbon dioxide
A gas in the air. Plants take it in through their leaves and use it to build their bodies.
dióxido de carbono: Un gas del aire. Las plantas lo toman por las hojas y lo usan para formar su cuerpo.
experiment
A careful test to answer a question.
experimento: Una prueba cuidadosa para responder una pregunta.

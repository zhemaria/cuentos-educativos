The story

For the spring campout, the families pitched their tents in an Illinois state park. Daniel slept in one tent, and Elías and Athena slept in another a few steps away. They had brought two toy walkie-talkies to talk at night, but the batteries died before dinner.

"So how do we send messages now?" Elías asked. Daniel held up his flashlight. "With light. We can make up a , with a for each message. One flash means yes, and two flashes mean no."

That night, Elías whispered from his tent, "Do you have any cookies?" Daniel answered with one flash. Yes! Then Athena asked if Daniel was scared of owls. Two flashes. No. Well, sort of.

But soon their code wasn't enough. "We can only say yes and no," Athena complained. "How do I say come look at the moon?" That's when Daniel's dad, who used to be a scout, told them about .

"It's a very old code," he explained. "Each letter is a of short and long signals. For example, S is short, short, short, and O is long, long, long." With that, you can spell any word at all. Athena copied the whole alphabet into her sketchbook, with dots for short signals and dashes for long ones.

The next day, the friends tried out two different : sending messages with flashlight beams or with the sound of a whistle. Each of them took turns sending short and long signals, while the other one wrote down what came through.

Then they sat down to . The flashlight worked great at night and didn't make any noise, but you had to be able to see the other tent. The whistle could be heard behind the trees, but it woke up the whole campground. "My grandpa says if I whistle at six in the morning again, he's giving me to the raccoons," Elías reported.

"Both messages were the same," said Athena, "even though one was light and the other was sound. What matters is the pattern. If both people know the code, the pattern carries the ."

On the way home, Daniel's dad held up his phone. "Phones and computers send messages with patterns too," he said. "Really fast, really long patterns that travel as signals." That night, Daniel flashed a message to Elías from his bedroom window: short, short, short; long, long, long; short, short, short. Elías checked Athena's sketchbook: S, O, S, the signal people all over the world use to call for help. Then he saw Daniel in his window, holding up an empty cookie box, and he cracked up.

Glossary

pattern
Something that repeats or follows an order. A pattern can stand for a message.
code
A system of signals that stand for letters, words or ideas.
signal
Something you can see or hear that carries a message, like a flash of light or a whistle.
information
Facts or messages that tell us something.
Morse code
An old code that uses short and long signals to stand for each letter.
solution
A way to solve a problem.
compare
To look at how two or more things are alike and how they are different.

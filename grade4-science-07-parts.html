The story

Athena never goes anywhere without her sketchbook. On the field trip to the nature center, while Israel and Salma raced down the trail, she drew everything she saw. "Here's today's challenge," said Ranger Moreno. "Find out what the parts of living things are for."

By the pond, a painted turtle was sunning itself on a log. When Israel got close, the turtle pulled its head inside. "Its is an ," the ranger explained. "It's hard like a helmet, and it protects the turtle from animals that want to eat it."

"What about the rose bush?" asked Israel, who had just poked his finger. "Why does it have thorns?" "Thorns protect the plant," said the ranger. "A hungry deer doesn't like biting a stem full of prickles." Israel sucked on his finger. "I didn't like it either."

Athena sketched one of the roses, where a bee was looking for nectar. "Flowers aren't just pretty," the ranger said. "A flower helps the plant . The bee carries pollen from one flower to another, and that lets the plant make seeds."

Salma lifted her binoculars. On a high branch, a great horned owl was asleep. "Its eyes are huge," she said. Owls hunt at night, and their big eyes help them see in very dim light. Their sharp talons help them catch mice.

"So far we've only looked at outside parts," said the ranger, pulling out a model of a bird skeleton. "But living things have too. The skeleton holds up the body, and the heart pumps blood all through it." Bird bones are also light, with air spaces inside, and that helps birds fly.

"Do plants have inside parts too?" Athena asked. "Sure they do. A stem has tiny tubes inside that carry water from the roots up to the leaves. And the roots, underground, hold the plant in place and take in water so it can grow."

On the bus ride home, Athena flipped through her sketchbook: the turtle, the rose bush, the flower, the owl, the skeleton. Next to each drawing she wrote the of each . Every one of them helped a living thing , grow or reproduce.

Israel looked at the rose bush drawing and rubbed his finger. "Add a note," he said. "Thorns work great. Tested by Israel."

Glossary

structure
A part of a living thing that has a shape and a job, like a shell, a root or a heart.
external structure
A part on the outside of the body that you can see, like feathers or thorns.
internal structure
A part on the inside of the body, like the heart or the skeleton.
function
The job that a part of a living thing does.
survive
To stay alive, stay safe from danger and get what you need.
reproduce
To have young or make seeds so new living things can grow.
shell
The hard covering that protects a turtle's body.

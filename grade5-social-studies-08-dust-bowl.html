The story

Mr. Okafor set two trays of soil on the table. One was covered with green grass. The other was bare brown dirt. Then he turned on a fan. The grassy tray didn't move. But the bare tray sent a cloud of dust flying. Elías and Selena laughed and covered their eyes.

"That," said Mr. Okafor, "is the story of the ." On the screen, he showed an old photo of a dark cloud of dust rolling over a farm. "In the 1930s, this happened on the , in states like Oklahoma, Kansas and Texas."

"For thousands of years, deep grass covered the Plains," he explained. "Its roots held the soil in place. But when settlers arrived, new machines, like tractors and steel plows, let farmers plow up millions of acres of grass to plant wheat."

"Then, in the 1930s, a long came. Without rain and without grass, the dry soil turned to dust, and the wind blew it away." Selena looked at the bare tray. "Just like our experiment."

"The storms were terrible," said Mr. Okafor. "On April 14, 1935, a storm called Black Sunday turned the day as dark as night. Dust got into houses, food and lungs. Crops died, and many families lost their farms." It was a caused by nature and by people's technology.

"Around two and a half million people left the Plains states in the 1930s," the teacher said. "Many families packed everything into old cars and drove west, to California, looking for work." They became workers, picking fruit and cotton for very low pay.

The government wanted the country to see what was happening. It hired photographers, like Arthur Rothstein and Dorothea Lange, to take pictures of the farms and the families. "Their photos helped people in cities understand the problem," said Mr. Okafor, "and they helped convince leaders to act."

"The government started teaching farmers ," he continued. "Farmers learned to plow in curved rows along the hills, to leave stalks in the fields and to plant long lines of trees to block the wind." Little by little, when the rain came back, the land began to heal.

At the end of class, Elías carefully swept the dust off the table into his hand. "Mr. Okafor," he said, "can I keep the grassy tray? I want to make sure our classroom never becomes a Dust Bowl." "Deal," said the teacher. "You are now our official conservation officer."

Check your thinking

Details: the shed is half buried, there are no plants, the children bend forward against the wind, and the sky is gray. The photo makes people feel sorry for the family and understand how serious the problem was. That could make them support programs to help farmers and protect the soil. Photos, like other art, can change what people believe and do.

Glossary

drought
A long time with little or no rain.
sequía: Un tiempo largo con poca o ninguna lluvia.
Great Plains
A huge, flat region of grassland in the middle of the United States.
Grandes Llanuras: Una región enorme y plana de pastizales en el centro de Estados Unidos.
Dust Bowl
The part of the Great Plains where dust storms ruined farms in the 1930s.
Dust Bowl: La parte de las Grandes Llanuras donde las tormentas de polvo arruinaron las granjas en la década de 1930.
disaster
A terrible event that causes great damage.
desastre: Un suceso terrible que causa grandes daños.
conservation
Protecting and using natural resources carefully, so they last.
conservación: Proteger y usar con cuidado los recursos naturales para que duren.
migrant
A person who moves from one place to another to live or find work.
migrante: Una persona que se muda de un lugar a otro para vivir o buscar trabajo.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Analyze the effects

The Dust Bowl was caused by nature (the drought) and by people (plowing up the grass). Explain one effect on the land and one effect on where people lived. Then tell one thing farmers changed so it would not happen again.

Analiza los efectos. El Dust Bowl lo causaron la naturaleza (la sequía) y las personas (arar el pasto). Explica un efecto sobre la tierra y un efecto sobre dónde vivía la gente. Después di una cosa que cambiaron los granjeros para que no volviera a pasar.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

The story

Every Friday, the class took care of the community garden two blocks from school. This Friday, Israel carried a bucket of banana peels, apple cores and old lettuce from the cafeteria. "Where should I throw the garbage?" he asked.

"It's not garbage," said Ms. Kowalski. "It's lunch for the ." She opened the lid of a wooden bin in the corner. Inside there were brown leaves, old peels and a lot of dark, moist dirt that smelled like a forest after the rain.

Salma dug in with a small shovel. Pink earthworms wriggled away from the light, and a few small mushrooms grew on an old piece of wood. "Ew," said Israel. "Who invited them?" "They invited themselves," said the teacher. "They're the most important workers in the garden."

"Worms, mushrooms and tiny bacteria are ," Ms. Kowalski explained. "They eat dead plants and animals and break them down into tiny pieces." Salma held up a handful of dark soil. "Is this what's left of last month's banana peels?"

"Yes," said the teacher. "The decomposers turned them into rich soil. The matter from the peels went back into the soil, where plants can use it again." Israel looked at his bucket with new respect.

At the edge of a garden bed, a brown rabbit was nibbling a lettuce leaf. "Hey! That's our lettuce!" said Israel. Ms. Kowalski laughed. "The lettuce is a . It used sunlight, air, water and a few minerals from the soil to grow. The rabbit is a : it eats plants to get matter and energy."

Just then, a shadow passed over the garden. A hawk was circling high above. "And if the hawk eats the rabbit," said Salma, "then the matter from the lettuce goes into the hawk." "Exactly," said Ms. Kowalski.

That afternoon, Salma drew a on the board: lettuce, rabbit, hawk, and arrows pointing down to the worms and mushrooms. "When the hawk dies one day, decomposers will break it down too, and its matter will go back to the soil." Then she drew one last arrow, from the soil back to the lettuce. "It's a circle," she said.

"That's the big idea," said Ms. Kowalski. "In an , matter moves around and around, among plants, animals, decomposers and the soil, the air and the water." Israel emptied his bucket into the compost bin and put the lid back on carefully. "Enjoy your lunch, guys," he told the worms. "See you next month as soil."

Glossary

ecosystem
All the living things in a place and the nonliving things around them, like soil, water and air.
ecosistema: Todos los seres vivos de un lugar y lo que no está vivo a su alrededor, como la tierra, el agua y el aire.
producer
A living thing that makes its own food, like a plant.
productor: Un ser vivo que fabrica su propio alimento, como una planta.
consumer
An animal that gets its food by eating plants or other animals.
consumidor: Un animal que obtiene su alimento comiendo plantas u otros animales.
decomposer
A living thing, like a worm, a mushroom or bacteria, that breaks down dead plants and animals.
descomponedor: Un ser vivo, como una lombriz, un hongo o las bacterias, que descompone plantas y animales muertos.
compost
Food scraps and leaves that decomposers have turned into rich soil.
composta: Restos de comida y hojas que los descomponedores han convertido en tierra fértil.
food web
A model that shows how matter and energy move from one living thing to another in an ecosystem.
red alimentaria: Un modelo que muestra cómo pasan la materia y la energía de un ser vivo a otro en un ecosistema.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Draw a model

Draw a food web for a park near your home with at least one producer, two consumers and one decomposer. Use arrows to show where the matter goes. Explain what would happen if there were no decomposers.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Check your thinking

Example: grass → squirrel → hawk, and leaves → worms → soil → grass. Without decomposers, dead leaves and animals would pile up, and their matter would not go back to the soil. Plants would have fewer minerals to grow, and the whole ecosystem would suffer.

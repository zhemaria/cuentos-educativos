The wheel that answered the Eiffel Tower

The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Mr. Mensah held up an old drawing of a giant steel wheel. "In 1889, Paris built the Eiffel Tower for its World's Fair. For the Chicago World's Fair of 1893, the city wanted something just as amazing." "An engineer named George Ferris built a wheel 264 feet tall, with 36 cars as big as streetcars. Each car could carry 60 people. It was the first Ferris wheel."

"How did they make it turn?" asked Elías. "Two huge steam engines," said Mr. Mensah. "The engines had to lift thousands of people high into the air. And lifting things takes ." He set a ruler on a stack of books to make a ramp. "Let's see where that energy goes."

Selena held a big marble at the top of the ramp. "Right now, the marble isn't moving, but it has stored energy because it's high up," said Mr. Mensah. "That's . The higher you lift something, the more potential energy it has." "Like the people at the top of the Ferris wheel," said Israel.

Selena let go. The marble rolled down faster and faster and hit a paper cup at the bottom. The cup slid across the floor, and Israel measured: 18 centimeters. "As the marble rolled down, its potential energy changed into , the energy of motion," said Mr. Mensah. "When it hit the cup, some of that energy was to the cup and made it move."

Next, Elías rolled a small marble from the same height. The cup moved only 7 centimeters. "Same height, same at the bottom, but less ," said Mr. Mensah. "An object with less mass has less kinetic energy, so it pushed the cup a shorter distance."

Then they added more books to make the ramp higher and rolled the big marble again. It reached the bottom much faster, and the cup slid 40 centimeters. Israel did the math. "That's more than double!" "Speed matters a lot," said Mr. Mensah. "If an object goes twice as fast, it has four times as much kinetic energy. That's why a car crash at a high speed is so much more dangerous."

Selena drew two graphs on the board. On the first, kinetic energy went up in a straight line as the mass grew. On the second, the line curved up more and more steeply as the speed grew. "Mass makes it grow steadily," she said, "but speed makes it grow fast."

At the end of class, Mr. Mensah showed one last fact: the first Ferris wheel turned so slowly that one ride, two full turns, took about 20 minutes. "Slow and high," said Elías. "Lots of potential energy at the top, but not much kinetic energy." "So nobody got scared," said Selena. "Except the people afraid of heights," said Israel.

Glossary

energy
The ability to cause motion or change.
energía: La capacidad de causar movimiento o cambio.
kinetic energy
The energy an object has because it is moving. It depends on the object's mass and speed.
energía cinética: La energía que tiene un objeto porque se mueve. Depende de la masa y de la rapidez del objeto.
potential energy
Stored energy. An object raised higher has more gravitational potential energy.
energía potencial: Energía almacenada. Un objeto que está más alto tiene más energía potencial gravitatoria.
mass
The amount of matter in an object.
masa: La cantidad de materia que tiene un objeto.
speed
How fast an object moves: the distance it travels in a certain time.
rapidez: Qué tan rápido se mueve un objeto: la distancia que recorre en cierto tiempo.
transfer
To move from one object to another. Energy can transfer when objects collide.
transferir: Pasar de un objeto a otro. La energía se puede transferir cuando los objetos chocan.

Questions

  1. AQuestion A. What did George Ferris build for the 1893 World's Fair?

    ¿Qué construyó George Ferris para la Feria Mundial de 1893?

    He built a wheel 264 feet tall with 36 cars, each able to carry 60 people: the first Ferris wheel.

  2. BQuestion B. Why does the marble at the top of the ramp have potential energy?

    ¿Por qué la canica en lo alto de la rampa tiene energía potencial?

    It has stored energy because it is high up; the higher it is, the more potential energy it has.

  3. CQuestion C. What happened to the energy as the marble rolled down?

    ¿Qué pasó con la energía mientras la canica bajaba?

    Its potential energy changed into kinetic energy, and some was transferred to the cup.

  4. DQuestion D. Why did the small marble move the cup a shorter distance?

    ¿Por qué la canica pequeña movió el vaso una distancia menor?

    From the same height it reached the same speed, but with less mass it had less kinetic energy.

  5. EQuestion E. What happens to kinetic energy if the speed doubles?

    ¿Qué le pasa a la energía cinética si la rapidez se duplica?

    If an object goes twice as fast, it has four times as much kinetic energy.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Analyze the data

Look at the three results: big marble, low ramp: 18 cm; small marble, low ramp: 7 cm; big marble, high ramp: 40 cm. Which two trials show the effect of mass? Which two show the effect of speed? Explain what each pair tells you.

Analiza los datos. Mira los tres resultados: canica grande, rampa baja: 18 cm; canica pequeña, rampa baja: 7 cm; canica grande, rampa alta: 40 cm. ¿Qué dos pruebas muestran el efecto de la masa? ¿Cuáles muestran el efecto de la rapidez? Explica qué te dice cada par.

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Mass: compare the big and the small marble on the low ramp (18 cm and 7 cm). Only the mass changed, and more mass meant more kinetic energy. Speed: compare the big marble on the low and the high ramp (18 cm and 40 cm). Only the height changed, so the marble was faster at the bottom, and the extra speed gave it much more kinetic energy. A fair test changes only one thing at a time.

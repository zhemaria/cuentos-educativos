The story

It had rained for three days in a row. Salma traced a raindrop down the window with her finger. "Where does all this water come from?" she asked. "And where does it go?" Ms. Rivera handed Israel a bowl. "Let's build a model and follow one drop."

Israel poured a little warm water into the glass bowl, covered it with plastic wrap and put ice cubes on top. Ms. Rivera turned a lamp on it. "The lamp is our sun," she said. "Energy from the sun warms the water, and some of it turns into invisible . That's ."

After a few minutes, tiny drops appeared under the plastic, right below the ice. "When the water vapor rises and hits the cold plastic, it cools and turns back into liquid drops," said Selena. "That's . It's how clouds form, high up where the air is cold."

The drops grew bigger and heavier until one fell back into the water with a tiny plip. " pulled it down," said Ms. Rivera. "In the real world, the drops in a cloud join together until they're too heavy to float. Then they fall as : rain, snow, sleet or hail."

"So the sun lifts the water up and gravity pulls it down," said Salma. "Exactly. Those are the two engines of the water cycle," said Ms. Rivera. "And gravity keeps working after the rain lands."

She pointed out the window at the playground, where water was running into the drains. "Some rain flows downhill into streams, rivers and lakes. Some soaks into the ground and becomes , moving slowly between rocks and soil. Some is taken up by plants and released into the air from their leaves."

"So the raindrop on my window could have been in Lake Michigan last week?" asked Salma. "Or in the ocean last year, or in a glacier thousands of years ago," said Ms. Rivera. "In the water cycle, Earth's water is used again and again. The same water keeps moving between the oceans, the air, the land and living things."

Selena looked at her water bottle with suspicion. "So this water might have been… a dinosaur's drink?" "Possibly," said Ms. Rivera. Selena put the cap back on. "I'll drink it later," she said.

Glossary

evaporation
When liquid water gains energy and turns into water vapor, an invisible gas.
evaporación: Cuando el agua líquida gana energía y se convierte en vapor de agua, un gas invisible.
water vapor
Water in the form of a gas. It is invisible and mixes with the air.
vapor de agua: El agua en forma de gas. Es invisible y se mezcla con el aire.
condensation
When water vapor cools and turns back into tiny drops of liquid water.
condensación: Cuando el vapor de agua se enfría y vuelve a convertirse en gotitas de agua líquida.
precipitation
Water that falls from clouds as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
precipitación: El agua que cae de las nubes como lluvia, nieve, aguanieve o granizo.
gravity
The force that pulls objects toward the center of Earth.
gravedad: La fuerza que atrae los objetos hacia el centro de la Tierra.
groundwater
Water that soaks into the ground and fills the spaces between soil and rock.
agua subterránea: El agua que se filtra en el suelo y llena los espacios entre la tierra y las rocas.

  1. AQuestion A. In the model, what made the water turn into vapor?

    En el modelo, ¿qué hizo que el agua se convirtiera en vapor?

    Energy from the lamp, like energy from the sun, warmed the water, and some of it evaporated.

  2. BQuestion B. Why did drops form under the plastic wrap?

    ¿Por qué se formaron gotas debajo del plástico?

    The water vapor cooled when it touched the cold plastic and condensed into liquid drops.

  3. CQuestion C. What makes water fall from clouds?

    ¿Qué hace que el agua caiga de las nubes?

    Drops join together until they are too heavy to float, and gravity pulls them down as precipitation.

  4. DQuestion D. Where can rainwater go after it lands?

    ¿Adónde puede ir el agua de lluvia después de caer?

    Rain can flow into streams and lakes, soak into the ground as groundwater, or be taken up by plants.

  5. EQuestion E. Why could Salma's raindrop have been in a glacier long ago?

    ¿Por qué la gota de Salma pudo haber estado en un glaciar hace mucho tiempo?

    In the water cycle, water is used again and again; the same water keeps moving between the oceans, air, land and living things.

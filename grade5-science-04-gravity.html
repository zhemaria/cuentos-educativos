The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

After school, Elías, Daniel and Israel went to the basketball court in the park. Elías threw the ball as high as he could. It went up, up, up... and then it came back down, as always, and bounced off Israel's head. "Ow!" said Israel. "Why does everything always fall on me?"

"Not on you," said Daniel, pushing up his glasses. "Down. Everything falls down. It's ." "And what is gravity?" asked Israel, rubbing his head. Daniel had studied this in class with Ms. Kowalski.

"Gravity is a , a pull," Daniel explained. "Earth pulls every object toward it, even if nobody touches it. That's why the ball comes back down." "Even a feather?" asked Elías. "Even a feather. It just falls more slowly because the air gets in its way."

Israel thought for a moment, and then he looked worried. "Wait. If Earth is round, what about the kids on the other side, in Australia? Are they upside down? Why don't they fall off into space?"

Daniel smiled and took a small globe out of his backpack. He had been waiting for this question. All around the globe, he had taped tiny paper people: one in Chicago, one at the North Pole, one in Brazil and one in Australia, on the bottom.

"Earth is a , almost like a big ball," said Daniel. "Gravity pulls everything toward the of Earth. So for every person, 'down' means toward the center of the planet, under their feet." He turned the globe. Every little paper person had their feet on the ground and their head pointing out to space.

"So in Australia, down is under their feet too," said Elías. "Right. Nobody is upside down. Gravity pulls all of them toward the middle of Earth, just like it pulls us." Israel looked relieved. "Good. I'd hate for a kid in Australia to have a ball fall on his head and then keep going into space."

Elías picked up the ball. "Then why do I feel heavy when I jump?" "That's your ," said Daniel. "Your weight is the pull of gravity on you." "So gravity is why I can't dunk," Elías said. "Mostly," Daniel answered. "Being short helps too."

Israel tossed the ball straight up one more time and stepped quickly out of the way. The ball came straight down in the same as always: toward the center of Earth. It bounced right where he had been standing. "Ha!" said Israel. "I finally beat gravity." "No," said Daniel, laughing, "you just finally moved."

Glossary

force
A push or a pull on an object.
fuerza: Un empujón o un jalón sobre un objeto.
gravity
The force that pulls objects toward the center of Earth.
gravedad: La fuerza que jala los objetos hacia el centro de la Tierra.
center
The middle point of something. Earth's gravity pulls toward its center.
centro: El punto de en medio de algo. La gravedad de la Tierra jala hacia su centro.
direction
The way something moves or points, like up, down or sideways.
dirección: Hacia dónde se mueve o apunta algo, como arriba, abajo o de lado.
weight
The pull of gravity on an object. It is how heavy the object feels.
peso: El jalón de la gravedad sobre un objeto. Es lo que pesa.
sphere
A round shape, like a ball. Earth is almost a sphere.
esfera: Una figura redonda, como una pelota. La Tierra es casi una esfera.

The story

Alejandro came to Chicago for winter break. To play, Israel spread a giant of Illinois on the floor. "Let's look for a treasure," he said, "but you have to read the map."

First they looked at the : the little box that explains what each picture on the map means. A star was the capital, dots were cities and blue lines were rivers.

"The tells us where north, south, east and west are," Israel explained, pointing to the arrow with the letter N.

The first clue said: "Find the biggest water in the state." Alejandro found a huge blue shape at the top right: Michigan, one of the five Great Lakes. Chicago is on its shore.

The second clue: "Follow the western border." It was a long blue line: the Mississippi , which separates Illinois from Iowa and Missouri. In the south, the Ohio River forms another border.

The third clue: "Find the star." It was Springfield, the of Illinois, where the governor works. Close by ran the Illinois River, which crosses the state from northeast to southwest.

"Why are there almost no mountains?" asked Alejandro. "Because Illinois is very flat," Israel answered. "It used to be covered with : huge fields of tall grass. That's why it's called the Prairie State."

Under the star, at the last clue, they found the treasure: two chocolates Great-Grandpa had hidden. "The map worked!" they shouted.

That night, Alejandro asked Israel to help him make a map of Texas. "We have the Rio Grande and the Gulf of Mexico," he said. "The next treasure hunt will be at my house!"

Glossary

map
A drawing of a place seen from above that shows where things are.
map key
The part of a map that explains what each symbol or color means.
compass rose
The drawing on a map that shows where north, south, east and west are.
river
A stream of water that flows across the land into another river, a lake or the sea.
lake
A large body of water surrounded by land.
prairie
Flat, wide land covered with tall grasses and wildflowers.
capital
The city where the government of a state or country works.

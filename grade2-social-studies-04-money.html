The story

In the neighborhood garden, Grandpa's plants grew lots and lots of tomatoes. "There are too many for us!" said Lucía. "What if we trade them for other things?"

That is how her first began. To barter is to trade one thing for another without using money. Mrs. Elena gave Lucía a jar of honey for ten tomatoes. They were both happy!

Then Lucía wanted bread. She went to Mr. Julio's bakery with her basket of tomatoes. "I am sorry," said Mr. Julio. "I already have lots of tomatoes. What I need is eggs."

Lucía looked for someone with eggs. The neighbor with chickens wanted carrots, not tomatoes. And the neighbor with carrots wanted flowers. "This is very hard!" said Lucía. "To barter, each person has to want what the other one has."

Grandpa smiled. "That is why people invented ," he explained. "Almost everyone accepts money, so is much easier." With money, you can get almost anything.

The next day, Lucía set up a little table in front of the garden. On a sign, she wrote the of the tomatoes: one dollar per bag. The price is what you have to pay for something.

That day, Lucía was the : the person who sells. Several neighbors were and paid her with money. With the dollars she earned, Lucía went back to the bakery.

This time, Mr. Julio gladly took the money and gave her a big loaf of freshly baked bread. "With money, everything was easier!" said Lucía, smelling the warm bread.

Glossary

barter
To trade one thing for another without using money.
money
Coins and bills that almost everyone accepts in exchange for things or work.
exchange
When two people give each other something: one thing for another, or a thing for money.
price
What you have to pay to buy something.
seller
The person who sells something for money.
buyer
The person who pays money to get something.

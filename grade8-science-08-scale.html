The story

El cuento está en inglés. Pulsa «Listen to the story» para escucharlo y toca las palabras verdes cuando no entiendas una.

Mr. Mensah handed out a table of about the planets: their and their distances from the Sun. "The numbers are so big that they stop meaning anything," he said. "So we're going to build a of the solar system, right here in the hallway."

Daniel stood at one end of the hallway holding a yellow ball about eight inches across. "That's the Sun," said Mr. Mensah. "At this scale, the Earth is about the size of a peppercorn, and it goes about 26 steps away." Athena counted the steps and put the peppercorn on an index card. Everyone stared at the tiny dot. "That's us?" said Selena.

"The Earth's average distance from the Sun is called one , or 1 AU," said Mr. Mensah. "Look at the data: Jupiter is about 5 AU from the Sun, so it goes about five times as far as the Earth." Israel stretched the tape measure past the classroom doors and put a small marble at about 130 steps.

"And Neptune?" asked Elías. Salma checked the table. Neptune was about 30 AU away. "Thirty times 26 steps is 780 steps," she said. "Our hallway isn't long enough. Neptune would be out in the parking lot!" The class laughed, and then went quiet. Almost everything in the model was empty space.

"And the stars?" asked Sophia. "At this scale, the nearest star after the Sun would be thousands of miles away," said Mr. Mensah. "For distances that big, astronomers needed a new kind of ruler. A woman named Henrietta Leavitt found one."

"Leavitt worked at the Harvard College Observatory, studying photographs of stars. In 1912, she reported that in a group of called Cepheids, the brighter stars took longer to brighten and dim." "So if you time the blinking, you know how bright the star really is," said Israel. "And if you know how bright it really is, you can tell how far away it is by how dim it looks," said Mr. Mensah.

"In 1924, Edwin Hubble used Leavitt's discovery with a Cepheid in the Andromeda nebula, and he showed that it was far outside our Milky Way. Andromeda was a whole other ." "Hubble from Wheaton?" asked Daniel. "The same one," said Mr. Mensah. "But he needed Leavitt's ruler first."

When they put the model away, Athena carried the peppercorn back to the classroom very carefully. Sophia wrote in her notebook: The universe is mostly space. To measure it, you need a ruler someone was patient enough to find.

Glossary

scale model
A model in which every size and distance is made smaller by the same amount.
modelo a escala: Un modelo en el que todos los tamaños y distancias se hacen más pequeños en la misma proporción.
diameter
The distance across a circle or a sphere, through its center.
diámetro: La distancia de un lado a otro de un círculo o una esfera, pasando por el centro.
astronomical unit
The average distance from the Earth to the Sun, about 93 million miles. It is written AU.
unidad astronómica: La distancia promedio de la Tierra al Sol, unos 93 millones de millas. Se escribe UA (en inglés, AU).
data
Facts and measurements collected to answer a question.
datos: Hechos y mediciones que se reúnen para responder una pregunta.
variable star
A star whose brightness changes over time. Some of them brighten and dim in a regular rhythm.
estrella variable: Una estrella cuyo brillo cambia con el tiempo. Algunas se encienden y se apagan con un ritmo regular.
galaxy
A huge group of billions of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity, like our Milky Way.
galaxia: Un enorme grupo de miles de millones de estrellas, gas y polvo que la gravedad mantiene unidos, como nuestra Vía Láctea.

  1. AQuestion A. In the scale model, how big was the Earth?

    En el modelo a escala, ¿de qué tamaño era la Tierra?

    At this scale, the Sun was an eight-inch ball and the Earth was a peppercorn about 26 steps away.

  2. BQuestion B. What is one astronomical unit?

    ¿Qué es una unidad astronómica?

    One AU is the average distance from the Earth to the Sun. Jupiter is about 5 AU from the Sun.

  3. CQuestion C. Why didn't Neptune fit in the hallway?

    ¿Por qué Neptuno no cabía en el pasillo?

    Thirty times 26 steps is 780 steps: much longer than the hallway.

  4. DQuestion D. What did Henrietta Leavitt discover about Cepheid stars?

    ¿Qué descubrió Henrietta Leavitt sobre las estrellas cefeidas?

    In 1912, she reported that brighter Cepheids have longer cycles, so their timing shows how bright they really are.

  5. EQuestion E. What did Hubble show with Leavitt's discovery in 1924?

    ¿Qué demostró Hubble con el descubrimiento de Leavitt en 1924?

    Using a Cepheid in Andromeda, Hubble showed that it was far outside the Milky Way: another galaxy.

Challenge: explain your thinking

Analyze data

Mars is about 1.5 AU from the Sun, and Saturn is about 9.5 AU. In the hallway model, where 1 AU is 26 steps, how many steps away from the Sun would each one go? What do the numbers tell you about the solar system?

Analiza datos. Marte está a unas 1.5 UA del Sol y Saturno a unas 9.5 UA. En el modelo del pasillo, donde 1 UA son 26 pasos, ¿a cuántos pasos del Sol iría cada uno? ¿Qué te dicen los números sobre el sistema solar?

Answer in your notebook or talk it over with a classmate. Use evidence from the story.

Responde en tu cuaderno o coméntalo con un compañero. Usa evidencia del cuento.

Check your thinking

Mars: 1.5 × 26 = 39 steps. Saturn: 9.5 × 26 = 247 steps. The inner planets are close together, but the outer planets are very far apart. The solar system is mostly empty space, and the outer planets need more room than a school hallway.
